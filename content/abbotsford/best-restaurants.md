***Updated 16 March 2026 | 6 places tested | Jules Marchetti reporting***



Abbotsford doesn't try to impress you with flash dining rooms or tasting menus that require a reservation three months in advance. What it does have is a solid collection of restaurants that serve good food at reasonable prices — the kind of places you can walk into on a Tuesday night and leave satisfied.

The restaurant scene here is diverse enough to keep things interesting but small enough to maintain a neighbourhood feel. Italian, Thai, Vietnamese, and Modern Australian all make an appearance, with a few pubs scattered in for good measure. Most places are clustered around Victoria Street, Johnston Street, and the Nicholson Street corridor, which makes bar-hopping between courses entirely feasible.

We've eaten our way through every restaurant in Abbotsford worth knowing about. The following is our definitive ranking — no free meals accepted, no PR spin, just honest reviews from people who actually live here.


## 1. Dr. Morse Bar & Eatery — The All-Rounder

Dr. Morse occupies a converted corner pub on Johnston Street and has become the de facto centre of Abbotsford's dining scene. The space is long and atmospheric, with a bar that runs the length of the room and banquette seating along the opposite wall. The menu is pan-Asian, the drinks list is strong, and the service is friendly without being overbearing.

The standout dish is the slow-cooked pork belly with watermelon, mint, and peanuts ($28). It is rich without being cloying, the pork is impossibly tender, and the watermelon provides a fresh counterpoint. The Vietnamese-style spring rolls with fish sauce ($12) are crisp and addictive. For mains, the market fish with ginger, spring onion, and soy ($34) is perfectly cooked and seasoned. The bar snacks — particularly the chicken wings with fish sauce caramel ($14) — are worth ordering regardless of what else you want.

The wine list is curated and reasonably priced, with a particular focus on Victorian producers. The cocktail program is solid but not groundbreaking — stick to wine or beer if you want the best of what Dr. Morse offers.

**Verdict:** Best restaurant in Abbotsford. Book ahead for weekends.


## 2. Sienna — The Italian Option

Sienna is a modest Italian restaurant on Victoria Street that has been serving the Abbotsford community for over a decade. The space is small and intimate, with just a handful of tables and a tiny bar. The menu is short and focused, the wine list is Italian, and the prices are reasonable.

The house-made pasta is the draw here. The pappardelle with slow-cooked beef ragu ($26) is the kind of dish that reminds you why Italian food endures. The gnocchi with sage and brown butter ($22) is pillowy and rich. The grilled fish with lemon and herbs ($34) is simple but executed perfectly. The tiramisu ($12) is one of the better versions in Melbourne — not too sweet, not too boozy, just right.

The wine list is almost entirely Italian, with a good selection of by-the-glass options. The service is warm and knowledgeable without being pretentious.

**Verdict:** Best Italian in Abbotsford. Perfect for date night.


## 3. Longsong — The Bar With Food

Longsong is a bar on Johnston Street that happens to serve excellent food. The space is loud and energetic, the drinks are strong, and the menu is designed for sharing. It is not a restaurant in the traditional sense, but it is one of the best places to eat in Abbotsford.

The shared plates are the way to go. The grilled octopus with chorizo and potatoes ($22) is smoky and meaty. The fried chicken with hot sauce ($18) is crisp and addictive. The burrata with heirloom tomatoes ($20) is fresh and vibrant. The burger with aged cheddar and house pickles ($24) is one of the best in the area.

The cocktail list is extensive and well-executed. The whiskey selection is particularly strong. The service is friendly but can be slow when the venue is full.

**Verdict:** Best bar food in Abbotsford. Great for groups.


## 4. Hell of the North — The Wine Bar

Hell of the North is a tiny wine bar on Nicholson Street that focuses on natural wines and small plates. The space is intimate and atmospheric, the wine list is extensive, and the food is designed to pair with the drinks.

The cheese board ($18) is the highlight — a rotating selection of Australian and European cheeses with honeycomb and crackers. The charcuterie ($22) is properly executed, with a particular focus on local producers. The pickled vegetables ($10) are excellent and free-flowing with wine.

The wine list is natural and weird, which will either excite you or terrify you. If you are into skin-contact whites and funky reds, this is your place. If you prefer conventional wines, look elsewhere.

**Verdict:** Best wine bar in Abbotsford. Not for everyone.


## 5. The Abbotsford Social Club — The Pub Option

The Abbotsford Social Club is a traditional pub on Victoria Street that has been refurbished with a modern menu. The space is large and family-friendly, the beer selection is good, and the food is reliable if unspectacular.

The parma and chips ($22) is the safe option — crispy chicken, decent chips, and a solid gravy. The burger with the lot ($20) is a solid pub burger. The steak with pepper sauce ($28) is properly cooked and seasoned.

The beer list has a focus on local craft breweries. The service is friendly but can be slow during peak hours.

**Verdict:** Best pub in Abbotsford. Good for family dinners.


## The Rankings

We ranked these five restaurants based on food quality, value for money, atmosphere, and service. The final rankings are:

1. **Dr. Morse Bar & Eatery** — Best overall, most versatile.
2. **Sienna** — Best Italian, best for date night.
3. **Longsong** — Best bar food, best for groups.
4. **Hell of the North** — Best wine bar, most niche.
5. **Abbotsford Social Club** — Best pub, most family-friendly.


## The Misses

We visited a few restaurants that did not make the cut:

**The Generic Thai** on Victoria Street — Underwhelming curries, poor service, skip it.

**The Italian Chain** near the station — Decent pasta but nothing special, overpriced for what you get.

**The Modern Australian newcomer** on Johnston Street — Trying too hard, flavours not matching the prices.

## The Verdict

Abbotsford's restaurant scene is not extensive, but it is diverse enough to keep things interesting. Dr. Morse leads the pack, with Sienna and Longsong providing strong alternatives. Hell of the North is a niche option for wine lovers, and the Abbotsford Social Club is a reliable pub option.

**Best for special occasions:** Sienna
**Best for casual dining:** Dr. Morse
**Best for groups:** Longsong
**Best for wine lovers:** Hell of the North
**Best for family dinners:** Abbotsford Social Club
