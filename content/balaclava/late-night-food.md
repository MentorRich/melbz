# Late Night Food in Balaclava 2026: After Dark on Carlisle Street

*Updated 16 March 2026 | 6 places tested | Ethan Cross reporting*

Carlisle Street doesn't get the hype it deserves. You hear about [St Kilda](/st-kilda/food-guide) strip, Acland Street cakes, and the [Windsor](/windsor/late-night-eats) rooftop scene, but the Balaclava–St Kilda East corridor quietly serves some of the best late-night food in the inner south. The suburb straddles a border dispute no one cares about — locals call it Balaclava, the postcode says St Kilda East, and the food runs well past 9pm on most nights.

We walked the strip on three weeknights and a Saturday to see what's actually open when hunger hits after dark. Here's what survived.

---

## 1. Rufio — Latin Fusion With a Rooftop

**Address:** 188 Carlisle St, St Kilda VIC 3182  
**Hours:** Wed–Fri 5pm–late; Sat–Sun 12pm–late (closed Mon–Tue)  
**Price range:** $25–$45 per person  
**Phone:** (03) 7003 4393

Rufio is the late-night heavyweight on Carlisle Street and the only venue here that genuinely earns the "rooftop bar" label. The team behind Repeat Offender in Elwood brought Peruvian, Brazilian and Argentine flavours across the highway and built a venue that pulls in a crowd well past midnight on weekends.

The menu is entirely gluten-free with solid vegan options — think smoky chargrilled meats, corn ribs, and sharing plates built for grazing over mezcal cocktails. Their $25 steak night (Wednesdays) and $30 Sunday roast are genuine value plays for a neighbourhood that's creeping upmarket.

Kitchen runs until late on Fridays and Saturdays. The cocktail list leans hard into tequila and mezcal, with a happy hour window that makes the rooftop affordable before the dinner rush. If you're eating after 10pm on a weekend, this is your first stop on Carlisle Street.

**Best for:** Date night that turns into a big night. The rooftop in warmer months is unbeatable.

---

## 2. Balaclava Hotel — The Pub That Never Sleeps

**Address:** 123 Carlisle St, St Kilda East VIC 3183  
**Hours:** Mon–Sun 10am–4am (bar); bistro served daily for lunch and dinner  
**Price range:** $16–$30 per person  
**Phone:** (03) 9531 2709

The Balaclava Hotel has been on this corner since 1887 and the current iteration — post-renovation — is genuinely good. The bistro does proper pub food: Graziers steaks, chicken parma, pizzas, and a changing specials board that runs midweek deals worth tracking.

What makes it relevant here is the hours. The bar stays open until 4am most nights, making it one of the last stops in the area for food and drinks. The kitchen keeps up for most of that window with a condensed late-night menu. Parma Night Thursdays and the sports bar setup mean there's usually something happening.

The rooftop deck is small but pleasant, and the heritage facade gives the whole place more character than your average chain pub. It's also one of the most affordable sit-down options on the strip.

**Best for:** The late-night parma and a jug of beer when everywhere else has shut.

---

## 3. Si Senor Art Taqueria — Tacos Until 10pm

**Address:** 219 Carlisle St, Balaclava VIC 3183  
**Hours:** Mon–Thu 4:30–9:30pm; Fri 12–10pm; Sat 12–10pm; Sun 12–9:30pm  
**Price range:** $18–$32 per person  
**Phone:** 1300 292 657

Si Senor is a mini-chain (they've got spots in Hampton and Richmond too) but the Balaclava taqueria is the original and still the most fun. It sits right in the middle of Carlisle Street and does authentic Mexican street food — proper al pastor tacos, guacamole made tableside, burritos, and churros that arrive hot and crispy.

The drinks menu pulls weight too: Tommy's Margaritas, frozen options, and a happy hour that runs until 7pm most days. At $69 for a weekend bottomless brunch, it's one of the better value propositions in the area.

Kitchen closes at 9:30pm weekdays and 10pm on weekends. That's late enough to be a genuine dinner option and the food travels well if you're grabbing takeaway.

**Best for:** Tacos and margies after work, or a group dinner that doesn't break the bank.

---

## 4. Mopho Canteen — Vietnamese That Runs Late

**Address:** 197 Carlisle St, Balaclava VIC 3183  
**Hours:** Mon–Thu 12–9:30pm; Fri 12–10pm; Sat 11am–10:30pm; Sun 11am–9:30pm  
**Price range:** $20–$40 per person  
**Phone:** (03) 9531 7145

Mopho Canteen has been a Carlisle Street fixture since 2015, started by chef Adam Davies (ex Mr Wolf and Melbourne Wine Room). It's casual Vietnamese dining done properly — steaming bowls of pho, bánh mì, rice paper rolls, and sharing plates that punch well above the price point.

Saturday night is the play here: kitchen runs until 10:30pm, which is late by Balaclava standards. The space itself is tight and buzzy, with a no-bookings walk-in culture that keeps turnover quick. It's the kind of place where you can get a genuinely excellent meal for under $30 and still be home before midnight.

The menu has strong vegetarian options and the flavour combinations are more considered than your average suburban Vietnamese joint. Worth the walk if you're staying in the [Caulfield](/caulfield/food) end of things.

**Best for:** A proper sit-down dinner when you want flavour without fuss. Weekend late-night slot.

---

## 5. The Local Taphouse — Craft Beer and Kitchen-Until-Late Pub Food

**Address:** 184 Carlisle St, St Kilda East VIC 3183  
**Hours:** Mon–Sun 12pm–1am  
**Price range:** $18–$35 per person  
**Phone:** (03) 9537 2633

The Local Taphouse claims St Kilda East and sits right next to Balaclava Station, so it counts. This European-inspired neighbourhood tavern has been running for years with a serious craft beer focus — 16 taps rotating Australian and international brews — and a food menu designed around beer matching.

The kitchen keeps things going well into the evening with burgers, share plates, seasonal specials, and a Sunday roast that draws a crowd. The upstairs rooftop beer garden has an outdoor fireplace in winter and is one of the best casual late-night eating spots in the area.

There's also regular trivia and comedy nights that bring in a mixed crowd of locals, students, and the after-work Carlton/Fitzroy crowd crossing south. It stays open until 1am every night, which gives it a genuine edge for late-night eats on the strip.

**Best for:** Craft beer drinkers who want decent food with their pints. The rooftop is a winter winner.

---

## 6. Ms Carlisles — The Late-Night Dark Horse

**Address:** 137 Carlisle St, Balaclava VIC 3183  
**Hours:** Closed Mon; Tues–Wed 4pm–late; Thu–Sat 4pm–late; check for Sunday hours  
**Price range:** $20–$40 per person

Ms Carlisles is the one most visitors walk past. Tucked between the Balaclava Hotel and the busier taquerias, it's a tapas-and-wine bar that opens at 4pm and runs "late" — which on weekends often means past midnight. The menu leans into share plates: charcuterie, grilled prawns, flatbreads, and desserts designed for lingering over a bottle.

The interior is all brick and warm wood, with a cocktail list that takes itself seriously without being pretentious. They do themed nights — think bottomless brunch on weekends and regular specials — but the real draw is that it's one of the few places on the strip where you can still get a proper sit-down meal after 10pm without ending up in a pub.

It's not cheap by Balaclava standards, but the quality-to-price ratio is strong, and the atmosphere is more intimate than anything else in the immediate area.

**Best for:** A quieter late-night option when the pubs feel too rowdy. Good date spot.

---

## What We Skipped and Why

Not every venue on Carlisle Street made the cut. Here's what we left out and why:

**Carlisle Seafood** (286 Carlisle St) — An absolute institution for fish and chips and Friday night queues, but the kitchen closes at 8:30pm. That's too early for a late-night list, no matter how good the battered barramundi is. We'll cover them in a daytime eats piece.

**Inkerman Hotel** (375 Inkerman St) — Great parma, 160-odd years of history, and a loyal local following, but the kitchen shuts at 9pm and the pub wraps at 10pm most nights. It's a solid early-dinner pick, not a late-night one. Worth noting for [Caulfield](/caulfield/food) crossover readers though.

**Feast on Carlisle** (240 Carlisle St) — Quick halal takeaway that closes at 8:30pm. The food is decent but the hours don't serve our purpose here.

**Any late-night delivery-only spots** — We deliberately excluded ghost kitchens and delivery-only operations. This list is about walking out your door and finding food. Delivery is a different article.

---

## Getting There and Getting Home

Balaclava Station sits right on the Sandringham line and most of these venues are within a 2-minute walk. Trains run until about midnight on weeknights and later on Friday/Saturday. If you're coming from [Windsor](/windsor/late-night-eats), it's one train stop or a 15-minute walk up Chapel Street. From [Caulfield](/caulfield/food), it's a short tram ride on the 96.

Trams along Carlisle Street itself run frequently during the day but thin out after 10pm. Rideshare is reliable in this pocket given the proximity to St Kilda and Chapel Street.

---

## The Verdict

Balaclava's late-night food scene is small but genuine. You're not going to find the volume of options you get on Lygon Street or in [St Kilda](/st-kilda/food-guide) proper, but what exists here is better than the suburb's profile suggests. Carlisle Street has a neighbourhood feel that keeps prices honest and the food honest-er.

The strip's sweet spot is Friday and Saturday from 8pm to midnight — Rufio is pumping, Mopho is still slinging pho, and the Balaclava Hotel is doing what it's done for 140 years. If you're after something more refined, Ms Carlisles is your play. If you want tacos and tequila, Si Senor delivers every time.

The lack of a truly late-night dedicated takeaway spot (after midnight) is the one gap. The Balaclava Hotel's bar food fills most of that hole, but a proper 24-hour banh mi or kebab shop would own this street. Someone get on that.
