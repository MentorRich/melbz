# Camberwell Honest Guide 2026: Burke Road & Real Opinions

*Updated 16 March 2026 | Jack Morrison reporting*

---

Look, Camberwell gets a rough rap from people who've never actually spent time here. The usual line is "it's where ambition goes to retire" or "it's basically a massive Coles parking lot with delusions of grandity." And honestly? There's a kernel of truth in that. But Camberwell is also one of those suburbs that rewards people who bother to look past the strip mall exterior and the median house price that makes even agents wince.

I've spent enough time on Burke Road, Riversdale Road, and wandering the backstreets to give you the honest picture — the good, the mediocre, and the stuff locals pretend doesn't exist.

## The Vibe: What Camberwell Actually Feels Like

Camberwell in 2026 sits in this weird space between aspirational and comfortable. It's not trying to be Fitzroy. It's not trying to be Toorak. It's the suburb that says, "I could live somewhere flashier, but why bother when everything I need is within walking distance and I don't have to deal with the parking?"

The demographic skews young families, established professionals, and a solid chunk of downsizers who sold in Hawthorn or Canterbury and pocketed the difference. There's a genuine community feel — you see the same faces at the Saturday market, the same dog walkers on the Anniversary Trail, the same bloke at the bottle shop who remembers your pinot noir preference.

Burke Road is the spine of the suburb, and it's had a slow-burn transformation over the past few years. The old strip is still there — chemists, a bakery, the Op Shop that somehow always has something you need — but there's been a steady drip of new places moving in. Not a revolution. More like a quiet renovation.

## What's Actually Good

### The Food Scene (Yes, Really)

Camberwell's food offering has genuinely improved. It was never going to compete with [Hawthorn](/suburbs/hawthorn)'s lively pub-and-cafe corridor or [Glen Iris](/suburbs/glen-iris)'s quiet neighbourhood spots, but it's carved out its own thing.

**The coffee** is better than it has any right to be for a suburb that rolls up the sidewalks at 9pm. You'll find decent flat whites around $4.50–$5.00 across most of Burke Road. The usual suspects hold up — expect the specialty places to be heaving on weekend mornings with prams and golden retrievers.

**The standout is the multicultural mix.** Head towards the Camberwell Market end and you've got a cluster of Asian eateries that punch well above their weight. Dumplings for under $15. Pho that doesn't come with a surcharge for existing. These aren't "discoveries" — the local Vietnamese and Chinese communities have been eating here for decades. It's only recently that the wider Melbourne food crowd started paying attention.

**What to skip:** The Italian options are fine, not spectacular. If you want a proper Italian meal, honestly, you're better off heading down Burke Road into [Canterbury](/suburbs/canterbury) or swinging through to Lygon Street. Camberwell does "good enough" Italian, not "drive across town" Italian.

### Camberwell Market

Every Sunday, the car park off Camberwell Road transforms into one of Melbourne's longest-running markets. It's been running since the 1970s and it's the real deal — not the polished, Instagram-friendly artisan markets where everything costs $28 and comes in brown paper. This is proper second-hand, vintage, and craft market territory. You will find things you don't need. You will buy them anyway.

**Insider tip:** Get there by 8am if you want the good stuff. By 10am the best vintage has been picked over and you're left with hand-painted pet portraits and homemade candles.

### The Transport

Camberwell is well-served by trams — the 75 runs along Camberwell Road and the 70 down Riversdale Road. The train station sits on the Belgrave and Lilydale lines, which gets you into the city in about 25 minutes on a good day. "A good day" doing a lot of heavy lifting there, because Metro being Metro, you'll want to factor in the occasional 15-minute delay and a platform change nobody warned you about.

**The parking situation** is what it is. Burke Road has timed spots that disappear faster than dim sum on a Sunday. If you're driving, park on the side streets off Burke — the residential zones north of the strip are your best bet. Just check the signs, because Camberwell Parking Rangers are efficient, enthusiastic, and utterly without mercy.

## The Honest Downsides

### Nightlife? What Nightlife?

Let's not pretend. Camberwell at 10pm is a ghost town. There's no late-night bar culture, no laneway scene, no rooftop anything. The pubs wind down early, the restaurants close, and you're left staring at closed shopfronts wondering if you should've gone to [Hawthorn](/suburbs/hawthorn) instead.

If you want a proper night out, you're catching the tram to the CBD, heading into Richmond, or swallowing your pride and going to Glen Iris — which, to be fair, has been building a better late-night food scene than Camberwell for years.

### The Cost of Existing Here

Camberwell isn't cheap. Median house prices hover around the $1.8M mark in 2026, and renting isn't much of a reprieve — expect to pay $550–$700/week for a decent two-bedroom. The cafes and restaurants are reasonably priced, but the cost of actually *living* here — the groceries, the rent, the council rates — means you need a solid income to make it work without stress.

If you're looking for something more affordable with similar vibes, [Glen Iris](/suburbs/glen-iris) offers a bit more breathing room on the rental front, and [Canterbury](/suburbs/canterbury) has pockets that are surprisingly reasonable given the postcode cachet.

### The Age Factor

Camberwell skews older than its inner-east neighbours. This isn't inherently bad — older demographics mean quieter streets, lower crime, and a community that actually looks out for each other. But it does mean the energy is different. If you're after the social chaos of [Hawthorn](/suburbs/hawthorn) near Swinburne, or the young professional energy of Richmond, Camberwell will feel tame.

You will encounter at least one person per outing who is passionately opinionated about the correct way to prune roses. This is fine. This is Camberwell.

## What We Skipped and Why

We're not going to give you a "Top 20 Things To Do in Camberwell" list because half of those would be padding, and you'd see straight through it. Here's what we deliberately left out and why:

- **Individual restaurant reviews:** The food scene shifts too fast for a static guide. Check our [ongoing suburb reviews](/suburbs/camberwell) for current picks. We'd rather give you the framework for deciding than pretend a place we visited three months ago is still the same.

- **School rankings:** Camberwell has good schools. We know. Every parent in the inner east knows. The school catchment maps are public — go look at them yourself. We're not going to rank them because we don't think a school's worth is reducible to a number.

- **Property price predictions:** Nobody knows where the market is going. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. We cover cost of living honestly — the rest is between you, your mortgage broker, and whatever deity you pray to.

- **Gym and fitness comparisons:** There are plenty. They all have treadmills. Pick one near your house.

## The Neighbourhood Connections

One of Camberwell's real strengths is how well it connects to its neighbours. You're a short tram ride from:

- **[Hawthorn](/suburbs/hawthorn)** — 5 minutes by tram for Glenferrie Road's livelier dining and pub scene. If Camberwell is a quiet arvo, Hawthorn is a Friday night.
- **[Glen Iris](/suburbs/glen-iris)** — A gentle walk or one stop on the train for Ashburton's growing food strip and more affordable rental options.
- **[Canterbury](/suburbs/canterbury)** — Walkable from the southern end of Camberwell. Canterbury's quieter, leafier, and has some excellent neighbourhood spots that don't get the attention they deserve.

These aren't just neighbours on a map — they're part of the Camberwell lifestyle equation. Locals regularly drift between all four suburbs depending on the mood, the meal, and the Myki balance.

## The Verdict

Camberwell is not the most exciting suburb in Melbourne. It is not trying to be. What it offers is a well-located, community-oriented, slightly sleepy inner-east base with good transport, decent food, and a Sunday market that's been reliably excellent for fifty years.

It's the suburb you move to when you're done chasing the new and shiny and want somewhere that just *works*. That's not a criticism. In a city that's constantly reinventing itself, there's something genuinely appealing about a suburb that says, "We're good. We've been good. We'll still be good."

If Camberwell's not quite your speed but the inner-east is calling, check our honest guides to [Hawthorn](/suburbs/hawthorn), [Glen Iris](/suburbs/glen-iris), and [Canterbury](/suburbs/canterbury) for comparison.
