## Where the Money Actually Goes

**Groceries:** You've got options. Between the local IGA, any nearby Woolies/Coles, and the independent grocers and markets in the area, weekly shops run $80-$120 for one person depending on how much you cook.

**Eating out:** The average meal out in Carlton runs $18-$25 for lunch and $30-$50 for dinner. Check our [cheap eats guide](/carlton/cheap-eats/) for the spots that don't punish your wallet.

**Transport:** A full-price Myki costs $10 daily cap, $50 weekly cap in Zone 1-2. If you're in Carlton, you're firmly in Zone 1, which keeps your Myki costs predictable. An annual Myki pass is the smart move if you commute daily.

## The Hidden Costs Nobody Warns You About

1. **Parking permits** — If you drive, council parking permits run $50-$150/year depending on the area
2. **Brunch culture tax** — You will spend more on breakfast than you budget for. It's Melbourne law.
3. **The "quick drink after work" budget** — A pint in Carlton averages $12-$14. Cocktails run $18-$24.
4. **Laundry** — If your apartment doesn't have a washing machine, coin laundry runs $8-$12 per load

## Is Carlton Worth the Cost?

Carlton is mid-range for Melbourne — not cheap, not eye-watering. You get a A walkability score and A- food scene for the price. It's a fair deal if those things matter to your daily life.
