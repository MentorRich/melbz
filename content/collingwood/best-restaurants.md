# Collingwood's Table: The Definitive Guide to Eating Well in 2026

Collingwood’s culinary landscape has always been a story of evolution. Once the gritty heart of Melbourne’s industrial past, its streets now hold a concentration of dining rooms that are as diverse and dynamic as the suburb itself. Walking down Smith or Johnston Street today, you’re as likely to smell fermenting sourdough as you are the scent of spices being ground for a complex curry. This isn’t a scene that chases trends; it sets them, often in a beautifully unpolished room where the focus is squarely on what’s on the plate. As we settle into 2026, the neighbourhood has solidified its reputation, moving beyond hype to a mature, confident culinary identity. Here, we guide you through the tables that define it right now.

### 1. **Añada**
**197 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065**

While technically just over the border from Collingwood on Gertrude Street, Añada’s spirit is so intertwined with the area’s dining soul it feels like a local institution. Jesse Gerner’s ode to the flavours of Spain and the Levant remains a masterclass in warmth and generosity. The room, all exposed brick and soft lighting, hums with conversation. This is food meant for sharing—a parade of small plates that build into a memorable feast. Don’t miss the Moorish-spiced lamb ribs, impossibly tender and slick with a pomegranate molasses glaze, or the silky, smoky baba ganoush that’s a staple for a reason. The wine list, deeply knowledgeable and favouring small Spanish and Australian producers, is a journey in itself. Añada feels like a cornerstone, the kind of place you return to for reliable, soulful cooking.

### 2. **Lagoon Dining**
**360 Smith Street, Collingwood VIC 3066**

Tucked away on Smith Street, Lagoon Dining is a study in focused, produce-driven cooking with a distinct Asian accent. The space is minimalist and calm, allowing the packed dishes to take centre stage. Chef Ross Magnaye’s menu is a confident exploration of texture and bold, clean flavours. A plate of kingfish with fermented pineapple and a hint of finger lime is electric, while the crispy pork hock with a fiery vinegar dipping sauce demands to be ordered. Their take on fried rice, studded with smoked eel and prawns, is a comforting yet sophisticated staple. The cocktail list is inventive without being fussy, making it an ideal spot for a dinner that feels both exciting and effortlessly cool.

### 3. **Café Rosamond**
**191 Johnston Street, Collingwood VIC 3066**

For a taste of the Mediterranean that’s deeply personal and utterly transporting, Café Rosamond is a Collingwood essential. This isn’t a generic “Italian” spot; it’s a specific, sun-drenched slice of Sardinia and Corsica, run with palpable passion. The pasta is all made in-house, and you can taste the difference in dishes like the *culurgiones*, a delicate Sardinian dumpling filled with potato, mint, and pecorino, served in a simple, perfect tomato sauce. The grilled meats, particularly the lamb, are seasoned with wild herbs and cooked over coals. The dining room, with its checked tablecloths and rustic decor, is deliberately unpretentious. It’s a place where the food tells a story of heritage, and every visit feels like being welcomed into a family kitchen.

### 4. **CIBI**
**33/39 Keele Street, Collingwood VIC 3066**

CIBI is more than a café or restaurant; it’s a design-led concept space that champions a Japanese-inspired approach to daily living. Set in a sprawling, light-filled warehouse, it houses a café, a homewares shop, and a small grocery. The food philosophy is one of gentle nourishment and balance. Their signature CIBI plate is a masterpiece of composition—a selection of seasonal vegetables, pickles, a protein like miso-glazed salmon or tofu, and brown rice, all arranged with aesthetic precision. For breakfast, their fluffy Japanese-style pancakes or the *okonomiyaki* are standout choices. It’s a serene environment to enjoy a meal that feels both healthy and deeply satisfying, reflecting a holistic approach to food that’s increasingly sought-after.

### 5. **Lucky Coq**
**179 Chapel Street, Windsor VIC 3181** *(Worth the short tram ride)*

Okay, we’re bending the Collingwood boundary again, but the influence of this Windsor institution on Melbourne’s casual dining scene is undeniable, and it’s a quick trip down the road. Lucky Coq perfected the art of the gourmet pub pizza long before it was commonplace. In 2026, it still delivers with unwavering consistency. The vibe is always lively, the beer selection is vast and interesting, and the pizzas are brilliant. Think perfectly blistered, chewy crusts topped with combinations like the ‘Bastard’—spicy salami, capsicum, and olives—or the simple, elegant ‘Garlic.’ It’s the benchmark for a no-fuss, high-quality meal with friends, proving that sometimes the best dining experiences are the most relaxed.

### 6. **Marion**
**53 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065**

Another Gertrude Street gem that’s impossible to omit from any discussion of the area’s best dining. Marion, from the team behind Cutler & Co., is a wine bar with food that far exceeds the usual accompaniment. The space is elegant yet welcoming, with a focus on the stunning central bar. The menu is designed for grazing and sharing, featuring seasonal dishes with a European bistro lean. A simple plate of heirloom tomatoes with stracciatella and basil oil sings with quality, while the duck liver parfait with toasted brioche is rich and decadent. The true star, however, is the wine list—curated with expertise and a clear point of view, offering both classic and adventurous choices by the glass. It’s perfect for a sophisticated evening that doesn’t feel stuffy.

### 7. **Very Good Falafel**
**629 Sydney Road, Brunswick VIC 3056** *(A pilgrimage-worthy destination)*

Yes, it’s in Brunswick. But to talk about the best food experiences accessible from Collingwood without mentioning this place would be a disservice. Very Good Falafel has achieved legendary status for one simple reason: it does one thing, and it does it with near-perfection. The falafel are made fresh, fried to order, and are shatteringly crisp on the outside, impossibly green and fluffy within. Assembled into a wrap with all the trimmings—hummus, tahini, pickles, salads—it’s a handheld masterpiece. The line often snakes out the door, but it moves quickly, and the wait is part of the ritual. It represents the democratic, no-frills excellence that Melbourne’s food scene is built upon.

### 8. **Bistra**
**276 Smith Street, Collingwood VIC 3066**

Rounding out our list is Bistra, a relative newcomer that has quickly cemented its place in the Collingwood rotation. It captures the essence of a modern European bistro—think classic techniques with a light, contemporary touch. The room is cozy and intimate, ideal for a date night or a quiet dinner. The menu changes with the seasons but might feature a beautiful duck confit with lentils, a perfectly cooked market fish with beurre blanc, or a rich, comforting beef bourguignon. Their commitment to using quality Victorian produce is evident in every dish. The service is warm and knowledgeable, making Bistra feel like a discovery that’s already a local favourite.

Collingwood’s dining scene in 2026 is a testament to substance over spectacle. It’s a neighbourhood where chefs are cooking with conviction, whether that’s honouring a culinary tradition, pioneering a new fusion, or simply perfecting a single, iconic dish. The common thread is a respect for ingredients and a desire to feed people well. So pull up a chair—whether it’s at a always crowded counter on Smith Street or a quiet table on Gertrude—and explore the depth of flavour that this iconic Melbourne suburb has to offer.
