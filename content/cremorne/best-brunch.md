# Best Brunch in Cremorne — 2026 Local Guide

Here's something that surprises people: Cremorne ranked number one in Melbourne for cafe density in the Domain Liveable Melbourne study, beating the CBD, Southbank, and South Melbourne. For a suburb you can walk across in fifteen minutes, that's absurd. And it means the brunch game here is stupidly competitive — only the good survive.

Cremorne's brunch scene is shaped by two forces: the tech workers who need coffee at their desks by 7am, and the weekend crowd who've figured out that skipping the Chapel Street madness for a quieter, more interesting suburb is the move. Whether you're after sourdough that could change your life or a full English that fixes your Saturday, these are the spots worth waking up for.

## 1. Baker Bleu
**The vibe:** Melbourne's sourdough cult headquarters just got a flagship — and they added all-day dining to make sure you never leave.

Baker Bleu's Cremorne location on Dover Street is their biggest and boldest outpost, and it shows. The space is generous — blue-accented interiors, natural light pouring through floor-to-ceiling windows, and a bakery counter that will bankrupt you if you're not careful. The sourdough is the stuff of legend (crusty exterior, impossibly soft crumb, best eaten standing at the counter pretending you won't eat the whole loaf).

But it's the all-day brunch that makes Baker Bleu the anchor of Cremorne weekends. Challah French toast with figs and mascarpone, sourdough pizzas topped with stracciatella, and ficelles packed with mortadella or brie and fig jam. The Market Lane coffee is excellent, but honestly, you're here for the food.

**Order this:** Challah French toast with seasonal figs ($22) and a flat white ($4.80)
**Address:** 65 Dover Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 7am–4pm, Sat–Sun 8am–4pm
**Insider tip:** The toasted sandwiches are exclusive to the Cremorne location — you can't get them at their other stores. The ham and cheese on their house sourdough is embarrassingly good for something so simple.

## 2. Top Paddock
**The vibe:** The brunch institution that started Cremorne's café obsession. Still delivering, still packed, still worth the wait.

Top Paddock has been on Church Street long enough to qualify as heritage-listed (not actually, but give it time). From the same stable as Higher Ground and The Kettle Black, it's the kind of brunch spot that tourists find on Instagram and locals resent them for it. The eggs Benedict here set the standard that half of Melbourne's brunch spots are still trying to match.

The space is generous, the coffee is faultless, and the seasonal menu means there's always something new to try alongside the signatures that have kept tables full for years. If you haven't been, you're overdue. If you have, you already know.

**Order this:** The duck egg Benedict with truffle hollandaise ($24) or the ricotta hotcakes with honeycomb butter ($23)
**Address:** 67 Gwynne Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 7am–3pm, Sat–Sun 8am–3pm
**Insider tip:** Arrive before 8:30am on weekends or expect a 20–30 minute wait. Alternatively, come at 2pm when the crowd thins and you can linger.

## 3. La Manna & Sons
**The vibe:** Your Italian nonna's kitchen, if nonna had a warehouse space on Balmain Street and an Allpress espresso machine.

La Manna & Sons is the neighbourhood spot that regulars guard jealously. The deli counter is stacked with prosciutto, mozzarella, and salads that actually look like the photos. The toasted panini — stuffed with silky prosciutto and fresh mozzarella — is a simple thing done so well it becomes the only thing you ever order. The smoked salmon breakfast plate is generous and fresh, and the focaccia is soft, warm, and absolutely impossible to share (don't try).

Wani Sak and Melinda Aloisio opened the original 1983 Espresso on Cremorne Street back in 2012, then expanded into Ms Frankie next door. La Manna & Sons feels like the natural evolution — a place where coffee, deli food, and community blur together into something that genuinely anchors a neighbourhood.

**Order this:** Toasted prosciutto and mozzarella panini ($16) and an Allpress latte ($4.50)
**Address:** 98 Balmain Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 7am–3pm, Sat 8am–3pm, Sun closed
**Insider tip:** Grab a focaccia to go before 10am — they sell out most days, and once they're gone, they're gone.

## 4. Café Decjuba x St. Ali
**The vibe:** Corner café where the $4.50 coffee is genuinely excellent and the ham and cheese croissant has no right to be that good.

On the corner of Cubitt and Cremorne Street, this café is the reliable workhorse of the Cremorne morning commute. The partnership with St. Ali means the coffee is dialed in to a level most suburbs would kill for, and at $4.50 a cup, it's practically charity. The ham and cheese croissant is flaky, buttery, and the kind of thing that makes you understand why Europeans treat pastry as a food group, not a treat.

It's not trying to reinvent brunch. It's just doing the basics at a level that embarrasses flashier spots charging double.

**Order this:** Ham and cheese croissant ($8) and a long black ($4.50)
**Address:** 134 Cubitt Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 7am–3pm, Sat 8am–2pm
**Insider tip:** The outdoor tables on the Cubitt Street corner get the morning sun perfectly. Perfect for people-watching the Cremorne morning rush.

## 5. My Oh My Espresso
**The vibe:** Early-morning espresso bar on Swan Street that opens at 6am and doesn't judge you for showing up in yesterday's clothes.

My Oh My is where Cremorne's early risers start their day. Opening at 6am weekdays (7am weekends), it's built for the tradies, the personal trainers, and the tech workers who start stand-ups before most Melbourne brunch spots have ground their first beans. The space on Swan Street is compact but efficient — this is a grab-and-go operation that also happens to have a few seats if you want to linger.

The coffee is consistently good, the vibe is unpretentious, and the fact that it opens when actual humans need coffee (not when brunch culture decides is fashionable) makes it a local institution.

**Order this:** A flat white and a bacon and egg roll ($4.50 coffee, $12 roll)
**Address:** 232 Swan Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 6am–6pm, Sat–Sun 7am–6pm
**Insider tip:** The 6am weekday opening is no joke — by 7:30am there's a genuine queue. Come at 6:45am for the sweet spot of excellent coffee and no wait.

## 6. Hunted+Gathered
**The vibe:** Part café, part bean-to-bar chocolate factory, entirely intoxicating. The air alone is worth the visit.

Hunted+Gathered on Gwynne Street is Cremorne's sweetest secret — literally. The warehouse space smells like warm cacao the moment you walk in, and the hot chocolate is the kind of deep, velvety thing that makes you understand why the Victorians went mad for it. The brownies have a near-cult following, the coffee is smooth and balanced (sourced and roasted with the same care as their chocolate), and the overall experience feels like you've discovered something your friends haven't yet.

It's not a full brunch destination — you won't get a three-course Saturday feed here. But for a mid-morning pick-me-up that's genuinely different, it's unmatched.

**Order this:** Single-origin hot chocolate and a brownie ($7 hot choc, $6 brownie)
**Address:** 68 Gwynne Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 7:30am–3:30pm, Sat 8:30am–3:30pm
**Insider tip:** Ask for the drinking chocolate flight if it's available — it's not always on the menu, but when it is, it's three different cacao origins side by side. Changed this writer's understanding of chocolate.

## 7. Cheeky Monkey
**The vibe:** The all-day brunch spot where the portions are generous, the vibe is loud, and nobody's pretending to be somewhere else.

Cheeky Monkey is the brunch spot that doesn't take itself too seriously, and that's precisely why it works. On a strip of Cremorne that's increasingly tech-bro polished, Cheeky Monkey stays unashamedly casual. The menu covers all the bases — big breakfasts, benedicts, French toast, a solid lunch menu — and does them all at a level that keeps locals coming back weekly.

It's the anti-influencer brunch. Nobody's here for the photos. They're here because the food is good, the prices are fair, and the coffee keeps flowing.

**Order this:** The big breakfast with the lot ($26) or the corn fritters with avocado and poached egg ($22)
**Address:** 606 Church Street, Cremorne
**Hours:** Mon–Fri 7am–3pm, Sat–Sun 7:30am–3:30pm
**Insider tip:** Sit in the back courtyard if the weather's playing nice — it's quieter than the front and catches the mid-morning sun.

## What We Skipped and Why

**Proud Mary** — Technically in Collingwood, not Cremorne, despite what some lists claim. It's world-class (ranked 27th globally in the 2026 World's 100 Best Coffee Shops), but it's not a Cremorne venue. We don't claim other suburbs' wins.

**Higher Ground** — Similarly, this is a Southbank institution, not a Cremorne one, even though the owners have deep Cremorne roots through the Mulberry Group. We stick to actual postcodes.

**Square One Coffee Roasters** — Their Cremorne roastery isn't a public café. They've just opened a flagship cafe at the Rialto in the CBD, which is the one you can actually visit. We'll cover it if they open a retail space in 3121.

## The Bottom Line

Cremorne doesn't have a brunch problem. It has a brunch abundance problem — you genuinely can't go wrong. But if you're only going to do one? Baker Bleu for the sourdough alone. Top Paddock if you want the classic Melbourne brunch experience. La Manna & Sons if you want to feel like a local from your very first visit.

And if someone tells you Cremorne is "just offices," take them to any of these on a Saturday morning and watch them eat their words (and a panini).
