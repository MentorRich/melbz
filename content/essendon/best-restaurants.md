# Best Restaurants in Essendon 2026

Essendon doesn't care if you've never been. That's part of its appeal. While the inner-north suburbs fight over who has the most "authentic" whatever and the CBD restaurants charge $38 for a risotto, Essendon quietly gets on with the business of feeding people properly. Mt Alexander Road is the spine — a long, busy strip where Italian trattorias sit next to Thai kitchens, Japanese izakayas share walls with old-school fish and chip shops, and nobody's trying to reinvent anything. They're just cooking well.

We've eaten at every restaurant in Essendon worth mentioning. Here are eight that are genuinely worth your time and money.

---

**1. Sapori d'Abruzzo861 — Mt Alexander Road, Essendon VIC 3040**

This is the one. If you eat at only one restaurant in Essendon, make it Sapori d'Abruzzo. Named after the food traditions of Italy's Abruzzo region, this trattoria serves pasta that would make your nonna weep — in a good way. The pappardelle with slow-braised lamb ragu ($24) is rich, deeply savoury, and the pasta is made in-house with a satisfying bite that dried pasta can never replicate. The arancini ($14) are golden-crisp outside, molten with mozzarella inside.

The room is warm and slightly chaotic in the best way — families at big tables, couples sharing bottles of Montepulciano, the occasional loud declaration from the kitchen. It's not fine dining. It's something better: an Italian restaurant that treats food with the seriousness it deserves without any of the pretension that usually comes with that.

**2. Scalable Fish-Op — Mt Alexander Road, Essendon VIC 3040**

A fish and chip shop that thinks it's a restaurant — and honestly, it might be right. Scalable Fish-Op sits on the busier end of Mt Alexander Road and does one thing exceptionally well: ethically-sourced fish, cooked properly. The signature is their soda-based batter — light, crispy, and stays crunchy even after the ten-minute drive home. Line-caught flathead, flake, and snapper rotate based on what's available.

A fish and chips combo is $17. A grilled barramundi with salad is $19. The chips are hand-cut and properly double-fried. The tartare sauce is made in-house. It's the kind of place that makes you realise how bad most fish and chip shops actually are. No deep-fried dim sims, no questionable potato cakes — just fish, done right.

**3. Little Thai Kitchen — Essendon VIC 3040**

Every suburb needs a reliable Thai restaurant, and Essendon's is Little Thai Kitchen. Tucked off the main strip, it's easy to miss — which is probably why the locals have kept it to themselves. The green curry ($16) is fragrant, properly spiced (ask for "Thai hot" and they'll actually give it to you), and comes with jasmine rice that's cooked through, not crunchy. The pad see ew ($15) has that caramelised wok flavour with wide rice noodles, Chinese broccoli, and egg.

The lunch specials — any curry or stir-fry with rice for $13 — are outstanding value. The tom yum soup ($12) is sharp, sour, and full of prawns. Service is quick, the room is small, and the menu doesn't try to be everything to everyone. It's Thai food, cooked by people who know what Thai food should taste like.

**4. Yokoso Izakaya — Essendon VIC 3040**

Yokoso brings proper izakaya culture to the north-west, and it works. The concept is Japanese pub food — small plates, shared dishes, drinks flowing. Yakitori skewers ($4–$7) come off the charcoal grill with real smoky flavour. The agedashi tofu ($12) is silken inside, crisp outside, swimming in a warm dashi broth. Karaage chicken ($14) is juicy, properly seasoned, and served with kewpie and lemon.

The sashimi platter ($26) is generous and fresh — not Sydney-fresh, but Melbourne-fresh, which is plenty good enough. The sake list is small but curated, and the Asahi on tap is properly cold. The room is intimate — wooden benches, lantern lighting, Japanese pop music at a volume that lets you actually have a conversation. It's the kind of place that makes you forget you're in Essendon.

**5. Demazzi Stone Grill Steakhouse & Burger — Mt Alexander Road, Essendon VIC 3040**

Demazzi is the opposite of subtle. It's a stone grill steakhouse where your steak arrives on a searing-hot volcanic stone and you cook it to your liking at the table. The concept is gimmicky. The execution is surprisingly good. A 250g scotch fillet ($32) comes with chips, salad, and the fun of hearing your steak sizzle for ten minutes straight. The burgers are also strong — the Demazzi burger ($18) with bacon, cheese, pickles, and a thick beef patty is legitimately one of the better burgers in the north-west.

It's loud, it's family-friendly, and it's perfect for a weeknight dinner where nobody wants to think too hard about what to order. The kids love the stone grill concept. The adults love that the steak is actually decent. Everyone wins.

**6. Andy's Yum Cha House — Essendon VIC 3040**

Yum cha in Essendon sounds unlikely, but Andy's pulls it off. The trolley service runs on weekends from 11am to 3pm, and the selection is solid — har gow, siu mai, BBQ pork buns, cheung fun, and the essential egg tarts. The dumplings are made fresh, the trolleys come around frequently, and the tea is bottomless.

For a suburb without a significant Asian population, the quality here is a genuine surprise. The prawn dumplings are plump and properly seasoned. The turnip cake has actual chunks of lap cheong sausage in it. At around $25–$30 per head for yum cha, it's good value for a weekend lunch. No booking needed — just show up, grab a table, and start pointing at trolleys.

**7. Ristorante Mambo Italiano — Essendon VIC 3040**

Mambo Italiano is Essendon's other Italian option, and it takes a different approach to Sapori d'Abruzzo — more Southern Italian, more seafood-forward, and slightly more polished. The linguine ai frutti di mare ($26) is loaded with prawns, mussels, clams, and a tomato sauce that tastes of actual tomatoes. The pizza menu is strong too — a proper Napoletana with anchovies, olives, and capers ($19) hits all the right notes.

The room has white tablecloths and candles without feeling stuffy. It's the kind of Italian restaurant that existed in Melbourne before the "modern Italian" trend took over — straightforward, generous, and focused on making food that people actually want to eat rather than photograph.

**8. Green Bamboo — Essendon VIC 3040**

Green Bamboo does Vietnamese, and does the essentials well. The pho ($15) has a clear, fragrant broth with proper rice noodles and your choice of rare beef, brisket, or chicken. The broken rice plates ($16) — grilled lemongrass pork, a fried egg, pickled vegetables, and fish sauce — are filling and honest. The fresh spring rolls ($10 for four) are tightly wrapped and served with a sweet chilli dipping sauce that has actual chilli in it.

It's not going to win any awards. It's not trying to. Green Bamboo is the kind of restaurant every suburb needs: reliable, affordable, and there when you don't feel like cooking but also don't feel like making a decision. Walk in, sit down, order the pho. You'll be fine.

**The Verdict: Essendon Feeds You Properly**

Essendon's dining scene doesn't need validation from food critics or Instagram influencers. Mt Alexander Road has been feeding the north-west for decades, and the restaurants here — Italian, Thai, Japanese, Vietnamese, and good old fish and chips — are run by people who care about food more than trends.

The best approach for a first visit: start with arancini at Sapori d'Abruzzo, walk up Mt Alexander Road for a main at Little Thai Kitchen, then finish with sake and yakitori at Yokoso. Total damage: under $50 per person. You'll leave full, satisfied, and wondering why you've been paying $35 for pasta in Carlton.
