Every autumn, as Melbourne's city gardens begin to quiet, something extraordinary blooms in the Dandenong Ranges. The Tesselaar KaBloom Flower Festival transforms 14 acres of hillside farmland into a riot of colour, with millions of flowers creating one of Victoria's most spectacular seasonal displays. Whether you're a photography enthusiast, a family looking for school holiday entertainment, or simply someone who needs a break from the city, KaBloom offers a day out that engages all the senses.

The 2026 festival runs from Saturday, March 21, through Sunday, April 19, giving you nearly a month to plan your visit. This guide covers everything you need to know—from ticket options and what's currently in bloom to practical tips for making the most of your day among the flowers.

## What Is KaBloom?

KaBloom is Victoria's largest flower festival, hosted at the Tesselaar Flower Farm in Silvan, in the Dandenong Ranges approximately 50 kilometres east of Melbourne's CBD. The festival began as a daffodil show in the 1950s and has grown into a month-long celebration of spring and autumn flowering bulbs, with displays spanning tulips, daffodils, hyacinths, ranunculus, and an ever-expanding collection of specialty blooms.

What makes KaBloom special is the sheer scale. We're not talking about a formal garden with carefully curated beds—this is a working flower farm opened to the public, where rows upon rows of colour stretch across the hillsides. The effect is almost overwhelming in the best possible way. Bring a camera, because you'll want to capture every vista.

The festival also supports the Royal Children's Hospital through its charity partner program, with a portion of ticket sales contributing to this worthy cause. It's a feel-good day out that genuinely gives back.

## Festival Details: When and Where

### Dates and Times

**2026 Festival Dates:** March 21 – April 19  
**Opening Hours:** 9:30am – 5:30pm daily (last entry 4:30pm)  
**Location:** Tesselaar Flower Farm, 357 Monbulk Road, Silvan, Victoria  

The festival runs every day during this period, including weekends and public holidays. Good Friday falls within the festival dates (March 28), and the grounds remain open as usual.

### Getting There

**By Car:** From Melbourne's CBD, take the Eastern Freeway (M3) and exit onto Maroondah Highway (B360) towards Lilydale. Continue through Lilydale and follow signs to Silvan. The farm has extensive on-site parking, with standard parking free and premium parking (closer to the entrance) available for $10.

**By Public Transport:** Take a train from Melbourne CBD to Lilydale Station (approximately 1 hour on the Belgrave line). From Lilydale, catch the 688 bus to Silvan, which stops near the festival entrance. Note that bus frequency is reduced on weekends and public holidays, so check the route 688 timetable before you go.

**By Tour:** Several tour operators run day trips to KaBloom from Melbourne, often combining the flower festival with other Dandenong Ranges attractions like Puffing Billy or the Yarra Valley wineries. These tours typically include transport and sometimes meals, making them convenient if you prefer not to drive. Search "KaBloom tours Melbourne 2026" on platforms like Viator or GetYourGuide for options.

### Fuel Considerations

Given the current fuel situation affecting regional Victoria, if you're driving, we recommend filling up in the city before heading to the ranges. Fuel stations in the Dandenong Ranges can have limited availability, and the drive back to Melbourne is easier with a full tank.

## What's in Bloom (2026 Edition)

The flower displays at KaBloom vary each year depending on the season, weather, and what the farm has chosen to plant. Based on historical patterns and early reports, here's what you can expect during the 2026 festival:

### March 21-31 (First Half)

The festival opens with the tail end of the daffodil season, transitioning into tulip season. Early March visitors typically catch the last of the jonquils and smaller daffodil varieties, while the larger show varieties are at their peak. Tulips begin emerging in the second week, with the first flush of colours appearing by late March.

**Key blooms:** Daffodils (including rare and heritage varieties), early tulips, hyacinths, muscari

### April 1-19 (Second Half)

As autumn deepens, the tulip display reaches its full glory. The farm plants hundreds of thousands of tulip bulbs in a rainbow of colours, from classic reds and yellows to exotic fringed varieties and dramatic parrots. Ranunculus also peak during this period, with their rose-like blooms adding textural interest to the displays.

**Key blooms:** Tulips (peak season), ranunculus, anemones, remaining daffodils

### Photography Tips

For the best light, arrive early (gates open at 9:30am) or visit in the late afternoon (after 3pm). The midday sun can be harsh on flower colours, and the crowds are thinnest at these times. Bring a wide-angle lens for capturing the sweeping vistas, and a macro lens if you want to focus on individual blooms.

The flower farm has a few designated photography spots where you can capture the classic "flowers with hills" vista. These tend to get busy, so claim your spot early if a particular angle matters to you.

## Ticket Options and Pricing

KaBloom offers several ticket tiers to suit different visitor needs. Here's the breakdown for 2026:

### Standard Admission

**Adult (16+):** $28 online, $32 at the gate  
**Concession (Centrelink card holders, students):** $22 online, $26 at the gate  
**Children (4-15):** $12 online, $15 at the gate  
**Family (2 adults + 2 children):** $70 online, $80 at the gate  
**Under 4:** Free  

Online tickets are significantly cheaper than gate prices, and they also help you skip the queue on arrival. Book at least a few days ahead for weekend visits, as the festival can sell out during peak periods like Easter weekend.

### Premium Experiences

**Behind the Scenes Tours:** $45 per person (includes standard admission)  
These guided tours take you into the farm's production areas, where you can learn about bulb cultivation and get planting tips for your own garden. Tours run at 10am and 2pm daily, with numbers limited to 20 participants. Book in advance—these sell out weeks ahead.

**Morning Tea with the Growers:** $55 per person (includes standard admission)  
A more intimate experience where you enjoy a country-style morning tea while hearing from the Tesselaar family about their generations of flower farming. Includes a small bulb or plant gift to take home.

### Group and School Visits

Schools and organised groups can book weekday visits at discounted rates. The festival offers educational programs for primary and secondary students covering plant science, sustainable agriculture, and the history of the Tesselaar farm. Contact the festival office directly for school booking rates and curriculum-aligned programs.

## What to Do Beyond Looking at Flowers

KaBloom isn't just about walking through flower beds (though that's certainly the main event). The festival has expanded over the years to include activities for all ages and interests.

### Food and Drink

**Food Trucks and Stalls:** A rotating selection of food vendors sets up across the grounds, offering everything from wood-fired pizza and gelato to coffee and Devonshire tea. Most vendors accept card payments, but bring some cash for the smaller stalls.

**Wine Tasting:** Several Yarra Valley wineries have pop-up tasting stations where you can sample and purchase their wines. This is a convenient way to explore the region without driving between cellar doors.

**Picnic Areas:** The grounds include several picnic tables and grassed areas where you can enjoy your own food. There are also BBQ facilities available on a first-come, first-served basis.

### Activities for Kids

**Kids' Discovery Trail:** A scavenger-hunt-style activity booklet that keeps younger visitors engaged as they explore the gardens. Pick up a trail map at the entrance ($5) and search for hidden garden gnomes, flower species, and wildlife.

**Face Painting:** Available near the main lawn, with flower-themed designs that match the festival colours.

**Farm Animals:** A small petting zoo includes friendly goats, sheep, and chickens that children can interact with. Free with admission.

### Shopping

**Plant Sales:** The farm's retail nursery opens during the festival, offering bulbs, potted flowers, and garden accessories at festival prices. This is your opportunity to take a piece of KaBloom home—whether it's a bag of tulip bulbs for your own garden or a flowering plant for your balcony.

**Art and Craft Stalls:** Local artisans sell handmade gifts, jewellery, and homewares throughout the grounds. Great for souvenir shopping.

## School Holiday Programming (April 7-19)

The second week of the festival coincides with the Victorian autumn school holidays, and KaBloom rolls out additional programming for families:

**Daily Kids' Activities (10am-3pm):** Including flower crown making, seed planting workshops, and garden games on the main lawn.

**Easter Celebrations (April 7-10):** The festival adds Easter-themed activities including egg hunts, chocolate tastings, and special appearances by the Easter Bunny. Note that these days are the busiest of the festival—if you prefer a quieter experience, avoid the school holiday period.

## Best Times to Visit

The festival's popularity means crowd levels vary significantly depending on when you go. Here's our assessment:

### Best for Small Crowds

**Weekdays (especially Monday-Thursday):** You'll have the run of the gardens, with few fellow visitors to compete with for photos. The peaceful atmosphere suits photographers and anyone who prefers a contemplative experience.

**Early March (March 21-31):** Before the school holidays begin, crowd levels remain moderate. You'll also catch the transition from daffodils to tulips, which offers unique photo opportunities.

### Expect Crowds

**Weekends:** Saturday and Sunday see the highest attendance, with the gardens feeling more like a festival and less like a peaceful retreat. Arrive at opening time (9:30am) to get the best experience before crowds build.

**Easter Weekend (April 7-10):** This is peak season, with families flooding in during the school holidays. If you must visit during this period, book tickets well in advance and expect the gardens to be busy from opening to closing.

### Best Photography Conditions

**Overcast Days:** Surprisingly, Melbourne's grey days are ideal for flower photography. The diffused light prevents harsh shadows and keeps colours true. If rain is forecast, bring an umbrella—the gardens remain open in light rain, and you'll often have them almost to yourself.

**Early Morning (9:30-11am):** Soft light and minimal crowds. The flowers are also at their freshest, before the heat of the day causes them to droop slightly.

## Practical Tips for Your Visit

### What to Bring

- Comfortable walking shoes (the grounds are grass and gravel, but there's significant walking involved)
- Sun protection (hat, sunscreen, sunglasses) – even on cloudy days, the UV can be strong
- Camera or smartphone with storage space
- Water bottle (refill stations available)
- Light jacket (the Dandenong Ranges are cooler than Melbourne, especially in the morning)
- Cash for plant sales and smaller vendors (though most accept cards)

### Accessibility

The festival grounds are partially accessible, with wheelchair-accessible paths covering the main flower displays. However, some areas have uneven ground and steps. Contact the festival office ahead of your visit if you have specific accessibility requirements, and they can advise on the best routes.

### Weather Contingency

KaBloom proceeds rain or shine. In the event of severe weather (storms, high winds), the festival may close temporarily for safety. Check the KaBloom social media accounts or website for real-time updates before heading out if the forecast looks uncertain.

### Dogs

Well-behaved dogs on leads are welcome at KaBloom, making it a pet-friendly day out. However, during busy periods, you may find the experience more enjoyable without your four-legged friend, as the crowds can be overwhelming for some pets.

## Combining KaBloom with Other Dandenong Ranges Activities

The Dandenong Ranges offer plenty to fill a day or a weekend. Here are some ideas for extending your KaBloom visit:

### Same-Day Combinations

**Puffing Billy Railway:** The heritage steam train departs from Belgrave (20 minutes from Silvan) and offers beautiful views of the ranges. Book tickets in advance, especially for weekend travel.

**SkyHigh Mount Dandenong:** Visit after KaBloom for sunset views over Melbourne. The restaurant offers dinner with a view, or simply enjoy a coffee on the observation deck.

**Yarra Valley Wineries:** On your way back to Melbourne, detour through the Yarra Valley for wine tastings at any number of excellent cellar doors. This works best if you have a designated driver.

### Weekend Getaway

If you're coming from regional Victoria or want to make a weekend of it, consider staying overnight in the Dandenong Ranges or Yarra Valley. Accommodations range from budget B&Bs to luxury retreats. Book well in advance for Easter weekend, as this is one of Victoria's peak tourism periods.

## Why KaBloom Matters

Beyond the obvious appeal of beautiful flowers, KaBloom represents something increasingly rare: a genuinely family-owned agricultural business opening its doors to share what they love doing. The Tesselaar family has been farming this land for three generations, and that history comes through in every aspect of the festival.

It's also a reminder of the importance of supporting local agriculture. When you buy bulbs from the plant sales or flowers from the retail nursery, you're supporting a Victorian family business that employs local people and maintains agricultural land in the face of suburban development pressure.

For Melbourne families, KaBloom has become a tradition—a marker of the seasons that signals autumn's arrival and gives children an up-close experience of where food and flowers come from. For visitors from further afield, it's one of Victoria's unique events, offering a rural experience just an hour from the city.
