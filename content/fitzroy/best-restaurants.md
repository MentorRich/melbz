Here are the 23 best restaurants in Fitzroy for 2026, from hatted fine dining to hidden neighbourhood gems. This guide covers every corner of Melbourne's most diverse food suburb, with venues across Brunswick, Gertrude, and Smith Streets. Whether you're after Andrew McConnell's legendary dry-aged beef, authentic Filipino breakfast, or charred miso ramen, Fitzroy delivers.

**What's inside:**
- **Fine dining:** Cutler, Marion, Gaea, Carnation Canteen
- **Neighbourhood essentials:** Poodle, Norma, Alta Trattoria, Bar Liberty
- **International flavours:** Añada (Spanish), Toddy Shop (Indian), Masti (Punjabi), Palay (Filipino)
- **Casual classics:** Belles Hot Chicken, Vegie Bar, Mario's, Rice Paper Scissors
- **Specialty spots:** Ramen Ako's, Gogyo (ramen), Deep End Pizza, Tamura Sake Bar

Forget what you think you know about Fitzroy. Yes, it's still got that artsy edge that made it famous, but the food scene here has evolved into something extraordinary. This isn't your mate's share house suburb anymore — it's Melbourne's most democratic dining destination, where a $9 banh mi from a hole-in-the-wall shares the same street as hatted restaurants charging $240 for dry-aged beef.

Walking down Gertrude Street past Andrew McConnell's empire, you'll catch aromas of natural wine and fire-grilled octopus. Turn the corner to Brunswick Street, and it's ramen steam mixing with Nashville fried chicken smoke. Smith Street brings you Sri Lankan spices and French bistro charm. This is what makes Fitzroy special — it's never tried to be anything other than itself, and that authenticity has created Melbourne's most diverse culinary ecosystem, earning it top marks on our [Suburb Vibe Score](/vibe-score/).

The beauty of eating in Fitzroy is the choice. You can blow your monthly food budget on a degustation at Cutler, then walk 200 metres for late-night dumplings at a fraction of the price. Both experiences will be exceptional, because in Fitzroy, quality isn't about price point — it's about passion. From the teenage line cooks at the ramen joints to the sommeliers curating natural wine lists, everyone here takes food seriously. For more inner north dining, pair this with our [Carlton cafe guide](/carlton/best-cafes/), or explore all our [Fitzroy guides](/fitzroy/).

What's changed in 2026? The suburb has doubled down on what it does best — authentic neighbourhood dining that happens to be world-class. New openings like Palay are bringing Filipino flavours to Greeves Street, while institutions like Marion continue to set the standard for wine bar excellence. The result is a food scene that feels both cutting-edge and comfortably familiar, where innovation meets tradition over shared plates and natural wine. This guide is part of our comprehensive [Melbourne food guide](/food/), covering the city's best dining across every suburb.

## 1. Cutler

![Cutler restaurant interior](/images/venues/cutler.jpg)

**The vibe:** Andrew McConnell's flagship fine diner where Gertrude Street goes upscale without losing its soul.

Fifteen years on, Cutler remains the gold standard for Melbourne fine dining, housed in that stunning converted metalworks factory at 55-57 Gertrude Street. The 2026 menu still centres around that legendary $240 dry-aged rib-eye, but McConnell's seasonal approach means every visit feels fresh. The autumn vegetable entree with golden beetroot, red radish, fig, walnut and goat's cheese showcases why this place earned its hats — it's produce-driven cooking at its most elegant, in a space that feels distinctly Fitzroy rather than trying to cosplay as some European establishment.

**Order this:** Dry-aged rib-eye (1.2kg) ($240)  
**Also try:** Autumn vegetable entree with golden beetroot ($34)  
**Address:** 55-57 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065  
**Hours:** Wednesday to Saturday from 5:30pm, Sunday from 12pm  
**Price range:** $$$ ($160+ per person)  
**Book:** [OpenTable via Seven Rooms](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/cutlerandcoaustralia)  
**Insider tip:** Sunday lunch is legendary — book weeks ahead and pace yourself for the best long lunch in Melbourne.

## 2. Marion Wine Bar

![Marion Wine Bar](/images/venues/marion.jpg)

**The vibe:** The wine bar every other wine bar wishes it could be.

Sitting next door to Cutler, Marion might be the most impressive wine list in Fitzroy, which is saying something in a suburb full of serious grape lovers. The constantly changing Euro-inspired menu matches the wine program's ambition — think mussels with 'nduja, fried bread and aioli, or that silky chicken liver parfait with madeira that regulars rave about. The space spans two shopfronts, and whether you're at the bar or a proper table, the vibe is neighbourhood wine bar elevated to art form.

**Order this:** Chicken liver parfait with madeira and cornichons ($28)  
**Also try:** Mussels with 'nduja and fried bread ($32)  
**Address:** 51-53 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065  
**Hours:** Tuesday-Sunday from 5pm, Saturday-Sunday from 8am  
**Price range:** $$ ($60-80 per person)  
**Book:** [Seven Rooms](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/cutlerandcoaustralia)  
**Insider tip:** The sommelier's selection changes daily — trust their recommendations for the best natural wine discoveries.

## 3. Poodle Bar & Bistro

![Poodle Bar & Bistro](/images/venues/poodle.jpg)

**The vibe:** Euro bistro meets Melbourne swagger in the most Instagram-worthy dining room in Fitzroy.

Poodle has become a Fitzroy institution in record time, and for good reason — Josh Fry's cooking combines French technique with Italian soul and just enough Australian irreverence to feel like home. The space feels like a grand European bistro that accidentally landed on Gertrude Street, with art-deco touches and enough personality to fill both shopfronts. Chef's Selection menu for groups of 6-12 takes the stress out of ordering and lets you experience the full range of Fry's talents.

**Address:** 81-83 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065  
**Hours:** Lunch Saturday & Sunday, dinner Tuesday-Sunday  
**Price range:** $$ ($70-90 per person)  
**Book:** [Seven Rooms](https://www.sevenrooms.com/reservations/poodlebarbistro)  
**Insider tip:** The vintage rib-eye is worth the splurge, especially when paired with one of their endless martinis.

## 5. Belles Hot Chicken

![Belles Hot Chicken](/images/venues/belles.jpg)

**The vibe:** Nashville heat meets Gertrude Street cool.

Since 2014, Belles has been perfecting the art of Nashville-style hot chicken, and by 2026, they've got it down to a science. The spice levels range from gentle to face-melting, and the crispy coating stays crunchy even when drenched in that signature hot oil. Weekend brunch brings chicken and waffles to Fitzroy, one of the few places in Melbourne doing it properly. The boozy slushies and craft beer selection help cool the fire.

**Order this:** Hot chicken sandwich with pickles ($22)  
**Also try:** Mac and cheese side ($12)  
**Address:** 150 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065  
**Hours:** Monday-Sunday 11:30am-9pm (until 9:30pm Friday-Saturday)  
**Price range:** $ ($25-35 per person)  
**Book:** Walk-in only  
**Insider tip:** Start with "Hot" spice level on your first visit — their "Fire" and "XXX" will genuinely test your limits.

## 7. Mario's

**The vibe:** Time-warp Italian that's been feeding Fitzroy since 1986.

Mario's feels like stepping into old Melbourne — crisp white tablecloths, neon signage, and that famous window bar overlooking Brunswick Street. This is simple Italian done right: handmade pasta, classic garlic bread, and coffee that tastes like it came straight from a Roman bar. The menu hasn't changed much in decades because it doesn't need to. Sometimes perfection is aglio e olio and a glass of house wine while watching Fitzroy walk past the window.

**Order this:** Spaghetti aglio e olio ($18)  
**Also try:** Garlic bread (obligatory) ($8)  
**Address:** 303 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065  
**Hours:** Daily 7am-late  
**Price range:** $ ($25-40 per person)  
**Book:** Walk-in only  
**Insider tip:** Grab a window seat for the full Mario's experience — people-watching on Brunswick Street is part of the entertainment.

## 15. Ramen Ako's

**The vibe:** Intimate ramen counter where less is definitely more.

With just 31 seats and three ramen options, Ako's proves that focus beats variety every time. Named for owner Sho Iijima's mother, this Brunswick Street spot honours her chicken soup tradition with two exceptional broths: light chintan or rich paitan, plus a standout vegan mushroom option. The simplicity extends to everything — gyoza, karaage, beer, and sake complete the offering without unnecessary complexity.

**Order this:** Chicken ramen with paitan (rich) broth ($18)  
**Also try:** Gyoza (6 pieces) ($12)  
**Address:** 368 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065  
**Hours:** Tuesday-Sunday 6pm-9pm (100 bowls daily maximum)  
**Price range:** $ ($25-35 per person)  
**Book:** Walk-in only  
**Insider tip:** Arrive early — once they've served 100 bowls, they close for the night.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best street for restaurants in Fitzroy?
Brunswick Street, Gertrude Street, and Smith Street each offer distinct dining experiences. Brunswick Street is known for its eclectic mix of casual eateries and institutions like Vegie Bar and Mario's. Gertrude Street delivers fine dining and wine bars like Cutler and Marion. Smith Street brings international flavours like Toddy Shop's Keralan cuisine. Explore all our [Fitzroy guides](/fitzroy/) for more.

### Is Fitzroy good for vegetarians?
Absolutely. Fitzroy has been a vegetarian and vegan hub for decades, with Vegie Bar leading the way since the 1990s. Many restaurants offer strong plant‑based options, and the suburb's diverse culinary scene ensures vegetarians won't miss out. Check our [Melbourne food guide](/food/) for more vegetarian‑friendly spots across the city.

### Where should I go for a special occasion in Fitzroy?
For celebrations, head to Cutler for its hatted fine dining and dry‑aged rib‑eye, or Gaea for an avant‑garde degustation experience. Marion Wine Bar offers a more intimate but equally impressive setting with its curated natural wine list. For something different, Carnation Canteen's ever‑changing Mediterranean menu delivers memorable dining. Pair your meal with a visit to one of Fitzroy's [best bars](/st-kilda/best-bars/) for after‑dinner drinks.

## The Bottom Line

Fitzroy's restaurant scene in 2026 represents Melbourne dining at its most diverse and democratic. Whether you're after a $15 bowl of ramen or a $200 fine dining experience, the quality bar remains consistently high across every price point. The suburb's strength lies in its refusal to choose between authenticity and innovation — you'll find traditional Italian cooking at Mario's, experimental fermentation at Gaea, and everything in between executed with the kind of passion that makes Melbourne's food scene world-renowned.

The best strategy for eating in Fitzroy? Start with the institutions to understand the foundation, then explore the newcomers to see where the scene is heading. Book where you can, but leave room for spontaneity — some of the best meals happen when you follow your nose down Brunswick Street and see what catches your attention.

Most importantly, eat with friends. Fitzroy restaurants are built for sharing — whether it's pintxos at Naked for Satan, family-style curries at Toddy Shop, or splitting that massive deep dish pizza at Deep End. The suburb's community spirit extends to its dining culture, where solo diners are welcomed but group meals are celebrated.
