## Gertrude Street: Where Tradition Meets Tomorrow

Gertrude Street remains Fitzroy’s cultural spine, but 2026 sees it evolving beyond its gallery-row reputation. The street now functions as a living laboratory for interdisciplinary practice, with long-standing institutions adapting to new realities while experimental spaces push boundaries.

**Gertrude Contemporary** (formerly Gertrude Street Projection Space) has transformed into a hybrid venue offering both physical exhibitions and immersive digital experiences. Their current show, "Algorithm Ancestors," features Indigenous artists using AI to reinterpret traditional patterns – a must-see until April 30th. Don’t miss their Friday night artist talks, which consistently draw thoughtful crowds.

Next door, **Collette Dinnigan Gallery** has shifted focus to sustainable fashion exhibitions, proving that craft and conscience can coexist. Their "Waste Not" showcase (running through June) transforms discarded textiles into wearable art, challenging fast fashion’s dominance.

The real surprise of 2026 Gertrude Street? **The Empire** – a former nightclub reborn as a 24-hour creative co-working space. By day, it houses designers and makers; by night, it becomes an underground performance venue. Their secret speakeasy-style comedy room in the basement sells out weeks in advance – ask for the password at the bar (hint: it changes weekly based on a local poet’s latest work).

## Johnston Street: The Creative Engine Room

While Gertrude Street gets the glory, Johnston Street is where Fitzroy’s creative muscles actually flex. This artery stretching from Smith Street to Nicholson Street hums with maker spaces, independent studios, and grassroots initiatives that keep the suburb’s DIY ethos alive.

**Jersey Road Arts Precinct** deserves special mention. This cluster of converted warehouses now hosts over 50 independent artists working in ceramics, textiles, and metalwork. Their open studio days (first Saturday monthly) offer rare access to creators in their natural habitat – bring cash for direct purchases and expect to leave with something uniquely Fitzroy.

For book lovers, **Hill of Content’s** Johnston Street annex has become a haven for zine creators and small press publishers. Their backroom "Micro Press Museum" showcases Melbourne’s underground publishing history, while their weekly zine-making workshops (Wednesdays 6pm) attract everyone from teens to retirees. Pro tip: their "Blind Date with a Zine" shelf lets you discover hidden voices for just $5.

Music throbs through Johnston Street’s veins. **The Old Bar** continues its legacy as a live music institution, but 2026 sees them prioritising local talent over touring acts. Their "Fitzroy Fridays" series showcases emerging Victorian bands – no cover before 10pm, and the burger special remains unbeatable value. Just around the corner, **Cherry Bar** has doubled down on soul and R&B, with their Sunday night gospel brunch becoming a legend worth waking up for.

## Street Art: Beyond the Postcard Murals

Fitzroy’s street art scene has matured beyond Instagram-friendly backdrops. In 2026, it’s a layered conversation between sanctioned works, illicit interventions, and community-driven projects that reflect the suburb’s social pulse.

Start your exploration at **Brunswick Street’s** infamous "Mural Mile," but look beyond the famous pieces. The real stories hide in the side streets: **Rose Street**’s ever-changing stencil layers comment on housing inequality, while ** Moor Street** hosts a rotating paste-up exhibition addressing climate anxiety – check the piece near the tram stop for the latest update.
