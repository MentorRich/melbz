Melbourne’s food scene doesn’t live in one postcode. It’s a sprawling, delicious mosaic that changes block by block, suburb by suburb. What you eat in Carlton is different from what you eat in St Kilda, which is different again from what you’ll find in Fitzroy. That’s the joy of eating here—every neighbourhood has its own personality, its own culinary traditions, and its own secret spots that locals guard fiercely.

This guide is your map to that diversity. We’ve broken down Melbourne’s best food by suburb, so you can explore with intention. Whether you’re hunting for the perfect espresso, a degustation that’ll blow your mind, or a cheap eat that tastes like a million bucks, we’ve got you covered. Forget the generic “top 10” lists—this is about understanding where to go, why it’s good, and how each suburb contributes to Melbourne’s reputation as one of the world’s great eating cities.

## Where to eat, suburb by suburb

### Fitzroy: The creative heart

Fitzroy’s dining scene is as bold and eclectic as its street art. Brunswick Street and Gertrude Street are lined with restaurants that range from casual bistros to white‑tablecloth destinations, plus wine bars that double as community hubs. Our [guide to Fitzroy’s best restaurants](/fitzroy/best-restaurants/) covers every essential bite, from morning coffee to late‑night snacks.

### Carlton: Little Italy and beyond

Carlton is where Melbourne’s coffee culture began, and the espresso machines are still humming. Lygon Street remains the spiritual home of Italian‑Australian dining, but you’ll also find modern Australian kitchens, Korean fried‑chicken joints and third‑wave roasters. Start with our [guide to Carlton’s best cafes](/carlton/best-cafes/) for your caffeine fix, then explore the trattorias and enotecas that line the strip.

### St Kilda: Bayside bites

St Kilda serves up seafood with a view, historic pubs with great parmas, and Acland Street’s legendary cake shops. The vibe is relaxed, the portions are generous, and the people‑watching is unmatched. For a proper taste of the bayside, check out our [St Kilda food guide](/st-kilda/best-restaurants/) (coming soon)—we’re currently compiling the must‑visit spots.

### Brunswick, Collingwood, Richmond and beyond

The inner‑north and inner‑east are packed with culinary gems. From Brunswick’s Middle Eastern bakeries to Collingwood’s hipster cafes and Richmond’s Vietnamese strip, there’s a world of flavours just a tram ride away. ## Frequently asked questions

### What’s the best food suburb in Melbourne?

It depends what you’re after. Fitzroy is hard to beat for variety and innovation, Carlton for coffee and Italian heritage, St Kilda for seafood and relaxed vibes. The real answer is to explore them all—they’re only 15‑20 minutes apart by tram.

### Is Melbourne expensive for eating out?

It can be, but it doesn’t have to be. Melbourne has a thriving cheap‑eat culture, from $5 banh mi to $15 bowls of ramen, $20 pizzas and pub meals under $25. Even at the higher end, prices are generally lower than Sydney or overseas capitals. Our suburb guides always include budget‑friendly options.

### Do I need to book restaurants in advance?

For popular spots, especially on weekends, yes. Many of Melbourne’s best restaurants are small and fill up days or weeks ahead. Use online booking platforms like Resy, OpenTable or the restaurant’s own website. For casual cafes and pubs, walk‑ins are usually fine, but there might be a wait during peak times.

### What’s the tipping culture in Melbourne?

Tipping is not expected in Australia—staff are paid a living wage. That said, if you receive exceptional service, a tip of 5‑10% is appreciated but never obligatory. Some restaurants add a weekend or public‑holiday surcharge (usually 10‑15%), which will be clearly noted on the menu.
