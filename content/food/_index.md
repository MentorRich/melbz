## Where to eat, suburb by suburb

### Fitzroy: The creative heart

Fitzroy\u2019s dining scene is as bold and eclectic as its street art. Brunswick Street and Gertrude Street are lined with restaurants that range from casual bistros to white\u2011tablecloth destinations, plus wine bars that double as community hubs. Our [guide to Fitzroy\u2019s best restaurants](/fitzroy/best-restaurants/) covers every essential bite, from morning coffee to late\u2011night snacks.

### Carlton: Little Italy and beyond

Carlton is where Melbourne\u2019s coffee culture began, and the espresso machines are still humming. Lygon Street remains the spiritual home of Italian\u2011Australian dining, but you\u2019ll also find modern Australian kitchens, Korean fried\u2011chicken joints and third\u2011wave roasters. Start with our [guide to Carlton\u2019s best cafes](/carlton/best-cafes/) for your caffeine fix, then explore the trattorias and enotecas that line the strip.

### St Kilda: Bayside bites

St Kilda serves up seafood with a view, historic pubs with great parmas, and Acland Street\u2019s legendary cake shops. The vibe is relaxed, the portions are generous, and the people\u2011watching is unmatched. For a proper taste of the bayside, check out our [St Kilda food guide](/st-kilda/best-restaurants/) (coming soon)\u2014we\u2019re currently compiling the must\u2011visit spots.

### Brunswick, Collingwood, Richmond and beyond

The inner\u2011north and inner\u2011east are packed with culinary gems. From Brunswick\u2019s Middle Eastern bakeries to Collingwood\u2019s hipster cafes and Richmond\u2019s Vietnamese strip, there\u2019s a world of flavours just a tram ride away. ## Frequently asked questions

### What\u2019s the best food suburb in Melbourne?

It depends what you\u2019re after. Fitzroy is hard to beat for variety and innovation, Carlton for coffee and Italian heritage, St Kilda for seafood and relaxed vibes. The real answer is to explore them all\u2014they\u2019re only 15\u201120 minutes apart by tram.

### Is Melbourne expensive for eating out?

It can be, but it doesn\u2019t have to be. Melbourne has a thriving cheap\u2011eat culture, from  banh mi to 5 bowls of ramen, 0 pizzas and pub meals under 5. Even at the higher end, prices are generally lower than Sydney or overseas capitals. Our suburb guides always include budget\u2011friendly options.

### Do I need to book restaurants in advance?

For popular spots, especially on weekends, yes. Many of Melbourne\u2019s best restaurants are small and fill up days or weeks ahead. Use online booking platforms like Resy, OpenTable or the restaurant\u2019s own website. For casual cafes and pubs, walk\u2011ins are usually fine, but there might be a wait during peak times.

### What\u2019s the tipping culture in Melbourne?

Tipping is not expected in Australia\u2014staff are paid a living wage. That said, if you receive exceptional service, a tip of 5\u201110% is appreciated but never obligatory. Some restaurants add a weekend or public\u2011holiday surcharge (usually 10\u201115%), which will be clearly noted on the menu.
