# Weekend Guide: Footscray 2026 — Markets, Dumplings & Sunsets

*Updated 16 March 2026 | Isabella Greco reporting*

---

Footscray has been Melbourne's most unfairly underrated suburb for long enough. While Fitzroy and Brunswick fight over brunch supremacy and Richmond hoards all the Vietnamese street cred, Footscray just keeps getting better — quietly, stubbornly, and on its own terms. This is the suburb where a Saturday morning means dodging prawn heads at the market, eating a $7 bowl of pho that would cost $22 in South Yarra, and watching the sun drop behind the Maribyrnong River with a $9 pint in your hand.

Here's how to spend a weekend in Footscray without putting a foot wrong.

## Saturday Morning: The Markets

### Footscray Market (89 Hopkins Street)

The market opens at 8am most Saturdays, and the sooner you get there, the better the seafood. By 10am, the prawns are still good but the oysters are thinning out and the fishmongers have already moved on to their afternoon crew. Go early. Go hungry.

This isn't Queen Victoria Market with its buskers and souvenir stalls. Footscray Market is raw and functional. The floor is perpetually wet. The butchers will tell you exactly which cut you need if you describe what you're cooking, and they won't charge you $40/kg for the privilege. Fresh sashimi-grade tuna runs about $20/kg. A kilo of tiger prawns will set you back around $25. If you're buying pork belly, ask for it scored — they'll do it on the spot.

The fruit and veg stalls lining the perimeter are cheaper than any supermarket within three suburbs. Expect to fill a bag for $15–20.

**Insider tip:** Walk straight to the back left corner where the Vietnamese families buy their groceries. That's where the best-value herbs, lemongrass, and fresh chillis live. Also, grab a takeaway coffee from the little Vietnamese place near the entrance — it's $3.50 and it'll keep you going while you shop.

### Barkly Square (Barkly Street, corner of Albert Street)

Just a five-minute walk from the main market, Barkly Square is where Footscray does its quiet Saturday morning community thing. The grassed area fills up with families, there's a playground for the kids, and the surrounding cafes hit a sweet spot between relaxed and caffeinated. **Grüven** on the Barkly Street side does a solid smashed avo ($16) and their flat white ($4.50) doesn't mess around.

If you're after something more substantial for brunch, the walk over to Seddon is worth it. **Seddon Deadly Sins** on Wilson Street has been a neighbourhood institution for years — their ricotta hotcakes ($19) with honeycomb butter are genuinely dangerous. Or try **The Grain Store** on Gamon Street in Seddon for a brunch that leans more European: house-baked sourdough, cultured butter, and eggs done properly. Expect to spend $20–28 per person for a full brunch with coffee.

## Saturday Late Morning: The Vietnamese Strip

Hopkins Street between Donald and Leeds Streets is the beating heart of Footscray's Vietnamese community, and it delivers some of the best cheap eats in all of Melbourne. Not "good for the price." Just genuinely excellent food that happens to cost very little.

### Where to Eat

**Pho Hung Vuong** (21 Hopkins Street) — The pho here is the reason people drive from across Melbourne. A bowl of rare beef pho is $14, and it's the real deal: deep, clean broth that's been simmering for hours, fresh rice noodles, and a plate of herbs that you load up yourself. There's usually a queue by 11:30am on Saturdays. Get there at 11am and you'll slide in.

**Banh Mi Boys** (various spots along Hopkins) — You can get a banh mi for between $6 and $9 depending on your filling. The pork roll with pâté and pickled vegetables is the classic. If you're feeling adventurous, go for the lemongrass chicken. These places are fast, cheap, and consistently better than anything in the CBD.

**Huong Viet** (86 Hopkins Street) — Slightly more sit-down than the banh mi joints. Their broken rice plate (cơm tấm) with grilled pork chop, egg, and fish sauce runs about $16 and is a full meal that'll keep you going until dinner. The fresh spring rolls ($9 for a plate of four) are the move if you're snacking between market stops.

### Getting Between Them

The entire strip is walkable. Park on Hopkins or off-street on one of the side streets (watch the permit zones — some areas are council-permit only on Saturdays). If you're coming by train, Footscray Station is a 4-minute walk to the start of the strip. The 216 and 220 buses also run along Hopkins Street.

## Saturday Afternoon: Maribyrnong River & Egress

After the food coma sets in, walk it off along the Maribyrnong River Trail. This is one of Melbourne's most underrated riverside walks — flat, paved, and far less crowded than the Yarra trail. From Footscray, head west along the trail towards Highpoint or east towards Ascot Vale. The path is fully accessible — smooth, flat, step-free — and there are benches along the river for sitting and staring at your life choices.

### Maribyrnong River Trail

- **Where to start:** Footscray Park (off Racecourse Road) or the river path access near Hopkins Street
- **Distance:** You can walk as far as you like — 2km to the Flying Saucepan sculpture or 5km+ to Highpoint
- **Cost:** Free
- **Time:** Allow 45 minutes to 2 hours depending on your ambition level

In March, the late afternoon light on the river is golden. This is prime sunset-watching territory. Grab a takeaway coffee and a pastry from **Bakery Hill** on Hopkins Street (their almond croissant is $6.50 and worth every cent) and walk the trail as the afternoon slides into evening.

**Getting home safe:** The river trail is well-lit along the main stretches near Footscray Park, but some of the side paths get dark after sunset. Stick to the main paved path after 7pm. Footscray Police Station is at 467 Barkly Street.

## Saturday Night: Bars & Dinner

Footscray's bar scene has matured considerably. It's still not Chapel Street — and that's exactly the point.

### Bars

**The Fox Hotel** (71–73 Wellington Street) — A Footscray classic that's been around through every wave of gentrification talk. The rooftop is where you want to be on a Saturday evening — small, no pretension, and the views over the suburb are surprisingly good. Pints run $10–13 depending on what's on tap. They do decent pub food if you need a top-up between drinks.

**Lulie Street Eatery** (66–68 Lulie Street, Abbotsford — okay, technically Abbotsford, but it's a 10-minute drive from Footscray and the vibe fits) — Craft beer focused with a rotating tap list. Pints $10–14. Good if you want to avoid the Fitzroy crowds on a Saturday.

**B.EAST** (807 Nicholson Street, Brunswick East — worth the trip) — Not in Footscray proper, but if you're bar-hopping and want to continue the night, this spot has a massive beer garden and live music most Saturdays. Pints from $9.

### Dinner

**Taj Indian Restaurant** (478 Barkly Street) — An old-school Indian that doesn't try to be fancy. Butter chicken ($18), lamb rogan josh ($20), garlic naan ($3.50). This is comfort food that does exactly what it says on the tin.

**Ying Chow** (12 Hopkins Street) — Cantonese-style Chinese that's been serving Footscray for decades. Salt and pepper squid ($14), sweet and sour pork ($16), fried rice ($12). A feed for two will cost you about $50–60 with drinks. Cash only last time I checked — bring notes.

**Saha** (323 Barkly Street) — Lebanese-meets-Middle Eastern. The mixed grill plate ($28) is enormous and perfect for sharing. Their hummus is silky and their flatbread is made fresh. BYO-friendly, which keeps the total cost down if you bring a bottle.

## What We Skipped and Why

**The new "artisan food hall" on Leeds Street** — It's shiny and Instagram-friendly, but the portions are small, the prices are CBD-level, and the food doesn't match the hype. Three months old and already feeling like a tourist trap. Wait six months for the rent to bite and the mediocre vendors to cycle out.

**Any Footscray restaurant charging more than $35 for a main** — There's a creeping wave of overpriced dining that hasn't earned its price tag. A few new spots on Hopkins are charging Fitzroy prices for food that's still finding its feet. We'll revisit in winter when they've figured out their identity.

**The weekend markets at Footscray Community Arts Centre** — Great concept, patchy execution. Some weekends it's full of interesting makers and local artists; other weekends it's three candles and a lady selling resin coasters. Go if it's on, but don't plan your weekend around it.

## Cross-Link Play: Extending Your Footscray Weekend

One of the best things about Footscray's location is how easily it connects to surrounding suburbs for different vibes:

**Seddon** (one train stop away on the Sunbury line) — More refined brunch territory and smaller, curated shops. If Footscray is the loud, unapologetic sibling, Seddon is the one who reads novels and has opinions about linen. Worth a morning walk from Footscray Station — it's literally the next stop.

**Yarraville** (one stop further on the train) — Home to the gorgeous Sun Theatre, a 1930s art deco cinema that's still showing films. Pair a Saturday night movie with dinner at one of Yarraville's quieter restaurants. Tickets around $17–20. The whole village feels like a step back in time.

**West Melbourne** (towards the CBD) — If you're heading into the city after your Footscray weekend, the 57 tram from West Melbourne takes you straight into the CBD. Or walk through E-Gate and Flagstaff Gardens for a nice transition from west-side grit to city polish.

## Getting There & Getting Home

- **Train:** Footscray Station is on the Sunbury and Werribee lines. From Flinders Street, it's about 12 minutes.
- **Tram:** Route 82 runs along Footscray Road and connects to Moonee Ponds.
- **Driving:** Available on weekends but parking on Hopkins Street fills up by 10am. Try the side streets off Barkly Street or use the free car park near the market.
- **Night home:** The last Sunbury line train from Footscray on Saturday night is around 1:30am. Outside that window, rideshare runs about $18–25 to the CBD.

## The Verdict

Footscray doesn't try to be Melbourne's coolest suburb. It doesn't need to be. What it offers is something rarer: authenticity that hasn't been polished into blandness. The market is real, the food is cheap and genuinely excellent, the river walk is peaceful without being precious, and the bars are full of actual locals who've been coming here for years.

Come for the pho. Stay for the sunset. And for the love of god, don't call it "up and coming" — it's been here the whole time.
