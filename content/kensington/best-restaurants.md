# Best Restaurants in Kensington 2026: Where to Eat

Kensington's food scene is proper. Tucked between the tram lines and Racecourse Road, this pocket of Melbourne has quietly built itself into one of the west's most underrated dining strips. No flashy openings, no Instagram stunts — just bloody good food from people who actually care about what they're serving.

Walk down Bellair Street any weeknight and you'll see what we mean: locals queuing for tacos at La Tortilleria, couples sharing Ethiopian injera at The Abyssinian, and shift workers grabbing Thai curry from PIMM before the late service. This isn't tourist food. This is neighbourhood eating at its finest.

## 1. The Abyssinian
**The vibe:** Intimate Ethiopian warmth where eating with your hands isn't just encouraged — it's the whole point

The Abyssinian serves the kind of food that makes you reconsider everything you thought you knew about Melbourne dining. This 40-seat restaurant on Racecourse Road has been dishing up authentic Ethiopian and Eritrean cuisine for over a decade, and frankly, it should be packed every night.

The injera bread arrives like a spongy tablecloth, ready to be torn and used as both plate and cutlery. The vegetarian combination ($28) is a revelation — lentils, chickpeas, and vegetables that have been slow-cooked with berbere spice until they sing. If you eat meat, the doro wot (chicken stew, $32) will ruin you for every other curry you've ever had.

**Order this:** Vegetarian combination ($28) — even carnivores leave converted
**Address:** 277 Racecourse Road, Flemington (right on the Kensington border)
**Hours:** Tuesday to Sunday, 5:30pm-10pm
**Insider tip:** They don't take bookings — rock up at 6pm or prepare to wait. The wait is worth it.

## 2. La Tortilleria
**The vibe:** Mexico City taqueria vibes in a Kensington warehouse — as authentic as it gets this side of the Pacific

La Tortilleria doesn't mess about. This is proper Mexican food, not the cheese-heavy Tex-Mex nonsense that passes for "Mexican" at most Melbourne joints. They make their own masa harina tortillas on-site, and once you taste the difference, you'll never go back to the supermarket cardboard.

The al pastor ($6 each) are textbook perfect — marinated pork, charred pineapple, white onion, coriander. Simple, honest, magnificent. The fish tacos ($7 each) use fresh barramundi with a slaw that cuts through the richness perfectly. Come hungry and order five different types. Share them. Fight over the last carnitas.

**Order this:** Start with 2 al pastor and 2 fish, then order more
**Address:** 72 Stubbs Street, Kensington
**Hours:** Monday-Thursday 5:30pm-9pm, Friday-Saturday 12pm-9:30pm
**Insider tip:** BYOB with a $2 corkage fee — grab a Dos Equis from the bottle shop next door

---

## 3. Mama Le
**The vibe:** Vietnamese fusion that takes mum's recipes seriously but isn't afraid to add a contemporary twist

Mama Le gets Vietnamese fusion right by starting with Vietnamese fundamentals and building from there. No fusion for fusion's sake — just smart tweaks that make sense. The "feed me" banquet at $49 per person is phenomenal value and takes the decision-making out of your hands completely.

The soft-shell crab banh mi ($18) shouldn't work but absolutely does — crispy crab in a pillowy baguette with proper Vietnamese pickles and herbs. The beef pho ($16) is traditional and excellent, but it's the duck pancakes ($22) that'll have you booking your next table before you've finished eating.

**Order this:** Feed me banquet ($49pp) — trust the kitchen
**Address:** 31 Stubbs Street, Kensington
**Hours:** Wednesday-Saturday dinners
**Insider tip:** Book ahead — this 35-seat restaurant fills up fast on weekends

## 4. White Rabbit Record Bar
**The vibe:** Vinyl-spinning hybrid where you can browse records while waiting for your mushroom toast

White Rabbit is that rare thing: a concept that could easily be wanky but absolutely isn't. Half record store, half cafe-bar, it sits opposite Kensington Station playing proper music while serving solid food and excellent coffee. The kind of place you duck into for a quick coffee and emerge two hours later with three LPs and a mild hangover.

The mushroom toast ($16) is a thing of beauty — field mushrooms, goat's cheese, and herbs on sourdough that's been griddled to perfection. The burger ($22) is no-nonsense excellent. The wine list focuses on natural drops, and the beer selection runs deep into craft territory.

**Order this:** Mushroom toast ($16) and whatever wine they're pouring by the glass
**Address:** 176 Bellair Street, Kensington
**Hours:** Wednesday-Saturday 11am-9pm (11pm Thursday-Friday)
**Insider tip:** Check the gig guide — intimate acoustic sets happen regularly

## 5. PIMM Thai Cafe
**The vibe:** Honest neighbourhood Thai that's been getting the fundamentals right for years

PIMM Thai Cafe is the kind of local Thai joint every suburb needs but few suburbs get. No fusion experiments, no Instagram-worthy presentations — just proper Thai cooking at prices that won't destroy your weekly budget. The green curry ($18) has proper heat, the pad thai ($16) isn't oversweetened, and the massaman beef ($19) is slow-cooked until it falls apart.

This isn't destination dining — it's Tuesday night dinner when you can't be bothered cooking. And sometimes that's exactly what you need. The tom yum soup ($14) will clear your sinuses and warm your soul, especially in winter when the westerly winds are howling down Bellair Street.

**Order this:** Massaman beef curry ($19) — comfort food perfection
**Address:** 170 Bellair Street, Kensington
**Hours:** Monday-Saturday 5pm-10pm
**Insider tip:** They do takeaway discounts — 10% off orders over $30

## 6. The Premises
**The vibe:** Industrial-chic breakfast and lunch spot where the coffee hits harder than the 6am freight train

The Premises occupies a corner site that could have been just another generic cafe but instead delivers something special. Exposed brick, green subway tiles, and serious coffee from Seven Seeds. The breakfast menu changes seasonally but always includes their signature scrambled eggs ($16) — creamy, perfect, served with sourdough that's baked fresh daily.

The lunch offerings are equally impressive. The roast beef sandwich ($17) is generous without being stupid, and the seasonal salads actually taste like someone put thought into them. This is the kind of cafe that makes you move to the neighbourhood just so you can be a regular.

**Order this:** Scrambled eggs ($16) — simple perfection, done properly
**Address:** 202 Bellair Street, Kensington
**Hours:** Monday-Friday 7am-4:30pm, Saturday-Sunday 8am-4pm
**Insider tip:** Park on McCracken Street rather than Bellair — meters are 60 cents cheaper

## What We Skipped and Why

**The Kensington Fish & Chips** — Solid local chippy but nothing that'll make you travel across town. Good for when you need hot chips at 8pm on a Tuesday, not much more.

**Parrot Seafood BBQ House** — Cantonese seafood that's perfectly fine but doesn't crack the must-visit list when The Abyssinian and La Tortilleria are doing more interesting things nearby.

**Sugar Skulls** — Mexican that tries too hard to be everything to everyone. La Tortilleria does authentic Mexican better; stick with what works.

We also checked out a few spots that are technically in Flemington but serve the same catchment — **Wayo Japanese Dining** on Racecourse Road is excellent for sushi and bento, but it's geographically Flemington's problem, not Kensington's glory.

---

## The Bottom Line

Kensington's dining scene punches way above its weight for a suburb most people only know as "that place near the train station." The Ethiopian at The Abyssinian alone makes this strip worth the journey from anywhere in Melbourne. Add proper Mexican, excellent Vietnamese fusion, and cafes that understand coffee is serious business, and you've got a food destination hiding in plain sight.
