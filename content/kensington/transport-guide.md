Kensington sits 4km from the CBD in Melbourne's inner west. Transport is one of the suburb's strongest selling points, with two train stations providing direct CBD access in under 10 minutes.

## Trains

Kensington's biggest transport advantage is having two train stations.

**Kensington station** sits on the **Craigieburn and Sunbury lines**. It is the primary station for most of the suburb and takes roughly 10 minutes to reach Flinders Street Station. Trains run every 7-10 minutes during peak hours.

**South Kensington station** serves the **Werribee and Williamstown lines** and sits at the southern end of the suburb. Also about 10 minutes to Flinders Street. This station is particularly useful for residents in the southern pocket and for connections to the western suburbs.

Both stations are small but functional. Having two stations on different line groups means if one line has disruptions, you have a backup. That is a genuine practical advantage that many inner suburbs lack.

## Buses

The **402 bus** runs through Kensington and connects to Footscray and other western suburbs. It fills the gaps where trains do not reach directly.

No tram routes run through Kensington itself. However, the **57 tram** on Racecourse Road (on the Flemington border) provides tram access for residents near the northern end.

## Cycling

The **Moonee Ponds Creek trail** runs along Kensington's western edge and connects directly to the CBD via Docklands. The ride takes about 15-20 minutes at a comfortable pace and is mostly off-road.

The trail also connects north through Flemington, Moonee Ponds, and beyond. For daily commuting or weekend exercise, it is one of the better cycling corridors in the western suburbs.

Terrain is flat, which suits all fitness levels. Bike storage varies by building. Melbourne's helmet law applies.

## Driving and Parking

Kensington has better parking than most inner-city suburbs. Many houses have driveways or garages. Street parking is generally available, though it tightens near Bellair Street and Macaulay Road during busy periods.

The suburb has good arterial access via Macaulay Road and Epsom Road. Peak hour traffic on these routes can add 10-15 minutes to a CBD trip that would otherwise take 10 minutes.

Race days at the nearby Flemington Racecourse bring significant traffic and parking pressure, particularly on Melbourne Cup Day and other major events.

## FAQ

**What train lines serve Kensington?**
Kensington station serves the Craigieburn and Sunbury lines. South Kensington station serves the Werribee and Williamstown lines.

**Can you live in Kensington without a car?**
Yes, comfortably. Two train stations cover commuting. The Moonee Ponds Creek trail serves cycling. Walking reaches Bellair Street amenities. A car is helpful for larger grocery runs and weekend trips but not essential.

**Are there trams in Kensington?**
Not directly, but the 57 tram on Racecourse Road (Flemington border) is walkable from the northern part of the suburb.

## Verdict

Kensington earns an A- for transport. Two train stations on different line groups, a dedicated cycling trail to the CBD, and better parking than most inner suburbs. The lack of direct tram access is the only notable gap. For commuters, this is one of the best-connected suburbs in the inner west.
