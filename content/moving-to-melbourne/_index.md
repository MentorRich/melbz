Moving to Melbourne is one of the better decisions you'll make. Living in the wrong suburb of Melbourne is one of the worse ones.

The gap between "I love this city" and "I need to move back" is often just a bad suburb fit \u2014 too far from work, too expensive for what you get, the wrong kind of neighbourhood for how you actually live. This guide exists to help you get that right from the start.

We've covered 321 suburbs. Here's how to find yours.

## Step One: Settle the Big Trade-off First

Before you start researching suburbs, be honest about your non-negotiables:

**\u2192 Commute or lifestyle?**
Inner suburbs (3km from the CBD) give you walkability, density, and a 15-minute commute. They cost more. Middle-ring suburbs (5\u201315km) give you space for less money, but you're on public transport or in a car. Outer suburbs (15km+) give you the most space for the money, but Melbourne's outer ring is huge and some of it is genuinely remote.

**\u2192 Apartment or house?**
If you need a house with a garden, your inner-city budget evaporates fast. The same money that gets you a two-bedroom apartment in Fitzroy gets you a three-bedroom house with parking in Preston or Reservoir.

**\u2192 Car or no car?**
Melbourne's train network is a spine that runs north-south, not a grid. If you don't own a car, you need to be near a train line or a tram route with direct CBD access. Many suburbs look close on a map but aren't connected.

## The Real Melbourne Property Question: Rent or Buy?

### Renting

Melbourne's rental market is tight across the inner and middle ring. A few facts to set expectations:

- Inner suburbs (within 5km): 80\u201350+/week for a 2-bedroom, depending on quality
- Middle ring (5\u201315km): 60\u201350/week for a 2-bedroom
- Outer ring (15km+): 80\u201300/week for a 3-bedroom house

Vacancy rates have been hovering under 2% across inner Melbourne. Have references, payslips, and a cover letter ready. Properties at the right price go in hours.

### Buying

Melbourne median house price sits around **30,000** (Metro-wide, Q1 2026). The inner suburbs skew this significantly:

- **Inner ring:** .2M\u2013M+ for a house
- **Middle ring:** 00K\u2013.2M
- **Outer ring:** 00K\u201350K

First home buyers: look at the [First Home Guarantee](https://www.nhfic.gov.au/support-to-buy-a-home/first-home-guarantee) scheme. Victoria also has stamp duty concessions on homes under 00K.

## The Melbourne Commute: What's Actually Realistic

Melbourne's CBD is the centre of gravity for most jobs. Here's a realistic commute map:

| Zone | Example Suburbs | Commute to CBD | Mode |
|------|----------------|----------------|------|
| Zone 1 (inner) | Fitzroy, Richmond, Collingwood | 10\u201320 min | Tram or short train |
| Zone 2 | Brunswick, Footscray, Hawthorn | 20\u201335 min | Train or tram |
| Zone 3 | Preston, Box Hill, Moonee Ponds | 30\u201345 min | Train |
| Zone 4 (middle) | Coburg, Reservoir, Ringwood | 40\u201355 min | Train |
| Zone 5 (outer) | Frankston, Craigieburn, Pakenham | 60\u201380 min | Train |

If you're commuting daily and buying in Zone 5 to save money, run the actual numbers: the fare, the time cost, and the mental load. Sometimes the inner-ring premium makes sense.

## Five Questions to Ask Before You Sign a Lease

1. **When does the rubbish/recycling get picked up?** (Yes, this matters. You'll work it out within the first week.)
2. **Is there a car park or permit zone?** (Very different things. Know the difference before you move in.)
3. **Which direction does the main living area face?** (North-facing = warm in winter; south-facing = possibly a cave. Especially relevant in apartments.)
4. **Is there a laundromat nearby?** (If the property has no washing machine and the nearest laundromat is 2km away, reconsider.)
5. **What's the mobile coverage like inside?** (Test it before you sign. Some inner-city apartments are concrete bunkers for both signal and wifi planning.)
