Every suburb has a different kind of Saturday night. In St Kilda, it might start with a sunset cocktail on a rooftop overlooking the bay, then move to a heritage pub for a live band. In Fitzroy, you could be debating the merits of natural wine in a hidden bar behind a bookcase, then dancing in a warehouse turned club. In Carlton, the night is shaped by espresso‑martini‑fueled conversations in a moody wine bar, or a pint in a student‑filled beer garden.

Melbourne’s nightlife isn’t a monolith—it’s a collection of scenes, each with its own rhythm, crowd and signature drink. This guide is your key to unlocking them. We’ve mapped out the best bars, pubs, live‑music venues and late‑night spots suburb by suburb, so you can find the vibe that matches your mood. No generic “top 10” lists—just genuine local intel on where to go, when to arrive, and what to order.

## Where to drink, suburb by suburb

### St Kilda: Bayside beats

St Kilda’s nightlife is as iconic as its palm trees. Fitzroy Street is lined with historic pubs, many with live music and sprawling beer gardens. The Esplanade offers rooftop bars with bay views, while Acland Street keeps things sweet with cocktail lounges and late‑night cake shops. Start with our [guide to St Kilda’s best bars](/st-kilda/best-bars/) for the essential stops.

### Fitzroy: Creative cocktails

Fitzroy is Melbourne’s laboratory for innovative drinking. Here you’ll find tiny bars hidden down laneways, wine bars with encyclopedic lists, and pubs that double as gig venues. Brunswick Street and Gertrude Street are the main strips, but the real gems are often tucked away. Our Fitzroy bar guide is in the works—check back soon for the full low‑down.

### Carlton: Wine bars and student pubs

Carlton’s nightlife is a mix of sophistication and student energy. Lygon Street is home to intimate wine bars perfect for a date, as well as rowdy pubs where academic debates spill onto the footpath. The university crowd keeps things lively during semester, while the Italian‑Australian institutions provide a more settled, convivial atmosphere year‑round.

### Brunswick, Collingwood, Richmond and beyond

The inner‑north and inner‑east are packed with nightlife options. Brunswick’s Sydney Road is a strip of live‑music venues and eclectic bars, Collingwood’s Smith Street is a mix of dive bars and cocktail lounges, and Richmond’s Swan Street offers sports pubs and sleek rooftop spots. ## Frequently asked questions

### What time do bars close in Melbourne?

Most pubs and bars close between midnight and 1am Sunday‑Thursday, and between 1am and 3am on Friday and Saturday. Some late‑night venues (particularly in the CBD and inner‑city) have licenses until 5am or later, but these are less common than they used to be. Always check the venue’s website or socials for current hours.

### Is there a dress code?

Melbourne is generally casual. Jeans and sneakers are fine in most pubs and bars. Some upscale cocktail lounges or rooftop bars might have a “smart casual” expectation (no thongs, singlets or ripped clothing), but it’s rarely enforced rigidly. When in doubt, darker jeans and a collared shirt or neat top will get you in anywhere.

### How much does a night out cost?

A pint of craft beer typically costs $10‑$14, a glass of wine $9‑$16, and a cocktail $20‑$28. Many pubs offer happy‑hour deals (often 4‑6pm) with discounted drinks. Cover charges for live‑music venues range from $10‑$30, depending on the act. Lockout laws are no longer in effect, so you can move between venues freely.

### Is Melbourne safe at night?

Generally, yes. The inner‑city suburbs are well‑lit and busy until late, with plenty of people around. As always, stay aware of your surroundings, stick to well‑populated areas, and use licensed taxis or ride‑share services to get home. Police patrol entertainment precincts on weekends.
