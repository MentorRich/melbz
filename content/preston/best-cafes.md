## The Cafe Scene in Preston

Preston takes its coffee seriously — this is Melbourne after all. Whether you need a pre-work flat white, a lazy Saturday brunch, or somewhere to park yourself with a laptop for three hours, this suburb has options across High Street, Plenty Road, Murray Road, and the quieter side streets.

## Moon Rabbit — High Street

**The neighbourhood local**

Moon Rabbit is the kind of cafe where the staff know the regulars by name. Small, affordable, and genuinely warm. The menu does jaffles, house-made slices, and solid breakfast options that won't break the bank. The coffee is consistently good without any of the theatre. It's a "I live two streets away and this is my local" kind of place — and there's nothing wrong with that.

## Arepa Days — Dundas Place

**The one doing something different**

Colombian arepas and rich, full-bodied coffee that's a world away from the standard Melbourne flat white. Tucked away on Dundas Place off High Street, Arepa Days brings genuine Colombian flavour — arepas loaded with scrambled eggs and cheese, spiced hot chocolate, and sugarcane juice. The space is small and fills up fast on weekends. Arrive before 9:30am.

## George Jones — Murray Road

**The polished sit-down**

George Jones occupies the sweet spot between casual brunch cafe and proper restaurant. Excellent coffee from a local roaster, ricotta hotcakes that are worth the trip, and enough room that you don't feel like you're elbowing strangers. More refined than most Preston cafes — popular with families on Saturday mornings and couples on Sundays.

## Skinny's — High Street

**The breakfast sandwich specialist**

Skinny's is primarily food-first — the breakfast sandwiches and hoagies are the main draw — but the coffee program punches above what you'd expect. Well-extracted espresso, properly steamed milk, and flat whites that are consistently good. Casual, no-frills atmosphere. The kind of sit-down, eat-well, drink-well place Preston does best.

## Eat Cannoli — High Street area

**Coffee and the best cannoli in the north**

Famous for hand-crafted, gluten-free cannoli (the OG with whipped ricotta, candied orange, chocolate chips, and rooftop-bee honey), but the coffee deserves its own mention. Solid espresso-based menu that complements the pastries perfectly. Small space, mostly takeaway — grab a coffee and a cannoli and eat them on the walk.

## The Brickie & The Barista — Preston/[Reservoir](/reservoir/) border

**The northern edge go-to**

Sitting on the border between Preston and Reservoir near Plenty Road, The Brickie & The Barista serves both suburbs with reliably good coffee. Experienced baristas, well-chosen beans, consistent extraction. Straightforward breakfast menu, well-priced. If you're exploring the northern part of Preston, this is your stop before heading into [Reservoir](/reservoir/).

## Tips for Finding Your Spot

1. **Walk the back streets** — the best cafes aren't always on the main drag. Dundas Place is a prime example
2. **Go on a weekday first** — easier to get a feel for the place without weekend chaos
3. **Ask locals** — the barista at one cafe will usually tell you their favourite cafe (which is someone else's)
4. **Check the food** — great coffee with bad food is a red flag for the overall standard
5. **Look at who's there** — regulars and locals mean the place has earned repeat business

## FAQ

**What's the best coffee in Preston?**
George Jones on Murray Road for a refined espresso, Arepa Days on Dundas Place for Colombian-style, Moon Rabbit on High Street for honest everyday coffee.

**Are there cafes with good WiFi for working in Preston?**
Jackson Dodds on Plenty Road has the space and WiFi for laptop work. George Jones also works well on quieter weekday mornings.

**Where's the best cafe near Preston Market?**
Skinny's on High Street is the closest quality option to the market precinct.

## The Verdict

Preston's cafe scene is strong and growing. You won't struggle to find good coffee within a short walk from anywhere in the suburb. The variety — from Colombian-style to Italian-influenced to classic Melbourne flat whites — means there's something for every mood. The back streets off High Street reward exploration.
