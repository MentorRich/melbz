## Eating Out in Preston

Preston's restaurant scene reflects a suburb where Vietnamese pho joints, Turkish bakeries, fire-cooked modern Australian restaurants, and Italian pasta houses share the same strip. High Street between Bell Street and Murray Road is one of Melbourne's most underrated food corridors. You don't need to leave the suburb for a good meal — the variety is what makes it work.

## Dexter — High Street

**Best for:** Fire-cooked modern Australian that feels special without being stuffy

Dexter does modern Australian with a focus on fire — meat and vegetables kissed by flame, served with seasonal accompaniments that change regularly. The menu is concise, the execution is consistent, and the atmosphere is relaxed without being casual. Wine list is tight and well-chosen. Book ahead for Friday and Saturday nights.

## Tavolata — High Street

**Best for:** Handmade pasta that punches above the suburb's weight class

Tavolata is Dexter's sibling, doing handmade pasta in a room that's more casual than Dexter but more refined than a standard trattoria. Pasta dishes rotate with the seasons — expect cacio e pepe, ragu-based dishes, and seasonal specials. Portions are generous, prices are reasonable, and the atmosphere lets you linger over a second glass of wine without anyone giving you the look.

## Pho Hung — High Street

**Best for:** The $14 pho that Melbourne food writers keep coming back to

Pho Hung has been serving Vietnamese pho since before "foodie culture" existed. A bowl of rare beef pho costs around $14 with a mountain of fresh herbs, bean sprouts, chilli, and lime. The broth is deeply flavoured, clear, and consistent — no corners cut on the 12-hour simmer. Rice paper rolls $6-8, vermicelli bowls around $12. At lunchtime, the queue can stretch out the door. Turn up at 11:30am or after 1:30pm.

## Lam Lam — High Street

**Best for:** The pho rival that locals will fight over

Down the road from Pho Hung, Lam Lam is the other pillar of Preston's pho scene. The broth is slightly richer, the portions generous. The bun cha (grilled pork with vermicelli noodles) at $13-15 is one of the best in Melbourne's north. The Vietnamese pancakes (banh xeo) are crispy, loaded, and come with a dipping sauce worth the visit alone.

## Benzina Cantina — High Street

**Best for:** Mexican with a rooftop and margaritas

Neon-lit space with cactus-lined interiors and a rooftop bar that catches the afternoon sun. Tacos loaded with pork belly, spiced chicken, or mixed veg. Margaritas properly made. The rooftop on a warm evening with tacos and a margarita is one of Preston's best dining experiences. Go early or be prepared to wait for the rooftop.

## Chumanchu — Gilbert Road

**Best for:** Vietnamese-Thai that's been quietly excellent for years

Chumanchu has been serving Preston from Gilbert Road since 2018, with a 2025 renovation expanding the kitchen and seating. Vietnamese with Thai influences — the pho, bun cha, and Thai curries are all excellent. The larger space means less waiting for a table, making it more viable as a dinner destination.

## El Jannah Charcoal Chicken — Preston

**Best for:** Lebanese charcoal chicken that's become a Melbourne institution

Charcoal chicken seasoned with a spice blend refined over decades. A quarter chicken with garlic sauce, pickles, and bread runs around $12-14. The sides — tabbouleh, hummus, fattoush, batata harra — are equally good. A full meal for two for under $30.

## Weeknight vs Weekend Dining

**Weeknights** are your sweet spot. Walk-in friendly, quieter, and you get the kitchen's attention. Tuesday to Thursday is when locals eat out casually. **Weekends** bring the crowds — book ahead for Dexter and Tavolata, or go early (6pm) to beat the rush.

## FAQ

**What's the best restaurant in Preston for a date night?**
Dexter on High Street for something special, Tavolata for pasta with wine, Benzina Cantina for rooftop margaritas.

**Where's the best cheap dinner in Preston?**
Pho Hung or Lam Lam on High Street — both under $15 for a full meal. El Jannah does a quarter chicken dinner for $12-14.

**Is there good Italian food in Preston?**
Tavolata does handmade pasta that rivals inner-city Italian spots at better prices.

**What cuisines can you find in Preston?**
Vietnamese, Italian, modern Australian, Mexican, Thai, Lebanese, Turkish, Chinese — the multicultural mix is one of Preston's biggest strengths.

## The Verdict

Preston's restaurant scene covers genuine range. You can have a $14 pho that's among Melbourne's best, then come back the next night for fire-cooked modern Australian at Dexter. The mid-range is where Preston shines — restaurants that care about quality but don't gouge on price. High Street and Gilbert Road between them cover every mood and budget.
