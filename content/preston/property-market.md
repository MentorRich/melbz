## The Preston Property Market — What You Need to Know

Buying in Preston is a significant financial decision. Here's the reality of the market — not a sales pitch, not doom and gloom, just the facts and context you need.

See our [Preston Cost of Living](/preston/cost-of-living/) guide for the broader picture.

## What Kind of Property Can You Buy?

Preston's housing stock spans the full range:

- **Apartments** — New builds and established units. The entry point for most first-time buyers. Quality varies enormously — inspect thoroughly.
- **Townhouses** — Middle ground. Better for families, usually newer construction, often with small outdoor space.
- **Freestanding Houses** — The premium end. Older stock dominates along streets off **High Street**, **Bell Street**, and **Murray Road**. Land value drives the price.
- **Heritage Properties** — Character homes that attract renovation-minded buyers. Can be money pits or goldmines depending on skill and budget.

## Market Conditions — 2026

Median house price hovers around **$1.05-1.3 million**. Units and townhouses offer more realistic entry points, particularly for first-home buyers. Preston's fundamentals — 10km from CBD, three train stations on the Mernda line, Preston Market, and High Street's food scene — underpin consistent demand.

The suburb sits in the **City of Darebin** (postcode 3072). Properties on quieter streets off the main strips tend to hold value well. Proximity to **Preston station** or **Bell station** commands a premium.

## Who's Buying in Preston?

- **First-home buyers** looking at apartments and smaller properties
- **Upgraders** moving from apartments to townhouses or houses
- **Investors** seeking rental yield in a high-demand inner-north suburb
- **Downsizers** from outer suburbs, attracted by walkability and the market
- **Renovators** targeting older homes with potential

## Auction Day Reality

Melbourne runs on auctions, and Preston is no different. Saturday mornings on the residential streets off **High Street** and **Gilbert Road** draw crowds.

**Tips:**
1. Set a hard limit before auction day. Do not go past it.
2. Attend several auctions as an observer before bidding for real.
3. Have finance pre-approved — unconditional if possible.
4. Get a building inspection done before auction.
5. Understand the reserve price system.

## Common Mistakes

- **Falling in love before due diligence** — Preston's streetscape charm can override rational analysis
- **Underestimating renovation costs** — Older properties along **Murray Road** and **Bell Street** hide expensive problems
- **Ignoring body corporate fees** — For apartments, these add significantly to ongoing costs
- **Buying on a main road** — Cheaper for a reason — noise and re-sale difficulty
- **Not checking council development plans** — The City of Darebin has ongoing development projects

## FAQ

**What's the median house price in Preston?**
Around $1.05-1.3 million as of early 2026. Units are significantly cheaper.

**Is Preston a good investment?**
Long-term track record is solid. The suburb's fundamentals — location, [transport](/preston/transport-guide/) (three stations), market, food scene — underpin consistent demand. Standard Melbourne market cycles apply.

**Where's the best area to buy in Preston?**
Streets within walking distance of Preston station and High Street tend to be the most in-demand. The area near Preston Market (Murray Road, Cramer Street) is also popular.

## The Verdict

Buying in Preston makes sense if the suburb's lifestyle aligns with your life and budget. The inner-north location, three train stations, and Preston Market are genuine selling points that comparable suburbs lack. Don't buy just because it's popular — buy because you want to live here (or because the rental fundamentals support an investment). Do your research, know your numbers, and don't let auction-day pressure override your financial limits.
