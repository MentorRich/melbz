## The Drinking Scene in Reservoir

Reservoir's bar scene has matured over the past few years, and it now sits somewhere between the old-school pub culture that defined the suburb for decades and the newer wave of small bars that have started appearing along Broadway and the surrounding streets. Sitting 14km north of the CBD on the Mernda (South Morang) train line, Reservoir draws a crowd that is diverse, unpretentious, and loyal to its locals.

You are not going to find a rooftop cocktail bar with a $28 Negroni here. What you will find are places that know their regulars by name, pour a proper beer, and do not charge you for the privilege of sitting down.

## Where to Drink in Reservoir

**The Broadway Strip** — Broadway is Reservoir's main commercial artery, and it is where most of the after-dark action happens. A handful of pubs and bars line the strip between Reservoir station and Edwardes Street. The vibe on a Friday night is relaxed and local — people coming from work, meeting friends, settling in.

**The Local Pub** — Reservoir has several pubs that have been operating for years. Some have been renovated with better tap lists and updated kitchens, while others remain genuinely old-school. If you want a $9 pot and a parma, they still exist here.

**Wine Bars** — A few smaller wine-focused spots have appeared in recent years, usually with eight to ten wines by the glass and a snack menu. These tend to be quieter, suited to couples or small groups, and open Wednesday through Sunday.

**Beer Gardens** — Melbourne summers and beer gardens go together, and several Reservoir pubs have outdoor areas that fill up from October through March. The beer garden crowd here skews friendly and neighbourhood-oriented.

## When to Go

**After work (5-7pm)** — The sweet spot for most Reservoir bars. Happy hour pricing at some pubs, relaxed atmosphere, locals winding down from the day.

**Friday night** — Broadway fills up. Arrive by 6pm if you want a table. The atmosphere picks up by 8pm and stays steady until close.

**Saturday** — Peak social time. Bars are full, music is on, and expect to wait for a seat at popular spots. The vibe skews younger on Saturdays.

**Sunday session** — Underrated. Some of Reservoir's pubs do proper Sunday sessions with live music, cheaper jugs, and a completely relaxed dress code.

## What Makes Reservoir Different

Reservoir is not trying to compete with Fitzroy or Collingwood. The drinking scene here works because it serves the people who actually live here. With median rents around $320 per week for a one-bedroom, many residents have enough disposable income to be regulars without stretching. That creates a genuine neighbourhood feel at the bar — you recognise faces, bartenders remember your order, and nobody is there to be seen.

The proximity to Preston and Thornbury means you can easily hop on the 11 tram or catch a train one stop south for more options if you want variety.

## Tips

1. **Walk Broadway first** — get a feel for the strip before committing to one venue
2. **Try the local brewery taps** — craft beer has reached Reservoir and the quality is solid
3. **Weeknight drinking is underrated** — better service, more space, same drinks
4. **Ask the bartender** — they know what is good and what is not worth ordering
5. **Check for live music** — several pubs book local acts on Thursday through Saturday

## FAQ

**What are the best bars near Reservoir station?**
Broadway, which runs directly from Reservoir station, has the highest concentration of pubs and bars. You can walk from the station to multiple venues within five minutes without crossing a major road.

**Is Reservoir good for nightlife?**
Reservoir is good for relaxed, local nightlife — pubs, wine bars, and the occasional live music night. For club-style nightlife, you are better off heading to the CBD or Fitzroy, which is about 25 minutes by train.

**How much does a night out in Reservoir cost?**
A typical night out — a few beers and a pub meal — will run you $50 to $80 per person. Cocktails at the smaller bars cost $16 to $22 each. Significantly cheaper than inner-city equivalents.

**Can I get home late from Reservoir?**
The last train from the city to Reservoir runs around midnight on weekdays, later on weekends with Night Network services running hourly on Friday and Saturday nights.
