## Eating Out in Reservoir

Reservoir sits 14km north of the CBD on the Mernda train line, and its restaurant scene reflects the suburb's multicultural character. The Broadway shopping strip is the main dining hub, but good food is scattered through the surrounding streets — along Edwardes Street, Spring Street, and the pockets near Edwardes Lake. The suburb's Italian, Greek, Middle Eastern, and South Asian communities have shaped a food culture where quality and affordability go hand in hand.

You do not need to leave Reservoir for a good meal. From a $14 weeknight laksa to a $45 per-head Italian dinner, the range here is genuine and growing.

## What Kind of Food Can You Find?

**Italian** — Reservoir has a strong Italian heritage, and it shows. Proper pasta joints where the nonna-style recipes have not been touched, woodfired pizza that tastes like actual pizza, and family-run trattorias that have been operating for decades. Some of Melbourne's most authentic Italian outside of Carlton and Lygon Street is here.

**Vietnamese and Asian** — The Vietnamese community has a real presence in Reservoir. Pho, banh mi, and rice paper rolls at prices that remind you what affordable eating in Melbourne used to look like. Thai, Chinese, and Indian options are also solid and scattered across the suburb.

**Middle Eastern and Mediterranean** — Kebab shops, charcoal chicken, and Lebanese bakeries line parts of Broadway and the surrounding streets. Great for quick lunches and late-night feeds.

**Modern Australian** — A few newer restaurants along Broadway have gone the seasonal-menu route — proper chefs sourcing locally and doing interesting things with the food. These tend to be in the $30 to $50 per head range.

**Pub Food Done Right** — Several Reservoir pubs have upgraded their kitchens. You can still get a classic parma, but the tap list and the sides have improved considerably. Decent wine lists at some of the renovated locals, too.

## Weeknight vs Weekend Dining

**Weeknights** are the sweet spot in Reservoir. Walk-in friendly, quieter service, and kitchens that are not slammed. Tuesday to Thursday is when locals eat out casually — and when some restaurants run specials worth knowing about.

**Weekends** bring bigger crowds, especially Friday and Saturday nights on Broadway. Book ahead for the popular Italian spots, or arrive before 6pm. Sunday dinner is an underrated option — same food quality, fewer people, more relaxed pace.

## Price Range

Reservoir's affordability is a genuine advantage. A solid dinner for two with drinks will typically run $60 to $100 at most restaurants — considerably less than equivalent meals in Northcote or Fitzroy. Quick eats and takeaway can be under $15 per person easily.

The mid-range is where Reservoir excels. Restaurants that care about quality and do not gouge on price. That is the sweet spot here.

## Tips for Eating in Reservoir

1. **Walk Broadway end to end** — the best places are not always the most visible
2. **Weeknight date nights** — better experience, easier bookings, sometimes cheaper
3. **Try the specials** — chefs put energy into their specials, and it shows
4. **Do not judge by decor** — some of the best food comes from the plainest-looking shops
5. **Support the independents** — chain restaurants exist, but the soul of Reservoir's food scene is the family-run businesses

## FAQ

**What type of food is Reservoir known for?**
Reservoir is known for its Italian and Vietnamese food in particular, reflecting the suburb's multicultural makeup. You will also find strong Middle Eastern, Greek, and modern Australian options along the Broadway strip.

**Is Reservoir cheap to eat out?**
Yes, Reservoir is one of Melbourne's more affordable dining suburbs. A solid meal at a sit-down restaurant typically costs $18 to $30 per person for mains. Vietnamese and Middle Eastern takeaway options are often under $15.

**Where is the best place to eat in Reservoir?**
The Broadway shopping strip between Reservoir station and Edwardes Street has the highest concentration of restaurants. Edwardes Street itself and the streets near Edwardes Lake also have options worth exploring.

**Is Reservoir good for family dining?**
Absolutely. Many of Reservoir's restaurants are family-run and naturally family-friendly. Italian trattorias, Vietnamese restaurants, and the local pubs all cater well to families with kids' menus or flexible portion sizes.
