## Being a Dog Owner in Reservoir

Melbourne is a dog city, and Reservoir — sitting 14km north of the CBD along the Mernda train line — is one of the more dog-friendly suburbs in the northern corridor. Generous park space, wide residential streets, and a community that genuinely likes dogs make it a solid choice for pet owners. Median rent for a one-bedroom sits around $320 per week, so you can afford the vet bills and the premium dog food.

## Off-Leash Parks and Spaces

Reservoir has several dedicated off-leash areas, and they get proper use.

**Edwardes Lake Park** is the big one — a large green space around Edwardes Lake with designated off-leash zones. The area around the lake itself is popular for morning and afternoon dog socialisation. It is flat, mostly grassed, and the lake circuit gives dogs and owners a natural walking loop.

**Reservoir Leisure Reserve** on Cheddar Road has off-leash access during certain hours. Check the Darebin Council signage for current time restrictions — typically off-leash before 9am and after 5pm.

**Smaller pocket parks** along Broadway and the residential streets between Spring Street and Edwardes Street also have off-leash zones, though these are less spacious.

**What to expect:**
- Morning (before 8am) and late afternoon are prime dog socialisation hours
- You will start recognising the regular dogs before you learn their owners' names
- Edwardes Lake Park has the best fencing; smaller parks vary
- There is an informal dog community that meets daily at the lake — just show up

## Dog-Friendly [Cafes](/reservoir/best-cafes/)

Multiple cafes along Broadway welcome dogs in their outdoor areas. Water bowls appear without asking, and the occasional dog treat materialises from behind the counter. This is Melbourne — cafe dogs are part of the culture.

Cafes along the Broadway strip between Reservoir station and Edwardes Street are your best bet. Most have outdoor seating with enough space for a dog to sit comfortably. Weekend brunch is the peak dog-and-coffee time.

**Tips:**
- Outdoor seating areas are your safest option — not all cafes allow dogs inside
- Go during quieter weekday periods if your dog is new to cafe culture
- Tie-up points exist at most Broadway cafes

## Walking Routes

The walking options around Reservoir suit dogs of all energy levels:

- **Edwardes Lake circuit** — A 2.5km loop around the lake. Flat, paved, and a natural meeting point for other dog walkers. Takes about 30 to 40 minutes at a leisurely pace.
- **Broadway to Preston walk** — Head south along High Street from Broadway into Preston for a 3km each-way walk that passes shops, parks, and plenty of other dogs. Connect through to Thornbury if you want a longer route.
- **Residential streets loop** — 20 to 30 minutes through the quieter streets between Cheddar Road and Broadway. Good for daily maintenance walks with minimal traffic.
- **Merri Creek trail connection** — A longer weekend option. Drive or bus to the Merri Creek trail access point and walk south through Northcote and Clifton Hill. A proper adventure walk for high-energy dogs.

## Vet Access

Multiple veterinary clinics operate within Reservoir and the neighbouring suburbs of Preston and Thornbury. Emergency after-hours vet services are accessible within a 15-minute drive. Having a trusted vet nearby matters, and the northern suburbs are well-served on this front.

## What Dog Owners Love About Reservoir

- Edwardes Lake Park — genuinely good off-leash space
- Cafe culture that welcomes dogs along Broadway
- Wide, flat streets for easy walking

- Friendly neighbourhood feel — people stop to say hello to your dog

## What Could Be Better

- Off-leash areas at the smaller parks could be larger
- Peak times (Saturday mornings) get crowded at Edwardes Lake
- Some rental properties in Reservoir have strict pet policies — check before signing
- Not every Broadway cafe is equally welcoming — learn which ones are genuine about it

## FAQ

**Is Reservoir a good suburb for dogs?**
Yes. Edwardes Lake Park alone makes it worthwhile, and the suburb's wide residential streets and generally friendly community add to the experience. It scores well for off-leash space relative to its distance from the CBD.

**Are there off-leash dog parks in Reservoir?**
Edwardes Lake Park and Reservoir Leisure Reserve both have designated off-leash zones. Time restrictions apply — typically off-leash before 9am and after 5pm at most parks. Check Darebin Council signage for current rules.

**Which cafes in Reservoir allow dogs?**
Most cafes along the Broadway strip allow dogs in their outdoor seating areas. Water bowls are standard. Weekend brunch is the most popular dog-cafe time.

**Where can I walk my dog near Reservoir?**
The Edwardes Lake circuit is the most popular route. For longer walks, head south along High Street toward Preston and Thornbury, or drive to the Merri Creek trail for a proper weekend hike.
