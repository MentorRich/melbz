Just off Bridge Road, running parallel to the Yarra River, Victoria Street in Richmond has earned its reputation as Melbourne's premier Vietnamese dining strip. For more than four decades, this stretch of road has served as the heart of Melbourne's Vietnamese community, a place where the aroma of pho broth mingles with the scent of fresh herbs, where families have passed down recipes through generations, and where first-generation immigrants built businesses that now feed third-generation Australian-born Vietnamese families.

What makes Victoria Street special isn't just the quantity of Vietnamese restaurants—it's the quality and the authenticity. These aren't restaurants adapting Vietnamese food for Australian palates. They're the real deal, serving dishes that taste exactly what you'd find in Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi, or the countless regional cuisines of Vietnam. Whether you're hunting for the perfect bowl of pho, craving a crispy pork banh mi, or exploring less familiar Vietnamese dishes, Victoria Street delivers.

This guide covers everything you need to know about eating your way through Melbourne's Little Vietnam—from the essential institutions to the newer arrivals keeping the strip fresh.

## Understanding Victoria Street's Vietnamese Food Scene

Before diving into specific restaurant recommendations, it's worth understanding what you're looking for. Vietnamese cuisine is remarkably diverse, and Victoria Street reflects that variety. The strip isn't homogeneous—there are regional differences, stylistic variations, and different approaches to the classics.

### The Two Main Styles

Most Vietnamese restaurants in Melbourne fall into one of two broad categories, and Victoria Street has examples of both:

**Northern Vietnamese (Hanoi-style):** Characterised by lighter broths, wider rice noodles (bánh phở), and a greater emphasis on herbs. The pho tends to be more subtle, with the beef flavour taking centre stage without the sweetness found in southern preparations. Lemongrass and fish sauce play supporting roles.

**Southern Vietnamese (Saigon-style):** The more common style in Melbourne, featuring sweeter, richer broths, narrower noodles, and a generous use of fresh herbs, bean sprouts, and chillies. The pho is often more robust, and you'll find more rice paper rolls and fresh spring rolls on the menu.

Victoria Street leans southern, reflecting the origins of the Vietnamese-Australian community (most refugees who arrived in the 1970s and 1980s came from the south). But you'll find northern specialties at several restaurants, and some places blend both traditions.

### Key Vietnamese Dishes to Know

- **Pho:** Rice noodle soup with beef (or chicken), served with a side plate of fresh herbs, bean sprouts, Thai basil, lime, and chillies. The foundation of Vietnamese cuisine.
- **Banh Mi:** Vietnamese baguette sandwich with various fillings—most commonly pork (including paté and grilled pork), chicken, or tofu. The bread is the star: crisp outside, soft inside.
- **Bún:** Rice vermicelli bowls, typically served with grilled meat, fresh herbs, peanuts, and a fish-sauce-based dressing (nước mắm).
- **Gỏi Cuốn:** Fresh spring rolls—rice paper wrappers filled with pork, shrimp, rice vermicelli, and herbs, served with peanut sauce.
- **Nem Nướng:** Grilled pork sausage, often served with rice paper wrappers for DIY wraps with herbs and dipping sauce.
- **Cà Phê Sữa Đá:** Vietnamese iced coffee—strong sweet coffee condensed milk, served over ice.

## The Essential Victoria Street Restaurants

### Best Pho

**Thien Hien (燕樓)**

*191 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Thien Hien has been serving Victoria Street for over 30 years, and for many Melbourne pho obsessives, it's the benchmark. The broth is the draw here—clear, beefy, with a subtle sweetness that comes from hours of simmering bones. It's not the richest pho in Melbourne, but it's arguably the most refined.

The menu covers the standard pho options: rare steak (tái), brisket (gầu), tendon (gân), and combinations. Order your noodles separately if you want them firm (bánh phở chín)—the default preparation can be softer than pho purists prefer. The accompaniments arrive on a separate plate: bean sprouts, Thai basil, lime, and chillies. Add them progressively, tasting as you go.

Thien Hien is cash only, a relic of its old-school roots. Arrive early to avoid queues, especially on weekends. The lunch rush can see waits of 20-30 minutes, but the turnover is fast.

**Price:** Bowl of pho from $14  
**Best for:** Traditional pho lovers who appreciate subtle, refined broths

**Shop 1-2/191 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 7am-8pm daily*

---

**Pho Dzung (Phở Dũng)**

*145 Victoria Street, Richmond*

If Thien Hien represents the traditional approach, Pho Dzung represents a more contemporary interpretation. The broth here is richer and more aromatic, with a slight sweetness that appeals to those who find Thien Hien too subtle. The beef is well-prepared, and the service is efficient—important when you're in a hurry.

Pho Dzung is popular with office workers in the area, which means it handles volume well. The restaurant has more seating than some of the older establishments, making it a better option for larger groups. The menu includes some non-pho items (vermicelli bowls, rice plates) if you're not a noodle soup person.

**Price:** Bowl of pho from $15  
**Best for:** Quick lunches, groups, those who prefer richer broths

**145 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 7am-9pm daily*

---

**Ngan Lane (Ngân Lãnh)**

*133 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Ngan Lane is a smaller, more intimate operation than the big pho temples. The broth here walks a line between the traditional clarity of Thien Hien and the richness of Pho Dzung—balanced and satisfying without being overpowering. It's a favourite among locals who prefer to avoid the crowds at the more famous establishments.

The restaurant is tucked away from the main Victoria Street strip, slightly towards the Bridge Road end. Look for the modest signage and the constant stream of regulars who clearly know a good thing.

**Price:** Bowl of pho from $14  
**Best for:** Locals in the know, smaller crowds

**133 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 8am-7pm daily*

### Best Banh Mi

**Banh Mi Boys**

*194 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Banh Mi Boys has built a cult following for its exceptional banh mi, and the reputation is well-earned. The bread is sourced from a Vietnamese bakery and baked fresh daily—crisp, golden, with that distinctive airy interior that makes a great banh mi. The fillings are generous, properly seasoned, and the balance of meats, vegetables, and condiments is spot on.

The classic pork banh mi (bánh mì thịt nướng) is the way to go—grilled pork shoulder with paté, cold cuts, pickled vegetables, cilantro, and jalapeños. If you're feeling adventurous, try the beef or the special combination. The shop also offers rice bowls and other Vietnamese dishes, but the banh mi is the draw.

Expect queues, especially on weekends. The line moves quickly, and there's often a second line for ordering versus picking up. Don't be intimidated—it's worth the wait.

**Price:** Banh mi from $8.50  
**Best for:** The definitive Richmond banh mi experience

**194 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 8am-5pm daily*

---

**Banh Mi Huynh**

*108 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Banh Mi Huynh is the traditional alternative to Banh Mi Boys—smaller, older, and with a more old-school vibe. The bread is still excellent (you can't survive on Victoria Street with bad bread), and the fillings are generous. The difference is in the atmosphere: Banh Mi Huynh feels like a neighbourhood institution, while Banh Mi Boys has a slightly more commercial energy.

The cold cuts banh mi here is particularly good, with quality ham and a house-made paté that adds depth. It's a more affordable option too, with prices a dollar or two lower than Banh Mi Boys.

**Price:** Banh mi from $7.50  
**Best for:** Traditionalists, budget-conscious banh mi lovers

**108 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 7am-6pm daily*

### Best for Rice Noodles (Bún) and Other Dishes

**Vietnamese Sally (Sally Lê)**

*145 Victoria Street (upstairs), Richmond*

Vietnamese Sally is named after its founder Sally Lê, who built a reputation for authentic home-style Vietnamese cooking. The restaurant is upstairs from a more casual ground-floor operation, and the menu reflects that improved approach—still affordable, but with more care and presentation than your standard Victoria Street feed.

The bún thịt nướng (grilled pork with rice vermicelli) is excellent, with properly charred meat and a balanced dressing. But Sally also offers dishes you won't find everywhere: bún bò Huế (spicy beef noodle soup from Hue), bánh xèo (Vietnamese savoury pancake), and various clay pot preparations. If you're looking to explore beyond pho and banh mi, this is your spot.

**Price:** Main courses from $16  
**Best for:** Exploring beyond the classics, larger groups

**145 Victoria Street (upstairs), Richmond**  
*Open 11am-3pm, 5pm-9pm daily*

---

**Giang Bảo**

*118 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Giang Bảo is a solid all-rounder, covering pho, bún, and various rice plates in a casual, no-fuss environment. It's not the most famous on the strip, which means shorter queues, and the quality is consistent. The bún chả (vermicelli with grilled pork and spring rolls) is a particular highlight.

The restaurant has a more substantial dining area than some of the smaller banh mi shops, making it a good option if you're with family or friends who want to sit down together. The prices are reasonable, and the portions are generous.

**Price:** Main courses from $14  
**Best for:** Family meals, consistent quality, shorter waits

**118 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 9am-9pm daily*

### under the radars and Specialty Spots

**Cà Phê VN**

*177 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Cà Phê VN is first and foremost a Vietnamese coffee specialist, but the small food menu makes it worth a visit for a light meal or snack. The egg coffee (cà phê trứng) is a must-try—a rich, creamy preparation that's like drinking a liquid tiramisu. The coffee is properly strong, sweetened with condensed milk, and served either hot or over ice.

If you're hungry, the bánh mì cá (fish banh mi) is a departure from the usual pork offerings, with flaky white fish in a creamy sauce. It's not traditional, but it's delicious. The space is small and often busy, but the coffee is worth the wait.

**Price:** Coffee from $5, banh mi from $8  
**Best for:** Coffee enthusiasts, light bites

**177 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 7am-5pm daily*

---

**Huong Giang**

*165 Victoria Street, Richmond*

Huong Giang has been a Victoria Street institution for over 20 years, serving traditional Vietnamese dishes in a modest setting. The menu covers all the classics, but the strength here is the execution—the dishes are consistently well-prepared, with proper flavour balances and fresh ingredients.

The nem nướng (grilled pork sausage) is a highlight, served with rice paper wrappers, herbs, and a excellent nước chấm (dipping sauce) for DIY wraps. It's a shareable dish that works well as a starter before moving on to noodles or rice.

**Price:** Main courses from $15  
**Best for:** Traditional Vietnamese in a relaxed setting

**165 Victoria Street, Richmond**  
*Open 10am-9pm daily*

## Beyond Vietnamese: Victoria Street's Other Options

While Vietnamese food dominates Victoria Street, the strip has diversified over the years. If you're dining with a group that includes non-Vietnamese eaters, or if you're simply curious about what else the strip offers, here are some options:

**Asian supermarkets:** Victoria Street is home to several Vietnamese and Asian supermarkets, perfect for picking up ingredients, snacks, or imported goods you won't find in mainstream supermarkets. These are also great for browsing—look for unusual fruits, instant noodle varieties, and Vietnamese confectionery.

**Bubble tea shops:** Several bubble tea spots line the street, offering Taiwanese-style milk teas with various toppings. A refreshing break from the rich broths and heavy breads.

**Bakeries:** Vietnamese bakeries sell traditional pastries, including the sweet bean and mung bean treats that complement Vietnamese coffee perfectly.

## Practical Tips for Victoria Street

### When to Go

Victoria Street is busiest on weekends, especially Saturday and Sunday lunchtimes. If you want to avoid queues, visit on a weekday morning (around 10am) or early afternoon (after 2pm). The restaurants are open early (many from 7am or 8am) and stay open late, giving you flexibility.

### Payment

Many Victoria Street restaurants are still cash-only, especially the smaller establishments. Bring cash to be safe, though most of the newer or larger places accept card payments. Banh Mi Boys and the bigger pho shops have eftpos facilities.

### Language

Most staff speak Vietnamese as their first language, and menus are often Vietnamese-only or bilingual. If you're unfamiliar with the dishes, don't be afraid to point, ask for recommendations, or ask for the English menu. The staff at most restaurants are used to visitors and can guide you through the options.

### Dietary Requirements

Vegetarian options are limited at most traditional Vietnamese restaurants, though some places offer tofu versions of popular dishes. Vegan diners should ask about fish sauce (nước mắm), which appears in many dressings and sauces. If you have severe allergies, communicate clearly—staff are helpful but may not be familiar with Western dietary terminology.

### Parking

Victoria Street has limited parking, especially on weekends. The easiest option is to park in the retail parking off Bridge Road or along the side streets, then walk to Victoria Street. Public transport is a better option: the 48 tram runs along Victoria Street, stopping near most of the restaurants mentioned.

## The History of Victoria Street's Vietnamese Community

Victoria Street's Vietnamese identity dates back to the late 1970s and early 1980s, when Vietnamese refugees began arriving in Melbourne following the fall of Saigon. Many settled in Richmond and Footscray, bringing their food, culture, and entrepreneurial spirit.

The first Vietnamese businesses were modest operations—small cafés serving pho to the local Vietnamese community. As the community grew and thrived, so did the strip, eventually expanding to include dozens of Vietnamese restaurants, grocers, bakeries, and other businesses. Today, Victoria Street is not just a dining destination but a cultural hub, a place where Vietnamese-Australians maintain connections to their heritage while building new lives in Australia.

Understanding this history adds depth to your meal. When you eat at Thien Hien or Banh Mi Boys, you're not just eating good food—you're participating in a story of resilience, community, and cultural preservation that spans five decades.

## Final Thoughts

Victoria Street Richmond remains Melbourne's premier destination for authentic Vietnamese food. Whether you're hunting for the perfect pho, craving a banh mi, or simply want to explore one of the city's great food strips, Victoria Street delivers. The competition between restaurants means quality stays high, the prices remain reasonable, and the variety ensures there's you'll find what you want.

Start with the essentials—Thien Hien for pho, Banh Mi Boys for sandwiches—but don't stop there. The worth seeking outs and newer arrivals keep the strip evolving, and there's always something new to discover. Bring cash, come hungry, and be prepared to add Victoria Street to your regular rotation.
