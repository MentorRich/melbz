[St Kilda](/st-kilda/) East is honest about its dining scene: there are maybe five or six genuinely good restaurants in the suburb itself, and the rest of your eating life happens across the border in Balaclava, St Kilda, or Windsor. That's not a weakness — it's a feature. The venues that operate here serve the neighbourhood, and they've earned loyalty.

Here's where to eat right now.

## The Neighbourhood Staples

### Scheherazade — 99 Acland Street

Eastern European comfort food since 1958. The borscht ($14) is the benchmark — rich, proper, served with sour cream and black bread. The blintzes ($16) are filled with sweetened cheese and pan-fried. The schnitzel ($26) is enormous and comes with potato salad that hasn't changed in decades. The dining room feels like your grandmother's living room if your grandmother was from Odessa. Dinner for two: $60–$80.

### Glick's Bagels — 330 Carlisle Street

On the Balaclava border but every St Kilda East local claims it. The best bagels in Melbourne's south-east, baked fresh daily. A dozen plain bagels ($14), or filled — smoked salmon and cream cheese ($12), or egg and cheese ($9). No seating, no pretension. Queue on Saturday mornings.

### Balaclava Hotel — 28 Carlisle Street

The pub kitchen has been quietly upgraded without losing the pub feel. The parma ($24) is properly crumbed and the chips are hand-cut. The steak ($34) is good honest cooking. The front bar still feels like a front bar — old carpet energy, pot of Melbourne Bitter ($8), footy on the telly.

**When to go:** Tuesday to Thursday for walk-in ease. Book Friday and Saturday.
**Price:** Dinner for two with drinks: $80–$110.

## The Quick and Good

### Balaclava Fish and Chips — Carlisle Street

The fish is flake, battered fresh, and comes out hot. Minimum chips ($5.50), fish ($11). Eat in the park. This is the kind of no-frills local that every suburb needs and most suburbs have lost.

### Jerusalem Grill — Hotham Street

Shawarma, falafel, and grilled meats that hit right. Lamb shawarma plate ($18) with rice, salad, and hummus. Falafel wrap ($12) that's better than anything on Chapel Street. Fast, cheap, and run by people who've been cooking this food their whole lives.

## Weeknight vs Weekend

**Weeknights** are the sweet spot in St Kilda East. Walk-in friendly, quieter, and you get the kitchen's full attention. Tuesday to Thursday is when locals eat out casually.

**Weekends** bring the Carlisle Street crowds. Book ahead for the Balaclava Hotel or anywhere decent on the Balaclava strip. Sunday dinner is underrated — quieter than Saturday with the same quality.

## FAQ

**Is St Kilda East a food destination?**
No, and that's fine. The suburb has solid neighbourhood dining and world-class delis, but you're shopping at Balaclava's table for variety.

**What about kosher dining?**
St Kilda East and the surrounding area have Melbourne's best selection of kosher restaurants, bakeries, and delis. Glick's, Izzy's Bakery, and the butchers on Carlisle Street serve the suburb's established Jewish community.

**Best cheap eat?**
Jerusalem Grill on Hotham Street. Falafel wrap, $12, and you won't need dinner.
