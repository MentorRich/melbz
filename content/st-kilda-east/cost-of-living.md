# Cost of Living in St Kilda East 2026: The Real Numbers

St Kilda East sits in that sweet spot between the chaos of [St Kilda](/suburbs/st-kilda) and the calm of [Caulfield](/suburbs/caulfield). It's quieter than its famous neighbour, cheaper than people expect, and has a density of Eastern European bakeries that would make Budapest jealous. But does it actually stack up as a place to live in 2026? We did the maths so you don't have to.

If you're considering a move to St Kilda East — or already live here and want to know whether you're getting ripped off — this is the real breakdown. No aspirational budgets. No "you can live here on $40K" fantasy figures. Just honest numbers for a suburb that rewards people who do their homework.

**Updated 16 March 2026 | Marcus Cole reporting**

## The Quick Answer

A single person renting a one-bedroom apartment in St Kilda East in 2026 needs roughly **$65,000–$75,000 per year** after tax to live comfortably — meaning rent doesn't eat more than 30% of income, you eat well, get around the city without stress, and still have money for entertainment and savings. That translates to a gross salary of about **$82,000–$95,000**.

A couple sharing a one-bedroom? You can get away with around **$55,000–$65,000 each** gross if you're disciplined. A family of four in a two-bedroom? You'll want to be pulling in **$130,000+ combined** to avoid the financial squeeze.

These aren't arbitrary figures. They're what the rent, the groceries, the tram, the gym, and the occasional dinner out actually cost. Let's break it down.

## Rent: The Big One

St Kilda East has always been the more affordable cousin of [St Kilda proper](/suburbs/st-kilda). In 2026, that gap has narrowed but it still exists — roughly $40–$80/week less than equivalent places just across the road.

| Property Type | Weekly Rent (2026 avg) | Monthly | Annual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Studio | $320–$370 | $1,390–$1,605 | $16,680–$19,260 |
| 1-bedroom apartment | $400–$480 | $1,735–$2,080 | $20,820–$24,960 |
| 2-bedroom apartment | $550–$680 | $2,385–$2,950 | $28,620–$35,400 |
| 3-bedroom house | $750–$950 | $3,255–$4,120 | $39,060–$49,440 |

The sweet spot is the 1-bed apartment between Hotham Street and Carlisle Street — you'll find a mix of 1970s blocks and refurbished units that offer decent space without the bayside premium. Avoid anything on the immediate Fitzroy Street strip unless you enjoy Friday night noise and the occasional street confrontation echoing up to your balcony.

**Pro tip from Marcus:** If you're looking at a place on Alma Road or Chapel Street (the St Kilda East end, not Prahran), check the strata report. Many of these 1960s blocks have deferred maintenance that will eventually hit owners — and renters via rent hikes.

## Transport: Getting Around

St Kilda East is well served by public transport — the 96 tram along Carlisle Street is a direct line to the CBD, and the 75 tram takes you east toward [Balaclava](/suburbs/balaclava) and beyond.

### Monthly Transport Costs (2026)

| Mode | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Myki Zone 1+2 (full fare) | $181.60/month | Daily cap of $10.60 |
| Myki Zone 1+2 (concession) | $90.80/month | Student/healthcare card |
| E-scooter (Lime/Neuron) | $0.49/minute + unlock fee | Fine for short hops, expensive for commutes |
| Car parking (local garage) | $250–$350/month | Street parking is permit zone — apply via City of Port Phillip |
| Fuel (small car, 2 fills/month) | $180–$240/month | Based on $1.85–$1.95/L for ULP |
| Car insurance (comp, avg driver) | $120–$180/month | Varies hugely by age and history |

If you're working in the CBD, the 96 tram is genuinely one of the best public transport commutes in Melbourne. From the corner of Carlisle and Hotham, you're looking at about 30 minutes door to door, depending on whether you cop a signal failure at Flinders Street (you will, at least once a week).

**The honest take:** You don't need a car in St Kilda East unless you've got kids or you're regularly heading to places the trams don't reach. Factor in a $200–$350/month permit and the mental cost of parking on Alma Road at 6pm on a Friday — it's grim.

## Utilities: The Hidden Rents

Utilities in 2026 have settled somewhat from the energy crisis peaks, but St Kilda East's older building stock means you'll feel it in winter.

| Utility | Monthly Cost (1-bed apt) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity | $120–$180 | Higher in winter; many older apts lack good insulation |
| Gas (cooking/heating) | $40–$80 | If the place has gas heating, expect the upper end |
| Internet (NBN 50/20) | $65–$80 | Aussie Broadband, Superloop, or Tangerine recommended |
| Water (if metered) | $25–$40 | Often included in rent for apartments |
| Mobile phone plan | $30–$60 | Aldi Mobile or Boost for value |

**Total monthly utilities (single, 1-bed):** $280–$400

**Marcus's honest note:** If you're renting a ground-floor flat in one of those stucco 1930s buildings on Balaclava Road, budget toward the higher end. They're gorgeous but they leak heat like a sieve. A $50 electric blanket and a $30 draft snake from Bunnings will save you $60–$80/month in heating costs. I'm not joking.

## What We Skipped and Why

We deliberately left out a few categories that other cost-of-living guides include, and here's why:

**Childcare:** This varies so dramatically based on subsidy eligibility, provider type, and child's age that giving a single figure would be misleading. In 2026, out-of-pocket childcare costs in the City of Port Phillip range from $0 (full subsidy) to $350+/week (low subsidy, premium centre). Check the [Child Care Subsidy estimator on Services Australia](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/child-care-subsidy-calculator) for your specific situation.

**Private health insurance:** Again, too variable. A basic extras-only policy runs $25–$40/month. Hospital + extras runs $120–$280/month depending on age, cover level, and provider. Unless you're comparing policies, this number doesn't help you budget.

**Clothing and personal care:** We trust you to know what you spend on haircuts and shoes. A men's cut in St Kilda East runs $30–$55. Women's cut at a decent salon runs $80–$160. If you need us to tell you how often you buy new shoes, you have bigger problems than the cost of living.

**Alcohol:** We kept this out of the dining category because it deserves its own conversation. A pint at a local pub is $12–$16. A bottle of decent wine at the bottle shop is $18–$30. Whether you're a two-glasses-on-Thursday person or a Saturday-session enthusiast, your alcohol spend is deeply personal and we'll respect that by not moralising about it.

**Pet costs:** Dog owners in St Kilda East — the beach is right there, which is brilliant. Vet bills, food, registration with City of Port Phillip (~$85/year for desexed), and the inevitable destroyed shoe budget — we'll cover this in a dedicated pet cost-of-living piece if there's interest. Let us know.

[St Kilda proper](/suburbs/st-kilda) commands the premium for obvious reasons — beach, nightlife, Fitzroy Street — but the gap between it and St Kilda East has never been smaller than it is now.

## The Bottom Line

St Kilda East in 2026 is good value if you know what you're doing. It's not the cheapest pocket of Melbourne — you won't get Footscray or Reservoir prices — but you get bayside proximity, excellent transport, genuine multicultural food options, and a neighbourhood that feels lived-in rather than curated for Instagram.

The risk is the older building stock. Cheap rent often means a 1970s block with original wiring, thin walls, and heating that costs a fortune. Factor in utilities before signing a lease. A $40/week cheaper rent that costs you $80 more per month in heating isn't a bargain — it's a trap.

If you're moving here, visit the neighbourhood at 8am (commute reality), 6pm (parking reality), and 11pm on a Friday (noise reality) before signing anything. St Kilda East rewards the prepared and taxes the naive.
