St Kilda is Melbourne’s weekend escape without leaving the city. It’s where the tram lines end and the bay begins, where the scent of salt air mixes with espresso and beer, and where a century of carnival history lives on in the laughter echoing from Luna Park. This is a suburb that knows how to play—whether you’re here for a morning swim, an afternoon pint on a rooftop, or a late‑night dance in a heritage‑listed ballroom.

But St Kilda is more than just a postcard. Behind the palm‑lined Esplanade lies a community with a resilient, creative heart. The 1970s‑era housing commissions, the long‑standing Jewish and Russian communities, the artists’ studios tucked behind Fitzroy Street—all contribute to a texture that’s richer and more complex than the tourist brochures suggest. St Kilda has edges, and that’s what makes it interesting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is St Kilda safe at night?

Like any entertainment precinct, St Kilda has its moments. Fitzroy Street and the Esplanade can be lively (and occasionally rowdy) on weekend nights, but the area is well‑patrolled by police and security. Common sense applies: stick to well‑lit areas, travel in groups if possible, and be aware of your surroundings. During the day and early evening, the area is generally very safe.

### What’s the best way to get to St Kilda?

Tram is the classic route. Route 96 runs from Bourke Street in the CBD straight down St Kilda Road to the Esplanade, taking about 20 minutes. Route 12 also connects St Kilda to the city via Collins Street. Driving is possible, but parking is limited and expensive on weekends. Riding a bike along the dedicated path from Port Melbourne is a scenic alternative.

### Can you swim at St Kilda Beach?

Yes, but with caveats. The beach is sandy and sheltered, making it popular for paddling, sunbathing and beach volleyball. Water quality can vary after heavy rain (check EPA forecasts), and the bay is generally calm and shallow—more suited to a gentle dip than serious swimming. For proper laps, the historic St Kilda Sea Baths offer a heated saltwater pool.

Head to [Fitzroy](/fitzroy/) for Brunswick Street’s creative buzz and legendary dining, or to [Carlton](/carlton/) for Lygon Street’s espresso culture and Italian heritage. Each suburb has its own rhythm—part of what makes exploring Melbourne so rewarding.
