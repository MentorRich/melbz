## The Best Brunch in Windsor

Brunch in Windsor is not a trend anymore — it is an institution. This suburb has been quietly perfecting the weekend morning meal while the rest of Melbourne argues about whether avocado toast is over. Spoiler: it is not, and Windsor does it better than most.

The Windsor brunch scene runs along Chapel Street with a few tucked-away gems on the side streets and the Punt Road corridor. You will not find the queuing-for-90-minutes nonsense that plagues some inner-north suburbs. What you will find is consistently excellent food, coffee that does not treat the meal as an afterthought, and prices that will not destroy your weekend before it starts.

### 1. Franklin Windsor

**Address:** 177 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7:30am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm
**Budget:** $16-$24 per main

Franklin is the brunch spot that other brunch spots aspire to be. The kitchen here operates with the precision of a proper restaurant — seasonal ingredients, thoughtful plating, and dishes that feel considered rather than thrown together. The ricotta hotcakes ($21) with honeycomb butter get all the attention, and rightfully so, but the savoury menu is where Franklin quietly excels.

The breakfast ramen ($22) with a jammy soft egg, crispy shallots, chilli oil, and your choice of bacon or miso mushrooms is the kind of dish that redefines what brunch can be. The coffee is Five Senses — always good, never the star, always consistent.

**Insider tip:** Request a table in the courtyard if the weather is decent. It is the best outdoor brunch spot on Chapel Street.

### 2. Two Birds One Stone

**Address:** 120 Claremont Street, [South Yarra](/south-yarra/) (Windsor border)
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-4pm, Sat-Sun 8am-4pm
**Budget:** $17-$24 per main

Sitting on the Windsor-South Yarra border, Two Birds One Stone has been doing creative brunch since before "brunch culture" was something people had opinions about. The shakshuka ($20) — eggs poached in spiced tomato sauce with house-made za'atar flatbread — is the weekend order that makes you forget about eggs Benedict. The corn and zucchini fritters with whipped feta and chilli maple ($19) balance crispy and creamy in a way that is genuinely satisfying. Coffee runs $4.50 for a flat white.

**Accessibility:** Step-free entry, accessible bathroom on ground floor.

### 3. High Society

**Address:** 246 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7:30am-4pm, Sat-Sun 8:30am-4pm
**Budget:** $16-$21 per main

Half the appeal of High Society is the space itself — a cafe-florist hybrid that puts you in a room full of native flowers and greenery while you eat. The zucchini fritters with lemon yoghurt, herbs, and a poached egg ($18) are light but filling, and the mushrooms on sourdough with truffle oil and parmesan ($19) hit that savoury-satisfying brief perfectly. The Vietnamese iced coffee ($7.50) is a legitimate reason to visit on its own. On weekends, the outdoor terrace fills up fast, so get there before 9:30.

### 4. Mr Mister

**Address:** 228 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm
**Budget:** $16-$22 per main

Mr Mister is the reliable friend of Windsor brunch. The corn fritters ($19.50) with avocado salsa and two perfectly poached eggs are the benchmark for this dish in the area. The big breakfast ($22) covers everything — bacon, sausage, eggs, mushrooms, tomato, sourdough — with no filler. The espresso is chocolatey and robust, the portions are generous, and the space is large enough that you will not feel cramped even when it is full.

### 5. Cafe de la Ville

**Address:** 390 Punt Road, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-4pm, Sat-Sun 8am-4pm
**Budget:** $17-$24 per main

The outdoor terrace on Punt Road feels continental — sheltered from the wind, catching the morning sun, and just removed enough from Chapel Street's foot traffic to feel like a retreat. The eggs Florentine ($19) is done with proper hollandaise on a properly toasted English muffin. The French toast ($20) with berry compote and mascarpone is rich enough to cure whatever ailed you the night before.

### 6. Staple

**Address:** 173 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-2pm
**Budget:** $14-$19 per main

The budget-friendly hero of Windsor brunch. In a world where $24 eggs are increasingly normal, Staple keeps its mains between $14-$19. The breakfast roll ($14) with scrambled eggs, bacon, and house relish is the grab-and-go champion. The avocado toast ($15) with fetta, chilli flakes, and sourdough is as good as versions costing 50% more elsewhere.

### 7. Cheeky Monkey

**Address:** 298 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm
**Budget:** $15-$20 per main

Has been doing reliable, well-priced brunch on Chapel Street for long enough to have survived every food trend. The eggs Benedict ($17) is done properly and the big breakfast ($20) is hearty and uncomplicated. Coffee is $4.20 for a flat white, which is noticeably cheaper than most nearby [cafes](/windsor/best-cafes/).

## Windsor Brunch Economics

Brunch mains in Windsor average $18, which sits between [Prahran](/prahran/best-brunch/) ($19-$22) and [St Kilda](/st-kilda/best-brunch/) ($16-$20) in the inner-south bracket. You will struggle to spend more than $30 per person including a coffee.

The scene is most active on Saturday and Sunday mornings, with the peak crush between 9:30-11:30. If you want a table without queuing, aim for before 9am or after 12:30.

## FAQ

**What is the best value brunch in Windsor?** Staple, with mains from $14. The breakfast roll at $14 is the best grab-and-go option on Chapel Street.

**Which Windsor brunch spot is best for groups?** Mr Mister has the most space. Franklin works for groups of four but book ahead on weekends.

**Are Windsor brunch spots dog-friendly?** Most welcome dogs in outdoor areas. High Society and Staple are consistently accommodating.

## Verdict

Windsor's brunch scene is one of the best-value in Melbourne's inner south. Seven venues within a 10-minute walk, covering everything from $14 breakfast rolls to $24 French toast, all doing it well. The density means competition keeps quality high and prices honest.
