Windsor's cafe scene runs along Chapel Street with a few tucked-away spots on the side streets near High Street and Albert Street. The coffee is serious — locally roasted beans, proper extraction, and enough competition that bad coffee does not survive here. Here are the cafes that have earned repeat business.

### 1. Fourth Chapter

**Address:** 272 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-4pm, Sat-Sun 7:30am-4pm
**Budget:** Flat white $4.80, pour-over $6.50, brunch mains $17-$22

Fourth Chapter roasts in-house on a Probat, and the single-origin filter rotates weekly. The fit-out is minimal — polished concrete, big windows — but the coffee program is anything but. The baristas will tell you about whatever Ethiopian natural they are dialling in, with enthusiasm rather than arrogance. The smashed avo with dukkah on sourdough ($19) is worth ordering.

**Best for:** Serious coffee drinkers who care about origin and extraction.

### 2. Franklin Windsor

**Address:** 177 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7:30am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm
**Budget:** Flat white $4.80, mains $16-$24

Franklin uses Five Senses beans — always reliable, always consistent. The food is the real draw: ricotta hotcakes with honeycomb butter ($21) get the Instagram attention, but the breakfast ramen ($22) with jammy soft egg and crispy shallots is the order that redefines brunch. Request the courtyard table if the weather is decent.

**Best for:** Weekend brunch with proper food to match the coffee.

### 3. Mr Mister

**Address:** 228 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm
**Budget:** Flat white $4.50, mains $16-$22

The reliable friend of Windsor cafes. Espresso is chocolatey and robust, the space has high ceilings and big tables, and the corn fritters with avocado salsa and poached eggs ($19.50) are the benchmark dish. The iced latte in summer uses a cold brew base rather than hot coffee poured over ice. The takeaway toasties ($10-$12) are one of the best grab-and-go options on Chapel Street.

**Best for:** Solo mornings with a book, or groups of six who need space.

### 4. Staple

**Address:** 173 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-2pm
**Budget:** Flat white $4.50, mains $14-$19

The budget-friendly hero. Espresso is pulled on a La Marzocca with consistently silky milk. The breakfast roll ($14) with scrambled eggs, bacon, and house relish is the grab-and-go champion. No Instagram wall, no unnecessary fitout — just a neighbourhood cafe where the staff know regulars by name and order.

**Best for:** Daily morning rituals without breaking the budget.

### 5. High Society

**Address:** 246 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7:30am-4pm, Sat-Sun 8:30am-4pm
**Budget:** Flat white $4.50, Vietnamese iced coffee $7.50, mains $16-$21

Half cafe, half florist. You drink your coffee surrounded by native banksias and eucalyptus arrangements. The Vietnamese iced coffee ($7.50) with condensed milk is a legitimate reason to visit on its own. Zucchini fritters with lemon yoghurt ($18) and mushrooms on sourdough with truffle oil ($19) round out a Mediterranean-leaning menu. The outdoor terrace fills fast on weekends — arrive before 9:30.

**Best for:** Taking visiting friends somewhere that shows off your suburb.

### 6. Cheeky Monkey

**Address:** 298 Chapel Street, Windsor
**Hours:** Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm
**Budget:** Flat white $4.20, mains $15-$20

Has survived every food trend that has come and gone on Chapel Street. Eggs Benedict ($17) with house-made hollandaise and properly poached eggs. The big breakfast ($20) is uncomplicated and hearty. Coffee at $4.20 is noticeably cheaper than most nearby cafes. Wednesday mornings they do a two-coffee-and-toast special for $12.

**Best for:** The cafe you end up at every weekend because it is good and affordable.

## Windsor cafe economics

The average flat white in Windsor sits around $4.50 — roughly in line with Melbourne's inner south but cheaper than [South Yarra](/south-yarra/) five minutes up the road. Most cafes use locally roasted beans from Five Senses, Proud Mary, or micro-roasters. Chain coffee barely exists here.

The cafe strip is most active on weekday mornings (pre-work crowd) and Saturday-Sunday 9am-12pm (brunch rush). For a table without queuing, aim for before 8:30am or after 1pm on weekends.

## FAQ

**Which Windsor cafe has the best coffee?** Fourth Chapter for specialty single-origin. Mr Mister for a reliable everyday flat white. Cheeky Monkey for the best value.

**Are Windsor cafes dog-friendly?** Most welcome dogs in outdoor seating areas. Staple, High Society, and Mr Mister are consistently dog-friendly.

**Can I work from a Windsor cafe?** Mr Mister has the best space and does not mind lingerers. Fourth Chapter has good WiFi but gets busy on weekends.

## Verdict

Windsor's cafe scene is concentrated, competitive, and genuinely good. Six cafes within a 10-minute walk of each other, all doing something slightly different, all priced fairly. You will find your regular within a week of moving here.
