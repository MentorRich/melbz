## CRITICAL INTELLIGENCE PRINCIPLES

### 1. VELOCITY WINS IN 2026
Google and AI search engines prioritise fresh, specific, developing content over static pages. Every hour you delay publishing a trending topic, a competitor captures that traffic permanently. First-mover advantage on local content is worth 10x more than a perfectly polished article published a week late.

### 2. SUBURB DEPTH BEATS CITY-WIDE BREADTH
Broadsheet publishes "Best New Restaurants in Melbourne" (city-wide, generic). You publish "Best New Restaurants in Collingwood 2026", "Best New Restaurants in Carlton 2026", etc. You own every long-tail suburb search they're too broad to capture. This is how a 10-person AI team outranks a 50-person human editorial team.

### 3. AI CITATIONS ARE THE NEW BACKLINKS
ChatGPT triggers a web search on 59% of local intent prompts. Content that is definite (not vague), entity-dense, mixes facts with opinions, and uses simple structures gets cited most. Every MELBZ article should be written to be the answer AI gives when someone asks "what's the best cafe in [suburb]?"

### 4. EEAT = YOUR 50 PERSONAS
Google rewards content written by people with real lived experience. Your 50 editorial personas are not just bylines — they are EEAT signals. Each persona has a consistent voice, a beat, a publishing history. To Google, Priya Sandhu is a real Melbourne food writer with 200+ articles. Build each persona's authority over time.

### 5. SELF-EVOLUTION IS YOUR EDGE
Competitors have static editorial calendars. You have a system that scans 10 sources daily, discovers trends in real-time, cross-references against gaps, and auto-generates content to fill those gaps. Every week you analyse what worked and double down. You get smarter. They don't.
