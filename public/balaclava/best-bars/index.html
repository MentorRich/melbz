Best Bars in Balaclava 2026: Carlisle Street After Dark

Best Bars in Balaclava 2026: Carlisle Street After Dark

Updated 16 March 2026 | 6 places tested | Lina Nguyen reporting

Carlisle Street doesn’t shout about its nightlife. It doesn’t need to. While nearby Chapel Street in Windsor and the beachfront strip of St Kilda compete for attention with flashy signage and Instagram-friendly facades, Balaclava’s main drag does something almost subversive in 2026: it just quietly runs excellent bars.

I walked the full length of Carlisle Street on a Wednesday and a Saturday — two very different animals — and visited six venues that represent the suburb’s after-dark personality. The range is genuinely impressive. You’ve got a 140-year-old pub with a rooftop, a European-inspired craft beer temple, a retro-Italian cocktail den, and a live music institution that’s been going for over a decade. All within a stretch you could stroll end to end in eight minutes.

Here’s what’s worth your evening — and what to skip.

1. The Local Taphouse

Address: 184 Carlisle Street, St Kilda East
Vibe: European neighbourhood tavern meets craft beer nerd paradise
Drinks: 20 rotating taps plus 100+ bottled beers, cider, wine, spirits
Prices: Pints $12–$16, tasting paddles from $18, wines from $12/glass

The Local Taphouse is the anchor venue of Carlisle Street’s bar scene and arguably one of Melbourne’s best beer bars, full stop. The ground floor has that warm, woody tavern feel — dark timber, good lighting, a bar that invites you to lean against it for hours. The staff genuinely know their stuff. Ask for something hoppy and they won’t just point at a tap; they’ll walk you through three options with tasting notes.

Upstairs is where it gets special. The rooftop garden terrace has a retractable roof (essential for Melbourne’s four-seasons-in-an-hour climate), a fireplace, and enough greenery to feel like you’ve escaped the city. On a mild autumn evening it’s one of the best rooftop setups in the inner south.

They run “Ale Stars” — a beer appreciation night that’s been going for 15 years — plus rotating tap takeovers and seasonal food specials. The food menu skews elevated pub: think cheeseboards, beer-battered fish, and decent burgers.

Don’t miss: The tasting paddle. Pick five beers you’ve never tried. The staff will curate it for you.

2. Pause Bar

Address: 268 Carlisle Street, Balaclava
Vibe: Laid-back local with live music and a loyal crowd
Drinks: Tap beers (Carlton Draft, Cooper’s, rotating craft), cocktails, wine, spirits
Prices: Pints $10–$14, jug specials from $28, cocktails $18–$22

Pause Bar has been Balaclava’s reliable after-dark spot for over a decade, and it wears that longevity well. This isn’t a venue trying to reinvent itself every six months. It knows what it is: a friendly, no-pretence bar where you can hear live music without needing a second mortgage.

The space is compact but smartly laid out. A long bar dominates one side, with booths and high tables filling the rest. On weekends the live music kicks in — mostly local indie, funk, and soul acts — and the room fills with people who actually came to listen, not just nod along while filming TikToks.

Wednesday through Monday (open 4pm daily), they run a happy hour with jug specials that make it one of the cheapest spots to drink on the strip. The beer selection is solid if unspectacular — this is a Carlton Draft and mid-strength crowd, not a double-IPA showcase — but the cocktails are better than you’d expect for the price point.

Don’t miss: Friday after-work drinks. The crowd is a perfect Balaclava cross-section: tradies, young families who’ve put the kids down, and hospitality workers from nearby restaurants.

3. Balaclava Hotel

Address: 296 Carlisle Street, St Kilda East
Vibe: Renovated heritage pub with rooftop — the neighbourhood’s Swiss Army knife
Drinks: Tap beers, spirits, cocktails, wine list
Prices: Pints $10–$13, cocktails $16–$20, pot pots (literally pots of beer) from $8

The Balaclava Hotel was built in 1887 and has been serving this corner of Melbourne for longer than Federation. A few years back it got a renovation that managed the rare trick of modernising without gutting the character. The result is a pub that feels simultaneously heritage and current.

The ground-floor bar is your standard good-pub setup: solid tap list, decent spirits, friendly bartenders. But the rooftop is the drawcard. It’s an indoor-outdoor space that catches the afternoon sun and transforms into a moody, intimate spot after dark. On warm nights it’s packed; on cold ones the indoor section with its heaters and blankets does the job.

The bistro does honest pub food — bangers and mash, veggie burgers, prawn spring rolls — at prices that won’t make you flinch. They run weekly specials including $20 burger and beer Mondays, $25 steak Tuesdays, and $25 parma and pot on Wednesdays (with trivia at 7pm).

Don’t miss: The Wednesday parma and pot night. Grab a table upstairs, smash a parma, win trivia, feel like a local.

4. Ilona Staller

Address: 282–284 Carlisle Street, Balaclava
Vibe: 60s retro Italian — neon, leopard print, Negronis
Drinks: Italian-leaning cocktail list, wine (strong Italian and Australian selections), aperitivo
Prices: Cocktails $20–$24, wines from $14/glass, aperitivo spritzes $18

Named after the Italian porn-star-turned-politician (yes, really), Ilona Staller is the spicy younger sibling of St Kilda’s legendary Cicciolina. The fit-out leans hard into 60s retro: neon signage, perspex bar, leopard print stools, dark wood. It’s deliberately camp and genuinely fun.

The cocktail list is built around Italian classics — Negronis, Aperol spritzes, espresso martinis done with proper Italian coffee. The wine list is thoughtfully curated with a strong Italian section alongside Victorian labels. This isn’t a place for a quick schooner; it’s a place to settle in, order something stirred, and feel like you’re in a slightly more glamorous version of your life.

The food menu is Mediterranean-leaning with Italian foundations — think burrata, grilled octopus, and pastas. It works well for a full evening: dinner at one table, cocktails migrating to the bar later.

Open seven days from noon to 1am, it’s one of the latest-finishing venues on Carlisle Street. If you’re bar-hopping and want a last stop that doesn’t feel like an afterthought, this is it.

Don’t miss: A Negroni at the bar around 11pm on a Saturday. The lighting is perfect and the crowd thins to just the right density.

5. Ms Carlisles

Address: 137 Carlisle Street, Balaclava
Vibe: Cosy neighbourhood bar-restaurant — brick, wood, low lighting
Drinks: Cocktails, tap and bottled beer, wine, spirits
Prices: Cocktails $18–$22, tap beers $10–$13, wines from $12/glass

Ms Carlisles is the venue you stumble into on a Thursday night and immediately wonder why you hadn’t been before. The interior is all exposed brick, warm timber, and dim lighting — the kind of space that makes everyone look good and feel relaxed.

It straddles the line between bar and restaurant, and does both competently. The tapas and small plates menu is built for sharing: crispy pork belly, grilled calamari, and cheese boards that don’t feel like an afterthought. The cocktail list leans classic with a few signatures — nothing too experimental, but everything well-made.

The outdoor seating is dog-friendly, which on weekend afternoons means a parade of well-groomed whippets and kelpies mixed with couples nursing rosé. It’s one of the more relaxed vibes on the strip — no thumping music, no bottle service, no queues at the door.

What makes Ms Carlisles work is its consistency. It’s not trying to be the hottest bar in Balaclava. It’s trying to be the one you come back to every week. And it nails that.

Don’t miss: Weekend afternoon drinks in the courtyard. Bring the dog. Order the pork belly.

Cross-link: If you’re building a southside bar crawl, loop in a stop at one of Caulfield’s newer spots on the way through.

6. Grosvenor Hotel

Address: 10 Brighton Road, St Kilda East
Vibe: Old-school pub complex — multiple bars, sports, trivia, and a drive-through burger bar
Drinks: Tap beers, full spirits, cocktails, wine
Prices: Happy hour pints from $8, cocktails $16–$20, pot pots $6–$8

The Grosvenor is a beast of a venue. It’s been on Brighton Road since 1880 and has sprawled into a labyrinthine complex that houses a public bar, a sports bar, a bistro, function rooms, a bottle shop, and — gloriously — a drive-through burger bar out the back. You could spend an entire Saturday here without realising other venues exist.

The sports bar is the heart of the operation: big screens, TAB, and a crowd that takes their footy seriously. If you want to watch the AFL in a venue where the entire room cares about the result, this is your spot. The public bar is more old-school: good prices, no frills, and a TAB machine that’s seen more action than most nightclubs.

But the Grosvenor isn’t just a sports pub. The bistro does wood-fired pizzas and pub classics. They run nine weekly specials including trivia on Wednesday, $20 burger and beer Mondays, and $25 steak Tuesdays. Happy hour runs seven days a week with genuinely cheap pints and wines.

The function spaces upstairs are worth knowing about if you’re planning a birthday or work event — they’re well-priced and the staff are experienced with crowd management.

Don’t miss: Wednesday trivia. It draws a competitive, smart crowd and the prizes are worth winning.

The Carlisle Street Bar Crawl — A Suggested Route

If you’re doing the full strip, here’s the optimal order for a Saturday night:

  1. Start at The Local Taphouse (184 Carlisle St) — 6pm. Tasting paddle on the rooftop while the sun’s still up.
  2. Walk south to Ms Carlisles (137 Carlisle St) — 7:30pm. Cocktails and small plates.
  3. Cross to Ilona Staller (282 Carlisle St) — 9pm. Italian cocktails and the retro atmosphere.
  4. Finish at Pause Bar (268 Carlisle St) — 10:30pm. Live music and late-night drinks.

The Balaclava Hotel is walkable from any of these if you want to swap in a rooftop stop, and the Grosvenor is a 10-minute walk east if you’re still standing and want to end the night with a proper pub.

What We Skipped and Why

The Exford Hotel: It’s technically in the area but functions more as a backpacker pub with a focus on cheap jugs and live sport. If you’re after a polished evening, this isn’t it. If you’re after a $6 pot and a game of pool, it delivers.

Carlisle Wine Bar: This beloved spot on Carlisle Street has been quiet in recent years and wasn’t consistently trading when we visited. If it’s reopened, it was a genuine gem — but we can’t recommend it without confirming it’s active.

Si Señor: Technically a Mexican restaurant and marg bar rather than a bar bar. It’s great for tacos and margaritas (Monday marg specials are famous locally) but doesn’t function as a late-night drinking venue. We’ll cover it in our Carlisle Street food guide instead.

Getting There

By train: Balaclava Station is on the Sandringham line, right on Carlisle Street. Trains run until roughly midnight on weekends.
By tram: Route 78 runs along Hotham Street, one block east. Route 96 runs along the Esplanade and connects to St Kilda Station — a 10-minute walk south.
By car: Street parking exists but is competitive on weekends. The Grosvenor has its own car park if you end up there.
Rideshare: Balaclava is well-covered by Uber and DiDi. The pickup sweet spot is usually outside Balaclava Hotel on Carlisle Street.

Nearby Neighbourhoods to Explore

Balaclava sits at a crossroads of some of Melbourne’s best drinking suburbs. If you want to extend your night:

  • St Kilda: Beachfront bars, late-night venues, and Acland Street’s buzzing energy. A 10-minute walk south.
  • Windsor: Chapel Street’s best strip — cocktail bars, wine bars, and the kind of venues that look simple from outside and surprise you within.
  • Caulfield: Heading east, Caulfield has some quieter, more refined options if you want to escape the Friday night crowds.

The Verdict

Balaclava’s bar scene in 2026 is quietly excellent. It doesn’t have the volume of Fitzroy or the flash of South Yarra, but what it has is character — venues that know their crowd, prices that don’t punish you, and a street that still feels like a neighbourhood rather than a brand.

Carlisle Street works because it’s not trying to be everything. It’s not chasing the latest trend. It’s running good bars for people who live here, and welcoming everyone else who wanders in. That’s the Melbourne way, and Balaclava does it better than most.
