Balaclava After Dark: The Spots Only Locals Know

Words by Lina, Nightlife Writer | 18 March 2026

Balaclava doesn’t shout. It murmurs through cracked-paint doorways, flick-knife alleys and the low thud of bass you feel before you hear. Sandwiched between neon-lit St Kilda and postcard-perfect Ripponlea, the suburb keeps its best addresses half-lit on purpose. Below, the venues that reward the curious.

1. Mishka’s Aperitivo Bar

22 O’Donnell Gardens, Balaclava VIC 3183
Blink on Carlisle Street and you’ll miss the matte-black door with a single Cyrillic “M”. Push through and you’re in a 24-seat Soviet-kitsch salon where house-infused beet vodka arrives frozen in tiny laboratory beakers. The bartenders—ex-Attica and Above Board—run a tight eight-cocktail list that flips every fortnight. Ask for the “Dacha Fizz”: horseradish-distillate, prosecco and a salt-cured cherry that pops like caviar. DJs spin Ukrainian deep-house from 10 pm; no requests, no photos, no regrets.

2. The Drunken Poet’s Cellar

8-10 Chapel Street, Balaclava VIC 3183
Everyone knows the Drunken Poet upstairs—Irish tunes, sticky carpet, 3 am lock-ins. Fewer know the unmarked staircase behind the cigarette machine. Descend into a brick-lined 1890s coal cellar now fitted with Persian rugs and a single red bulb. Tuesday is “Whisky Confessional”: $20 gets you three half-pours of small-batch Irish plus a notebook to scribble your sins. Staff burn the pages at closing; the ash ends up in next week’s smoked-old-fashioned.

3. Sister Midnight Record Lounge

112 Carlisle Street, Balaclava VIC 3183
Half record shop, half listening bar. Owner Jaya curates 45s only—punk, Afro-beat, early electro—then pipes them through a valve amp that cost more than a hatchback. Cover charge is $10, redeemable for vinyl or natural wine. The house rule: no Shazam, no talking during the A-side. Midnight Fridays they dim the lights, pour zero-zero gamay and spin the full 17 minutes of “Marquee Moon”. Walk-ins welcome, but bring cash; the eftpos machine “interferes with the stylus”.

4. Bar Kismet

4/272 Carlisle Street, Balaclava VIC 3183
From the street it’s just another kebab shop. Push past the rotating meat and a velvet curtain slides open to a 30-seat Levantine cocktail den. Think amber lighting, brass trays and a drinks list built around arak, pomegranate molasses and Aleppo pepper. The “Midnight Beyrut” mixes gin, mint and fermented chilli for a slow burn that pairs with their 2 am lahmacue—a lahmacun/taco hybrid stuffed with harissa lamb and pickled turnip. Kitchen stays open till 3 am on weekends; thank me later.

5. The Velvet Shtetl

15 Inkerman Street, Balaclava VIC 3183
A queer-friendly, neo-Yiddish speakeasy hidden behind a faux bakery display of plastic challah. Ring the bell, name-drop “Hannah S.” (the password changes weekly on Instagram stories) and you’re ushered into a candle-lit salon decked with vintage portraits of drag ancestors. The drinks read like diaspora inside jokes: “Gefilte Daiquiri” (carp-fat-washed rum, pickle brine, dill oil) and “Sabra Sour” (cactus-pear, tequila, preserved lemon). Thursday hosts “Klezmer Karaoke” where punters yodel over live clarinet.

6. Lot 19 Rooftop Cinema

Cnr Hotham & Chapel, Balaclava VIC 3183
Above an abandoned carpet warehouse, enter through the graffiti’d roller door, climb five flights of splintered stairs and emerge onto a 50-seat astro-turf rooftop. Screens start at 9 pm sharp—cult classics only, think “The Hunger” or “The Castle” quoted ironically. BYO blanket; they supply wireless headphones so the cops can’t complain. The kiosk slings $12 negronis in plastic pouches and vegan jaffles that leak kimchi cheese. Book online; walk-ups cop the rusty beanbags.

7. The Last Chance Saloon

77 Grey Street, St Kilda VIC 3182 (five minutes walk, but worth it)
Okay, technically over the border, yet every Balaclava bartender finishes their shift here. Look for the red bulb above a boarded-up tattoo parlour; knock twice, pause, knock once. Inside: taxidermy, tax-time lighting and a jukebox loaded with Townes Van Zandt b-sides. They pour $10 boilermakers until the keg kicks—usually 2:37 am. Cash only, no menu, no wifi. Ask Lucy (the one with the snake tattoo) for “the usual” and you’ll get a shot of Old Forester plus a pot of Melbourne Bitter. She won’t remember your name, but she’ll remember your tip.

Map Your Crawl

Start with Mishka’s for low-light aperitivo, drift south to Sister Midnight for vinyl communion, then east to Bar Kismet for fire-level mezze. Finish on Lot 19 if the moon is out; if not, surrender to the Last Chance Saloon and let the night dissolve into Townes.

Pro Tips for the Uninitiated

  1. Postcodes matter. Balaclava spans 3183; stray into 3182 (St Kilda) only if you’re headed to the Last Chance.
  2. Cash is king. Three venues on this list lack eftpos; the others add 1.5 % surcharge after midnight.
  3. Password protocol. Velvet Shtetl rotates weekly; DM them a dumpling emoji for the current one.
  4. Last trains. Sandringham line from Balaclava station runs hourly after 1 am. Miss it and you’re in for a $45 Uber.
  5. No cameras. Mishka’s and the Cellar will ask you to leave if you Instagram the interior. Enjoy the moment, post the exterior tomorrow.

Final Word

Balaclava’s appeal isn’t the bright lights—it’s the low glow under roller doors, the bass lines leaking from basements, the bartenders who remember your drink but never your drama. Come curious, leave before sunrise, and whatever you do, don’t tell them Lina sent you. Some addresses are better when they stay between friends.
