18 Best Cafes in Carlton for Coffee, Brunch and Everything in Between

Priya Sandhu
13 Mar 2026 · 17 min read
Carlton

Here are the 18 best cafes in Carlton for 2026, from Lygon Street Italian institutions to Japanese lamington labs and specialty roasteries. This guide covers every corner of Melbourne’s coffee capital, with venues spanning Berkeley Street’s warehouse roasteries to Rathdowne Street’s leafy neighbourhood spots. Whether you’re after a traditional espresso, matcha perfection, or wild‑fermented bagels, Carlton delivers.

What’s inside:

  • Coffee institutions: Seven Seeds, Market Lane, University Cafe
  • Italian classics: DOC, Pidapipó, Brunetti Classico
  • Japanese‑Australian fusion: Tokyo Lamington, Attakai
  • Neighbourhood essentials: Baker D. Chirico, Florian, Rathdowne Street Cafe
  • All‑day venues: Sleepy’s Cafe & Wine Bar, Assembly

There’s something beautifully chaotic about Carlton’s cafe scene. Walk down Lygon Street on any given morning and you’ll find Italian nonnas arguing over espresso strength outside century-old institutions while twenty-somethings queue for $22 avo toast at the latest specialty roaster. This is a suburb where Melbourne’s coffee obsession began — University Cafe has been pulling shots since 1952 — and where it continues to evolve, with Japanese lamington labs and third-wave coffee temples sitting side by side.

Carlton doesn’t just do coffee; it does coffee culture. From the Berkeley Street warehouses housing some of Australia’s most respected roasteries to the Lygon Street gelaterias that have become pilgrimage sites, this pocket of inner Melbourne has mastered the art of taking your time. Whether you’re a Melbourne Uni student cramming for exams, a Carlton local grabbing your daily flat white, or a food tourist hunting down the city’s best cannoli, Carlton’s cafes deliver with the kind of confidence that only comes from decades of practice. Carlton sits just south of Fitzroy, Melbourne’s artsy dining hub, and consistently ranks high on our Suburb Vibe Score for its coffee culture.

The beauty lies in the diversity. You can start your day with a perfectly traditional espresso at an old-school Italian joint, lunch on inventive fusion at a Japanese-Australian hybrid, and finish with artisan gelato that would make Sicilian grandmothers weep. Here are the 18 cafes that make Carlton Melbourne’s undisputed coffee capital. For more inner north food adventures, explore our Fitzroy restaurant guide or browse all our Carlton guides.

1. Seven Seeds

The vibe: Melbourne coffee royalty in a converted warehouse that smells like heaven.

Seven Seeds isn’t just a cafe — it’s a coffee institution that helped put Melbourne on the global specialty coffee map. The Berkeley Street flagship sits in a soaring warehouse space that houses both the roastery and a cafe that seats close to 100 people. The industrial fitout, exposed brick walls, and the constant hum of roasting beans create an atmosphere that’s both serious about coffee and seriously welcoming. This is where Melbourne’s third-wave coffee movement found its voice, and the reverence for the bean is palpable in every corner.

Order this: House Blend flat white ($4.50)
Also try: F.Bomb filter coffee ($5.20)
Address: 114 Berkeley Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Mon-Fri 7:00am-5:00pm, Sat-Sun 8:00am-5:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-6, food $12-22)
Insider tip: Come early on weekends to snag one of the communal tables near the roastery — you’ll get a front-row seat to the daily coffee theatre.

2. Market Lane Coffee (Faraday Street)

The vibe: Stripped-back specialty coffee temple where every cup is a masterclass.

Market Lane’s Carlton outpost on Faraday Street is coffee minimalism at its finest. The space is deliberately unfussy — white walls, blonde timber, concrete floors — because when your coffee is this good, you don’t need distractions. This is serious coffee for serious drinkers, roasted at their Brunswick East facility and served with the kind of precision that makes other cafes nervous. The baristas here aren’t just making coffee; they’re conducting daily seminars in extraction science.

Order this: Single-origin pour-over ($6.80)
Also try: Market Lane blend espresso ($4.20)
Address: 176 Faraday Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Mon-Fri 7:00am-4:00pm, Sat-Sun 8:00am-4:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-7, light food $8-15)
Insider tip: Ask about the weekly single-origin rotation — the staff know their stuff and will guide you to something spectacular.

3. DOC Pizza & Mozzarella Bar

The vibe: Lygon Street’s Italian heart, where espresso is taken as seriously as mozzarella.

DOC isn’t just about pizza (though the pizza is exceptional). The Drummond Street venue channels the spirit of a Roman pizzeria, complete with marble-topped tables, vintage Italian signage, and the kind of espresso machine that looks like it could power a small Italian village. The coffee here is unapologetically Italian — short, strong, and meant to be consumed standing up while discussing the day’s headlines. This is where Carlton’s Italian community still gathers, and the conversations flow as smoothly as the coffee.

Order this: Traditional espresso ($3.50)
Also try: Cappuccino with house-made biscotti ($4.80)
Address: 295 Drummond Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Mon-Wed 5:00pm-late, Thu-Sun 12:00pm-late
Price range: $$ (coffee $3-5, pizza $18-28)
Insider tip: Come for lunch when the full menu is available — their mozzarella bar features some of the best buffalo mozzarella outside of Italy.

4. University Cafe

The vibe: Carlton’s living history lesson, serving coffee since before your grandparents were born.

University Cafe has been pouring shots on Lygon Street since 1952, making it one of Melbourne’s original espresso bars. The red vinyl booths, vintage signage, and old-school Italian waiters create an atmosphere that’s part museum, part neighbourhood institution. This isn’t Instagram coffee — it’s the real deal, served by people who’ve been doing this for decades to customers who’ve been coming here for just as long. The menu hasn’t changed much in 70 years, and that’s exactly the point.

Order this: Traditional long black ($4.20)
Also try: Spaghetti Bolognese with parmesan ($18)
Address: 257 Lygon Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Daily 7:00am-11:00pm
Price range: $ (coffee $4-5, pasta $16-24)
Insider tip: Grab a seat by the window for prime Lygon Street people-watching — it’s like having front-row seats to Carlton’s daily theatre.

5. Pidapipó

The vibe: Sicilian gelato perfection that happens to serve excellent coffee too.

While Pidapipó is famous for gelato that would make Sicilian nonnas weep with joy, their coffee game is equally impressive. The Lygon Street shop channels vintage Italian gelaterias with black and white tiles, marble counters, and display cases that look like jewellery stores for dessert lovers. The coffee is traditional Italian — none of this single-origin nonsense, just perfectly extracted espresso that cuts through the sweetness of whatever gelato you’re inevitably going to order.

Order this: Double shot macchiato ($4.50)
Also try: Affogato with vanilla gelato ($8.50)
Address: 299 Lygon Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Daily 11:00am-10:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-5, gelato $6-12)
Insider tip: The pistachio gelato is made from Sicilian pistachios and it’s worth the premium price — pair it with an espresso for the perfect Italian afternoon break.

6. King & Godfree

The vibe: Historic grocer turned modern food precinct, currently reinventing itself.

One of Australia’s oldest grocery stores (operating since 1884) is currently undergoing a major transformation. While the main deli and cafe sections are closed for renovation until late 2026, Johnny’s Green Room rooftop bar remains open via King & Godfree Lane. When it reopens, expect the full Italian experience — deli, wine bar, espresso bar, and upstairs dining. For now, it stands as a monument to Carlton’s Italian heritage and a promise of great things to come.

Order this: Check back in late 2026 for reopening
Also try: Visit Johnny’s Green Room upstairs for drinks
Address: 293-299 Lygon Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Currently closed for renovations
Price range: TBA upon reopening
Insider tip: Follow their social media for updates on the reopening — this is going to be one of Carlton’s biggest food stories when it returns.

7. Baker D. Chirico

The vibe: Artisan bakery where every loaf is a love letter to traditional bread-making.

Tucked away on Faraday Street, Baker D. Chirico represents the gold standard of Melbourne’s artisan bakery scene. The shop front is deliberately humble — timber shelves lined with perfectly formed sourdough loaves, rustic pastries, and Italian-inspired sweets. The coffee is excellent (they use Padre beans), but the real stars are the bomboloni — Italian-style doughnuts filled with house-made custard that sell out before lunch most days.

Order this: Flat white with custard bombolone ($9.50)
Also try: Sourdough toast with Pepe Saya butter ($8)
Address: 178 Faraday Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Mon-Sun 8:00am-6:00pm (closes 5:00pm weekends)
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-5, pastries $5-8)
Insider tip: Pre-order bomboloni for weekend mornings — they make limited numbers and locals know to call ahead.

8. Tokyo Lamington

The vibe: Cult fusion pastry shop where Australian classics get a Japanese makeover.

If you’ve wondered what happens when former Black Star Pastry chefs and N2 Extreme Gelato founders decide to reimagine the humble lamington, Tokyo Lamington is your answer. The tiny Elgin Street shop is marked by a colourful Lego-style sign and houses what might be Melbourne’s most innovative pastry project. Think yuzu-meringue lamingtons, fairy bread popcorn versions, and matcha numbers that would confuse and delight in equal measure.

Order this: Yuzu meringue lamington ($8)
Also try: Matcha lamington with white chocolate ($8)
Address: Elgin Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Wed-Sun 9:00am-4:00pm (or until sold out)
Price range: $$ (lamingtons $7-9, coffee $4-5)
Insider tip: Follow their Instagram for daily flavour updates — they’re constantly experimenting and limited-edition flavours disappear fast.

9. Brunetti Classico

The vibe: Italian cake palace where every visit feels like a celebration.

Brunetti’s Lygon Street flagship is Melbourne’s answer to a Milanese pasticceria. The glass cabinets stretch the length of the shop, packed with cakes that look like architectural marvels — tiramisu towers, cannoli mountains, and gelato displays that would make Willy Wonka jealous. The coffee is traditional Italian, served in proper cups with proper saucers, because this is a place that takes the ritual seriously.

Order this: Cappuccino with cannoli ($12.50)
Also try: Sicilian cassata slice ($8.50)
Address: 380 Lygon Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Daily 6:00am-10:00pm (until 11:00pm Fri-Sat)
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-6, cakes $6-15)
Insider tip: Come for the evening aperitivo service — they transform into a proper Italian bar after 5pm with Negronis and antipasti.

10. Standing Room (Carlton)

The vibe: University coffee perfected, now available to the masses.

Standing Room has been serving Melbourne University students and staff since 2013, perfecting the art of fast, excellent coffee for people who actually need caffeine to function. Their new Carlton location on Grattan Street brings that same no-nonsense excellence to the wider public. The design is minimalist — think Scandinavian coffee shop meets Japanese efficiency — and the focus is purely on coffee and pastries.

Order this: Seasonal shrub with soda water ($6)
Also try: Self-serve cold brew ($4.50)
Address: 183 Grattan Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Mon-Fri 7:30am-4:00pm, Sat 8:00am-4:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-6, food $8-15)
Insider tip: The seasonal shrubs are genuinely innovative — mango with vanilla cream in summer, chai-spiced versions in winter.

11. Attakai (Carlton)

The vibe: Japanese precision meets Australian cafe culture in perfect harmony.

Attakai’s Bouverie Street location brings serious Japanese cafe culture to Carlton. The matcha is whisked to order using leaves sourced from Shizuoka and Uji, served with rotating seasonal syrups that currently include strawberry, blueberry, and dragon fruit. But it’s the sando section that really sets this place apart — proper Japanese-style sandwiches that would fit perfectly in a Tokyo convenience store.

Order this: Whisked matcha with strawberry syrup ($7)
Also try: Wafu steak sando with wasabi aioli ($16)
Address: Bouverie Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Daily 8:00am-4:00pm
Price range: $$ (drinks $6-8, sandos $12-18)
Insider tip: The kome latte (espresso with rice milk) is surprisingly creamy and completely dairy-free — perfect for the lactose intolerant coffee addicts.

12. Assembly (Carlton)

The vibe: Neighbourhood cafe with serious coffee credentials and a cult following.

Assembly has quietly become one of Carlton’s most beloved coffee spots, partly because it feels like a secret worth keeping. The original location has developed such a devoted following that they’ve opened a second spot in North Melbourne. The coffee is exceptional (they roast their own), the pastries come from reputable sources, and the vibe is relaxed enough that you can actually work here without feeling like you’re taking up valuable real estate.

Order this: House blend piccolo latte ($4.50)
Also try: Austro pastries from South Melbourne ($6-8)
Address: Carlton (check current location)
Hours: Daily 7:00am-3:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-6, food $8-15)
Insider tip: They also function as a cellar door for Tina non-alcoholic drinks — perfect for the sober curious coffee crowd.

13. Florian

The vibe: Old-soul cafe that feels like it existed before Instagram ruined everything.

Florian has captured something increasingly rare in Melbourne’s cafe scene — genuine, unpretentious hospitality. The Rathdowne Village location has the relaxed, come-as-you-are feel of pre-Instagram cafe culture, where the focus is on simple, seasonal food rather than photogenic plates. The produce-driven menu changes daily based on what’s good, and the queues out the door prove that people are hungry for this kind of authenticity.

Order this: Gruyere omelette with seasonal greens ($18)
Also try: Seasonal tartine with Allpress coffee ($12)
Address: 617 Rathdowne Street, Carlton North VIC 3054
Hours: Mon-Fri 7:30am-4:00pm, Sat-Sun 8:00am-4:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-5, food $12-22)
Insider tip: They’ve now opened Florian To Go next door for takeaway service — same quality, no queues.

14. Auction Rooms

The vibe: Heritage-listed coffee temple where serious roasting happens alongside serious brunching.

While technically in North Melbourne, Auction Rooms is so close to Carlton it practically belongs here. The converted auction house on Errol Street houses both a roastery and one of Melbourne’s most respected breakfast destinations. The soaring ceilings, exposed brick, and industrial fitout create the perfect backdrop for coffee that’s been taken seriously since day one. This is part of the St Ali empire, which means the coffee credentials are beyond question.

Order this: Auction Rooms blend espresso ($4.50)
Also try: Corn fritters with bacon and avocado ($22)
Address: 103-107 Errol Street, North Melbourne VIC 3051
Hours: Daily 7:00am-4:00pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-6, brunch $18-28)
Insider tip: The venue is available for functions — imagine your next birthday party in a converted auction house with exceptional coffee.

15. Masses Bagels

The vibe: Wild-fermented bagel perfection that draws queues across Melbourne.

Masses Bagels started at Carlton Farmers Market and built such a devoted following that people would line up around the market stalls just for their wild-fermented bagels. Now permanently located in Collingwood, their weekend appearances at Carlton Farmers Market still create stampedes. The bagels are proper New York-style with that perfect chew, topped with house-made whipped quark or smoked rainbow trout.

Order this: Everything bagel with whipped quark and chives ($12)
Also try: Smoked rainbow trout bagel ($18)
Address: Carlton Farmers Market, Princes Park (Saturdays only)
Hours: Saturdays 8:00am-12:00pm (market stall)
Price range: $$ (bagels $8-18)
Insider tip: Get there early — they sell out by 11am most Saturdays, and for good reason.

16. Coffee on Drummond

The vibe: No-frills neighbourhood spot that does the basics brilliantly.

Sometimes you don’t need innovation or Instagram-worthy presentations — sometimes you just need really good coffee and a fresh croissant. Coffee on Drummond delivers exactly that with quiet efficiency and zero pretension. The space is simple, the service is quick, and the coffee hits the spot every single time. This is the kind of place that becomes part of your daily routine without you even realising it.

Order this: Flat white with butter croissant ($8.50)
Also try: Matcha latte for something different ($5.50)
Address: Drummond Street, Carlton VIC 3053
Hours: Daily 7:00am-3:00pm
Price range: $ (coffee $4-6, pastries $4-7)
Insider tip: Perfect pre-Carlton Gardens walk fuel — grab your coffee and pastry and head straight to the park.

17. Rathdowne Street Cafe

The vibe: Tree-lined Carlton North institution that feels like it’s been here forever (in the best way).

Set under the leafy canopy of Rathdowne Street, this neighbourhood cafe captures the relaxed, village-like atmosphere that makes Carlton North so appealing. The outdoor seating spills onto the footpath under mature plane trees, creating one of Melbourne’s most pleasant cafe environments. The menu keeps things simple — good coffee, honest food, and the kind of service that makes you want to linger over the weekend papers.

Order this: Long black with ricotta hotcakes ($16)
Also try: Smashed avocado with feta ($17)
Address: 619 Rathdowne Street, Carlton North VIC 3054
Hours: Daily 5:00am-2:30pm
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-5, food $12-20)
Insider tip: The early opening hours (5am!) make this perfect for pre-work coffee runs or post-exercise fuel.

18. Sleepy’s Cafe & Wine Bar

The vibe: All-day cafe that transforms into a neighbourhood wine bar after dark.

Sleepy’s represents the evolution of Melbourne cafe culture — places that work just as well for morning coffee as they do for evening wine. The Carlton North location serves excellent coffee during the day and natural wines by night, with a menu that spans both occasions. The fitout is relaxed and welcoming, with the kind of lighting that makes everyone look good and feel comfortable.

Order this: Batch brew coffee with house-made granola ($14)
Also try: Natural wine and cheese plate (evening, $28)
Address: Carlton North (check specific address)
Hours: Daily 7:00am-late
Price range: $$ (coffee $4-6, food $15-25)
Insider tip: Come back in the evening for their natural wine selection — they know their producers and pour generous glasses.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Carlton just Italian cafes?

While Lygon Street is famous for its Italian heritage, Carlton’s cafe scene has expanded dramatically. Alongside classic Italian espresso bars like University Cafe and DOC, you’ll find Japanese‑Australian fusion at Tokyo Lamington, specialty roasteries like Seven Seeds, and modern all‑day venues like Sleepy’s. Explore all our Carlton guides to see the full diversity.

Where should I go for the best coffee in Carlton?

For specialty coffee, head to Seven Seeds or Market Lane Coffee for precision brewing. For traditional Italian espresso, DOC or University Cafe deliver authentic Lygon Street experience. For something different, Attakai offers Japanese matcha and sandos. Check our Suburb Vibe Score to see how Carlton ranks for coffee culture across Melbourne.

What’s the best time to visit Carlton cafes?

Weekday mornings are ideal for avoiding crowds, but Saturday mornings offer vibrant market atmosphere at Carlton Farmers Market (where Masses Bagels operates). Many cafes open early (5am at Rathdowne Street Cafe) for pre‑work coffee, while venues like Sleepy’s transition to wine bars in the evening. For a different inner‑north experience, pair your cafe crawl with our Fitzroy restaurant guide.

How to Do a Carlton Cafe Crawl

The key to a successful Carlton cafe crawl is understanding the neighbourhood’s geography and pacing yourself appropriately. Start on Berkeley Street with Seven Seeds for your morning coffee education — this is where you learn what Melbourne coffee is all about. Walk down to Faraday Street for Market Lane’s precision brewing, then head to Lygon Street for the Italian experience at DOC or University Cafe.

Lygon Street is where you’ll spend most of your time, and for good reason. The strip between Grattan and Elgin Streets contains more coffee history per metre than anywhere else in Australia. Take your time here — duck into Pidapipó for gelato, browse Brunetti’s cake display, and soak up the atmosphere that’s been developing for over 70 years.

Don’t ignore the side streets. Elgin Street houses Tokyo Lamington’s experimental pastries, while Rathdowne Street in Carlton North offers a different vibe entirely with Florian and the tree-lined charm of neighbourhood cafes. The beauty of Carlton is that you can walk between wildly different coffee experiences in a matter of minutes.

Finish strong. If you’re still standing after multiple coffees, head to one of the all-day venues like Sleepy’s for wine, or catch Masses Bagels at Carlton Farmers Market if you’re doing this on a Saturday. Carlton’s cafe scene rewards exploration, but it also rewards knowing when to stop and properly appreciate where you are.

The secret to Carlton coffee crawling isn’t hitting every single cafe in one day (though we admire the ambition). It’s understanding that each place represents a different aspect of Melbourne’s coffee evolution, from 1950s Italian immigrants to modern Japanese precision. Take your time, drink plenty of water between stops, and remember that Carlton’s cafes have been perfecting this art for decades — they’ll still be here tomorrow if you need to pace yourself.
