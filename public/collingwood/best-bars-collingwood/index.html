Best Bars in Collingwood 2026: Live Music, Craft Beer and Late-Night Haunts

Collingwood doesn’t do boring drinks. Squeezed between Hoddle Street and Smith Street, this pocket-sized suburb packs more bars per square metre than most Melbourne postcodes — and every single one has a personality. Sticky-floored punk temples sit three doors from natural wine havens. Craft breweries share laneways with cocktail bars that seat twelve people and nothing more.

If you’re heading to Collingwood for a night out in 2026, this is where you should be drinking.

Smith Street: The Main Strip

The Gasometer Hotel

484 Smith Street | thegasometerhotel.com.au

The Gaso is a Collingwood institution. The blood-red exterior is unmissable on Smith Street, and behind those doors sits one of Melbourne’s most versatile live music venues. The retractable dome roof over the main bandroom is genuinely impressive — it transforms from an intimate gig space to an open-air courtyard depending on the weather and the mood.

The front bar is low-key enough for a midweek pint, but weekends bring a roster of local and touring acts that range from punk to electronica. If you only hit one venue on Smith Street, make it this one.

Best for: Live music, late nights, a proper pub experience.

The Grace Darling Hotel

114 Smith Street | thegracedarlinghotel.com.au

This two-storey bluestone pub has been on Smith Street since the 19th century — and fun fact, the Collingwood Football Club was founded here. The atmospheric, candle-lit bar upstairs is one of the most striking rooms in Melbourne, and the speakeasy-style basement bandroom hosts everything from jazz to indie rock.

Grab dinner in the front bar (the menu is better than it needs to be for a pub this old) and then drift downstairs when the music starts.

Best for: History buffs, live music, atmospheric date nights.

Fixation Brewing

414 Smith Street | fixationbrewing.com.au

If you’re an IPA person, Fixation is your church. This urban brewery operates out of an industrial warehouse and specialises in hop-forward beers that are unapologetically flavour-first. Their Collingwood-themed specialty brews rotate regularly — grab a pint of Easey Street and park yourself on the Smith Street seats.

The vibe is casual, the beer is excellent, and it’s one of the key stops on the Collingwood Beer Trail.

Best for: Craft beer enthusiasts, afternoon sessions, brewery crawls.

The Craft & Co.

390 Smith Street | thecraftandco.com.au

The Craft & Co. does everything. Beer brewed on-site. Wine made in-house. Spirits distilled out the back. It sounds like it shouldn’t work, but somehow it does — and the food is surprisingly good too. It’s been a Smith Street fixture since 2015 and remains one of the most reliable spots for a drink that doubles as an education.

Best for: Groups who can’t agree on what to drink.

The Hotel Collingwood (formerly Robert Burns Hotel)

376 Smith Street | hotelcollingwood.com.au

The old Robbie Burns has had a facelift under new owners, but the bones remain the same — a proper local pub with quality beers from nearby breweries and a menu that punches above its weight. The Bobby’s Parma is borderline famous, and the slow-braised beef pappardelle deserves more attention than it gets.

Best for: Classic pub vibes, parmas, supporting local breweries.

Goldy’s Tavern

Smith Street | No website — just show up.

Despite the modern refurb, Goldy’s feels like it’s been here forever. The sunny, plant-filled atrium is perfect for afternoon drinks, and the knockout-style pool competitions mid-week are worth showing up for even if you’re terrible at pool (you will lose, but you’ll have fun).

Upmarket parmas and a rotating tap list keep things interesting.

Best for: All-day drinking, pool competitions, sunny arvo sessions.

Hippo Bottle & Bar

290 Smith Street

Part independent bottle shop, part bar. The Hippo lets you try before you buy from a massive selection of craft beers, boutique spirits and natural wines. It’s the kind of place you pop into for one drink and leave an hour later carrying a bag of bottles you didn’t plan on buying.

Best for: Bottle shop browsing with a beer in hand.

Johnston Street: The Side Quest

Nighthawks

136 Johnston Street | @nighthawksbar

Inspired by the beautiful old dive bars found across America, Nighthawks is the kind of neighbourhood joint where you can walk in alone with a book and feel completely at home. Downstairs, a DJ spins records. Upstairs, you’ll find back-to-back gigs from stellar local acts. The pool table gets competitive after 10pm.

This is one of Melbourne’s most underrated live music venues. Don’t sleep on it.

Best for: Solo drinks, live music discovery, low-key weeknight outings.

The Tote

Johnston Street | thetotehotel.com

The Tote is arguably Melbourne’s most legendary live music venue. Full stop. It’s survived noise complaints, temporary closures, and cultural shifts, and it’s still here — still putting on punk, metal and stoner-rock acts in rooms with sticky carpets and a permanent buzz of energy.

Three gig spaces (main room, front bar, upstairs) mean there’s almost always something on. If you care about Melbourne’s live music scene, you’ve either been to The Tote or you need to go.

Best for: Live music pilgrims, punk and rock fans, gig-goers who don’t mind sticky floors.

Thank You Bar

210 Johnston Street

Easy to walk past if you don’t know it’s there — which is part of the charm. Thank You Bar runs pool in the courtyard, DJs on the decks, and regular collaborations with surrounding eateries. The cocktail list is tight and the wine selection leans natural. It’s the kind of bar that rewards repeat visits.

Best for: Hidden gem seekers, casual nights out, courtyard drinks.

Off the Beaten Track

Above Board

Smith Street (above a shopfront) | 16 seats only.

Melbourne’s most exclusive cocktail bar seats sixteen people. Finding it is half the experience — there’s no sign, just a staircase above a shop. Once you’re in, you’ll find one of Australia’s best bartenders making drinks with precision that borders on theatrical. The cocktail menu changes regularly but the quality never dips.

Arrive early or prepare to wait.

Best for: Cocktail obsessives, intimate date nights, bragging rights.

Hope St Radio

Collingwood Yards | hopestradio.com

A glowing wine and pasta bar inside Collingwood’s massive arts hub, doubling as the home of Hope St Radio (the community radio station). The rigatoni all’amatriciana is outstanding, the Fernet-Branca-and-cola sorbet is unexpected, and the lo-fi wines flow freely. Settle in on the rooftop or grab a booth inside.

Best for: Wine lovers, pasta cravings, arts precinct vibes.

Runner Up

Collingwood Yards rooftop

Above the Yards, Runner Up offers wallet-friendly wines and cocktails with a view that costs nothing extra. The rooftop is sunny and filled with fruit trees. Rotating DJ sets through a solid sound system keep the energy right without overpowering conversation.

Best for: Rooftop drinks on a budget, afternoon sun, good DJs.

Molly Rose Brewing

Wellington Street | mollyrose.com.au

In an old furniture warehouse, Molly Rose brews some of the most interesting beer in Melbourne — sour-coffee ales, beers that taste like wine, and seasonal specials that push boundaries. The Southeast Asian restaurant next door (with shared beer garden) makes this a full evening destination.

Best for: Adventurous beer drinkers, dinner-and-drinks combos.

Le Bon Ton

Collingwood | lebonton.com.au

New Orleans meets Texan BBQ meets Melbourne laneway bar. Le Bon Ton is loud, smoky, and serves brisket and bourbon until late. It’s the kind of bar where you start with a cocktail and end up at 1am listening to someone play slide guitar. The BBQ is serious — this isn’t just a bar with food, it’s a restaurant with an excellent bar.

Best for: BBQ lovers, late-night sessions, bourbon cocktails.

The Gem Bar and Dining

Collingwood

Saloon-style pub with an Elvis fixation. The boot-worn floorboards tell you everything about the crowd — this is a neighbourhood joint where country music acts play to a room of regulars, and the Southern fried chicken is cooked without irony. If you’re tired of precious cocktail bars, The Gem is the antidote.

Best for: Country music fans, no-nonsense pub food, character.

The Collingwood Beer Trail

If you’re serious about craft beer, Collingwood has its own dedicated Beer Trail connecting the suburb’s breweries and craft beer venues. It’s a self-guided crawl that hits Fixation, Molly Rose, and several other stops. Perfect for a Saturday afternoon with no agenda.

How to Get to Collingwood

Tram: Route 86 runs the full length of Smith Street. Jump on from the CBD and you’re there in 15 minutes.

Train: Closest station is Collingwood on the Hurstbridge/Mernda line — a short walk to Johnston Street.

Cycling: Collingwood is flat and well-connected by bike lanes from the CBD. Lock up on Smith Street and you’re sorted.

Driving: Street parking is limited. If you’re driving, try the side streets off Wellington or use the Collingwood Yards car park.
