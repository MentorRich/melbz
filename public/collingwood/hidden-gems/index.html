Hidden Gems & Suburb Secrets in Collingwood

Lina Nguyen

Nightlife Editor • Mar 18, 2026 • 6 min read

Updated 18 March 2026 | Nightlife Writer

Collingwood After Dark: The Spots Only The Locals Know

I’ve lost count of the nights I’ve started on Smith Street and ended up somewhere I never planned to be—down a laneway that smells like jasmine and hops, behind a roller door that rattles like it’s guarding treasure, or on a rooftop that doesn’t appear on Google Maps until you’re already up there. Collingwood keeps its best addresses quiet; you have to earn them. Below are eight places I’ve earned the hard way, mapped so you can skip the blind alleys and head straight for the good stuff.

1. Under The Counter

Address: 90-92 Johnston Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

Blink and you’ll walk past the narrow doorway wedged between two graffiti-splashed warehouses. Push through and you’re in a 28-seat basement that feels like a Tokyo jazz bar teleported to Melbourne. House-fermented plum wine arrives in tiny ceramic cups; yakitori is grilled over binchotan in the open kitchen; the vinyl-only soundtrack is curated by whoever’s bartending that night. No bookings after 9 pm, so rock up early, order the off-menu shōchū highball, and let the staff decide what you eat.

2. The Mill & Bakery

Address: 40 Sackville Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

By day, it’s a flour-dusted bakery turning out sourdough loaves that sell out before noon. By night, the original 1850s bluestone mill becomes a candle-lit wine bar pouring low-intervention Victorian pinot and pouring it hard. Chef Nadia swaps the bread oven for a wood-fired grill after 6 pm; the menu is three items long and changes daily. Ask for the “baker’s snack”—a slab of still-warm rye smeared with cultured butter and anchovy—then chase it with a glass of Syrahme from the Strathbogie Ranges.

3. Bobby’s Bodega

Address: 64-66 Oxford Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

Part corner store, part natural-wine tavern. Walk past the shelves of tinned fish and laundry powder, head toward the fridge humming with pet-nat, and keep going till the fluorescent lights fade into something warmer. The back room is lined with vintage Spanish tiles and smells like paprika and orange peel. Tuesday is conservas night: $15 gets you a can of razor clams, a fork, and a slice of sourdough. Order a glass of txakoli on tap and pretend you’re in San Sebastián until the last train home.

4. Radio Mexico HQ

Address: 12-14 Peel Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

You’ve probably eaten tacos at the St Kilda original, but the Collingwood satellite is where the chefs experiment after midnight. The roller-door entrance looks like a garage until you spot the tiny neon chili. Inside: concrete floors, a mezcal list longer than your arm, and a secret upstairs mezzanine that opens only on weekends. Ask bartender Karla for the “FM frequency” margarita—habanero-infused mezcal, charred pineapple, and a salt rim mixed with crushed barbecue corn chips. It shouldn’t work, yet it absolutely slaps.

5. The Shadowboxer

Address: 123 Johnston Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

From street level it’s just another Victorian pub. Head up the staircase marked “Private” and you’ll find a windowless cocktail bar that feels like a 1970s boxing gym turned louche lounge. Velvet drapes, red leather booths, and a drinks list categorised by “Round 1” to “Round 12”. Order the “TKO”—rum, coffee liqueur, and house cola reduction served in a chilled metal mug—then challenge the bartender to a speed-pour on the vintage arcade machine. Loser pays the next round; house always wins.

6. Clancy’s Fish & Chips

Address: 111 Queens Parade, Fitzroy North VIC 3068 (Collingwood side of the border)

Technically the postcode sneaks into Fitzroy North, but the deep-fryer sits two metres inside Collingwood’s imaginary line so I’m claiming it. Clancy’s is open till 1 am on weekends, frying barramundi in beef-tallow batter that stays crunchy even after a tram ride home. Skip the tomato sauce and ask for the “bartender’s condiment”: a mix of house-fermented hot sauce and tartare that the staff keep in unmarked squeeze bottles. Eat on the kerb, watch the 86 tram rattle past, and feel the city exhale.

7. The Craft & Co Cellar Door

Address: 390 Smith Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

Everyone knows the ground-floor restaurant, but few venture into the subterranean barrel room where single-batch gin and whiskey age in ex-wine casks. Tours run hourly until 8 pm, but if you loiter politely after the last group, the cellar hand might pour you an experimental gin infused with native pepperberry—strictly “lab” pours not yet cleared for retail. Bottles are numbered; ask to buy one and you’ll be met with a polite “not yet”, which only makes the next sip taste better.

8. The Night Heron

Address: 231 Napier Street, Collingwood VIC 3066

No signage, no Instagram, no menu on the door. The only marker is a brass heron nailed above an unmarked black gate. Ring the buzzer; give the name that was texted to you earlier that day. Inside: a candle-lit courtyard strung with climbing jasmine and a six-seat bar that specialises in vintage spirits. The bartender—always the same woman in a silk kimono—will ask what time you need to be home, then builds you a flight based on how many minutes you have left before the last Upfield train. You’ll probably taste a 1980s Canadian rye or a pre-phylloxera cognac. Pay by cash; leave a tip in the ceramic heron’s beak on your way out.

How To String Them Together

Start with natural-wine tins at Bobby’s Bodega while the commuter crowd thins out. Wander north to The Mill for a late carb-load, then double back to Johnston Street for mezcal at Radio Mexico and a knock-out cocktail upstairs at Shadowboxer. If you’re still vertical, finish at Night Heron—assuming you secured the text code before 6 pm. That’s five venues within a 900-metre radius; wear comfortable shoes and don’t plan on driving.

When You Finally Need Food Again

Skip the standard 2 am souvlaki and head to Clancy’s for that beef-tallow crunch. Or, if it’s Thursday, Under The Counter runs a midnight ramen service for industry types—ask for the “staff bowl” and slide a twenty across the bar.

Getting Home

The 86 tram runs all night; Smith Street stops are a five-minute stagger from every venue listed. If you’re south-side, the last Upfield train departs Flinders Street at 12:55 am on weekends—Night Heron will pour you into a taxi if you miss it.

One Last Piece of Advice

Collingwood rewards curiosity but punishes loud treasure-hunters. Don’t Instagram the unmarked doors, don’t ask for the “secret menu” at full volume, and never, ever name-drop this article while ordering. Just nod, say thanks, and maybe buy the bartender a beer. Do that, and the next time you ring the Night Heron buzzer, the gate might open a little faster.
