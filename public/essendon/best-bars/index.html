Best Bars in Essendon Melbourne

Priya Sandhu Mar 21, 2026
Essendon

The Drinking Scene in Essendon

Essendon sits 10km northwest of the CBD on the Craigieburn train line, and its bar scene reflects a suburb that is family-focused by day and quietly social by night. The two main commercial strips — Rose Street and Keilor Road — provide the drinking options, and while this is not a suburb you would move to for nightlife, the quality of what is here has improved steadily over the past few years.

The crowd skews older and more settled than the inner-city bar hubs. People here drink to catch up with friends, not to be seen. That creates a relaxed, no-pressure atmosphere that makes these bars easy to become a regular at.

Where to Drink in Essendon

Rose Street strip — Rose Street between Fletcher Street and Mount Alexander Road has the main concentration of restaurants and bars. A few wine-focused spots have opened here in recent years, sitting alongside established pubs and Italian restaurants that have been pouring for decades.

Keilor Road — The Keilor Road strip, running northwest from the Rose Street intersection, has its own collection of pubs and eateries. The dining here skews toward Italian and Modern Australian, with bars attached to several restaurants.

The Local Pubs — Essendon has several pubs that have been operating for generations. Some have been fully renovated with craft taps and modern menus. Others remain classic locals — TAB on the screen, $9 pots, and a parma that has not changed in 20 years. Both are worth knowing.

Wine Bars — A couple of smaller wine-focused spots along Rose Street do solid by-the-glass lists with cheese and charcuterie boards. These are the kind of places that close by 10pm but are perfect for a mid-week glass.

Beer Gardens — Several Essendon pubs have outdoor areas that come alive in the warmer months. The beer garden crowd here is family-friendly during the afternoon and shifts to a more social group after dark.

When to Go

After work (5-7pm) — Rose Street bars are at their best in this window. Locals unwinding, relaxed service, no queues.

Friday night — Both strips fill up from 6:30pm. Book a restaurant and move to a bar afterwards, or arrive early to grab a table.

Saturday — Peak social time. Bars are full by 8pm. The vibe is mature and friendly.

Sunday session — Some of Essendon’s pubs do proper Sunday sessions with live music or sport on the screen. Low-key and genuinely enjoyable.

What Makes Essendon Different

Essendon is Bombers territory — the football club’s identity is woven through the suburb, and that extends to the pubs. Footy season brings a particular energy to the local bars, especially on game weekends. Beyond football, the drinking scene is shaped by a community that values quality and consistency over novelty.

With one-bedroom rents around $350 per week, the crowd is established professionals and young families rather than students. The bars reflect that — well-run, fairly priced, and focused on getting the basics right.

Tips

  1. Walk Rose Street end to end — get a feel for the options before committing
  2. Check Keilor Road too — it has a different energy to Rose Street, often quieter
  3. Weeknight drinking is underrated — better service, more space, same drinks
  4. Ask the bartender — they know what to order and what to skip
  5. Game day energy — if you want atmosphere, come when the Bombers are playing

FAQ

What are the best bars near Essendon station? Rose Street is a 5-minute walk from Essendon station and has the highest concentration of bars and wine spots. Keilor Road is also accessible on foot from the station.

Is Essendon good for nightlife? Essendon is good for relaxed evenings — wine bars, pubs, and restaurants that stay open into the evening. For late-night or club-style nightlife, the CBD is about 20 minutes by train.

How much does a night out in Essendon cost? A few drinks and a bar meal will run $50 to $80 per person. Wine by the glass costs $14 to $18, beers $9 to $14 for a pot. Competitive with other middle-ring suburbs.

Does Essendon have live music? Several pubs book local acts on Thursday through Saturday nights. The scene is low-key but consistent — check venue social media pages for weekly listings.
