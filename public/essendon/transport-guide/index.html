Transport in Essendon 2026: Trains, Trams & the Commute Reality

J
Jack Morrison Mar 21, 2026 Updated Mar 2026
Essendon

Essendon’s transport game scored B+ in our latest review. Here is what that actually means for your daily commute. Sitting 10km northwest of the CBD, Essendon has reliable train access and reasonable road connections, though it lacks direct tram service.

Getting to the CBD

By train: Essendon station is on the Craigieburn line. Trains to Flagstaff take about 18 to 22 minutes during peak, running every 8 to 12 minutes. Off-peak services run every 15 to 20 minutes. This is the most reliable commute option.

By bus: Several bus routes connect Essendon to Moonee Ponds, Ascot Vale, and the western suburbs. Buses along Mount Alexander Road can connect you to tram routes at Moonee Ponds for an alternative city-bound trip.

By car: Driving to the CBD takes 20 to 40 minutes depending on traffic. Mount Alexander Road runs south-east toward the city. CityLink is accessible from Essendon, which is a genuine advantage for airport trips and cross-city driving.

Train Details

Essendon station sits on the Craigieburn line and is located near the intersection of Rose Street and the rail corridor. The line runs through Moonee Ponds, Newmarket, and North Melbourne before entering the City Loop (Flagstaff, Melbourne Central, Flinders Street).

Peak frequency: Every 8 to 12 minutes. Off-peak: Every 15 to 20 minutes. Night Network: Hourly all-night services on Friday and Saturday nights.

Glenbervie station (one stop north toward Strathmore) is also accessible from the northern parts of Essendon.

Tram Access

Essendon does not have direct tram access. The nearest tram route is Route 59 along Mount Alexander Road in Moonee Ponds, about 2km south-east of the Essendon centre. If tram access matters to your commute, you will need to bus or cycle to Moonee Ponds first.

Bus Routes

Buses connect Essendon to surrounding suburbs that are not directly on the train line. Routes along Keilor Road, Buckley Street, and Mount Alexander Road link to Moonee Ponds, Ascot Vale, Niddrie, and Highpoint Shopping Centre. Frequency varies — main routes run every 15 to 20 minutes during peak, less often on weekends.

Cycling

The flat terrain helps Essendon’s cycling credentials. The Moonee Ponds Creek Trail is accessible from the southern end of the suburb and provides a traffic-separated route toward the city via Flemington and North Melbourne. Cycling to the CBD takes about 30 to 40 minutes.

Residential streets are generally wide enough for comfortable cycling, though dedicated bike lanes on the main roads (Rose Street, Keilor Road) are limited.

Driving & Parking

Parking in Essendon is comfortable compared to inner-city suburbs. Most houses have driveways or garages. Street parking near Rose Street and Keilor Road has time restrictions during business hours, but residential streets are generally unrestricted.

CityLink access is a major practical advantage — you can reach the airport in about 15 minutes, the western suburbs quickly, and the southeastern suburbs via the Monash Freeway connection. This makes Essendon particularly practical for people who drive for work.

The Verdict

Essendon gets a B+ for transport. The Craigieburn line train is reliable and reasonably fast, CityLink access adds driving flexibility, and the Moonee Ponds Creek Trail gives cyclists a dedicated route. The absence of direct tram access is the main gap. Overall, it covers the basics well — functional, not outstanding, and a realistic daily commute option.

FAQ

How long does it take to get from Essendon to the CBD? By train, about 18 to 22 minutes to Flagstaff during peak. By car, 20 to 40 minutes depending on traffic.

What train line is Essendon on? The Craigieburn line. It runs through Moonee Ponds and North Melbourne before entering the City Loop.

Does Essendon have tram access? Not directly. Route 59 runs along Mount Alexander Road in Moonee Ponds, about 2km away. Train is the primary public transport option.

Is there night public transport from Essendon? Yes. Night Network services run hourly all night on Friday and Saturday on the Craigieburn line.
