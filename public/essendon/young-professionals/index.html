Essendon for Young Professionals 2026: Lifestyle, Cost & Nightlife

D
Dani Reyes Mar 21, 2026 Updated Mar 2026
Essendon

Young professionals in Essendon tend to love some things and tolerate others. The suburb sits 10km northwest of the CBD on the Craigieburn line, and its strengths — reliable transport, good food, and quiet residential streets — come with trade-offs around nightlife and social scene. Here is the honest breakdown.

The After-Work Scene

Essendon has enough for a weeknight drink — a few wine bars and pubs on Rose Street that attract a post-work crowd. The Italian restaurants along Rose Street and Keilor Road double as social spots for dinner and drinks. For anything more substantial, the CBD is about 20 minutes by train, and Moonee Ponds (one stop south) has additional options along Puckle Street.

The Essendon Football Club connection means the pubs have particular energy during footy season. If you follow the Bombers — or any AFL team — the local pubs on match day are worth experiencing.

The Weekend Factor

Weekends in Essendon have a settled rhythm. Saturday brunch on Rose Street is a thing — the cafes are good, the Italian bakeries on Keilor Road are excellent, and the queues are shorter than the inner-city strips. Windy Hill and the surrounding parks work for a morning run or casual sport. The Moonee Ponds Creek Trail gives you a proper cycling and walking route.

If you want more action on weekends, Moonee Ponds, Ascot Vale, and the CBD are all easily accessible. Essendon is a good base for exploring, even if the suburb itself is on the quieter side after dark.

The Commute

From Essendon to the CBD: 18 to 22 minutes by train to Flagstaff on the Craigieburn line. Trains run every 8 to 12 minutes during peak. Night Network services run hourly on Friday and Saturday nights. The commute is reliable and consistent — not the fastest in Melbourne, but good enough that it rarely factors as a complaint.

CityLink access is a bonus if you drive for work or travel frequently — the airport is about 15 minutes by car from Essendon.

Is Essendon Worth It for Young Professionals?

Essendon works for young professionals who value a quiet, well-serviced suburb with solid transport over a packed social calendar. The food scene is genuinely good (especially Italian), the train is reliable, and the rent is fair for what you get. It is a practical choice rather than an exciting one — and for many people in their late 20s and 30s, that is exactly what they want.

It is not for you if you need bars open past midnight on your doorstep or a packed weekend social scene. But if your Friday nights alternate between a glass of wine at a Rose Street bar and the train to the CBD, Essendon works well.

FAQ

Is Essendon good for young professionals? Yes, if you prioritise a reliable commute and quiet lifestyle over nightlife. The 18 to 22 minute train to the CBD, strong food scene, and one-bedroom rents around $350 per week make it a practical choice.

How much is rent in Essendon? Median one-bedroom rent is around $350 per week as of 2026. Share house rooms range from $195 to $240 per week.

What is the nightlife like in Essendon? Quiet. Wine bars and pubs on Rose Street for weeknight drinks, and the pubs come alive on footy match days. For late-night options, the CBD is 20 minutes by train.

How far is Essendon from the CBD? 10km northwest. About 18 to 22 minutes by train on the Craigieburn line.
