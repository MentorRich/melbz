23 Best Restaurants in Fitzroy You Need to Try in 2026

Priya Sandhu
13 Mar 2026 · 19 min read
Fitzroy

Here are the 23 best restaurants in Fitzroy for 2026, from hatted fine dining to hidden neighbourhood gems. This guide covers every corner of Melbourne’s most diverse food suburb, with venues across Brunswick, Gertrude, and Smith Streets. Whether you’re after Andrew McConnell’s legendary dry-aged beef, authentic Filipino breakfast, or charred miso ramen, Fitzroy delivers.

What’s inside:

  • Fine dining: Cutler, Marion, Gaea, Carnation Canteen
  • Neighbourhood essentials: Poodle, Norma, Alta Trattoria, Bar Liberty
  • International flavours: Añada (Spanish), Toddy Shop (Indian), Masti (Punjabi), Palay (Filipino)
  • Casual classics: Belles Hot Chicken, Vegie Bar, Mario’s, Rice Paper Scissors
  • Specialty spots: Ramen Ako’s, Gogyo (ramen), Deep End Pizza, Tamura Sake Bar

Forget what you think you know about Fitzroy. Yes, it’s still got that artsy edge that made it famous, but the food scene here has evolved into something extraordinary. This isn’t your mate’s share house suburb anymore — it’s Melbourne’s most democratic dining destination, where a $9 banh mi from a hole-in-the-wall shares the same street as hatted restaurants charging $240 for dry-aged beef.

Walking down Gertrude Street past Andrew McConnell’s empire, you’ll catch aromas of natural wine and fire-grilled octopus. Turn the corner to Brunswick Street, and it’s ramen steam mixing with Nashville fried chicken smoke. Smith Street brings you Sri Lankan spices and French bistro charm. This is what makes Fitzroy special — it’s never tried to be anything other than itself, and that authenticity has created Melbourne’s most diverse culinary ecosystem, earning it top marks on our Suburb Vibe Score.

The beauty of eating in Fitzroy is the choice. You can blow your monthly food budget on a degustation at Cutler, then walk 200 metres for late-night dumplings at a fraction of the price. Both experiences will be exceptional, because in Fitzroy, quality isn’t about price point — it’s about passion. From the teenage line cooks at the ramen joints to the sommeliers curating natural wine lists, everyone here takes food seriously. For more inner north dining, pair this with our Carlton cafe guide, or explore all our Fitzroy guides.

What’s changed in 2026? The suburb has doubled down on what it does best — authentic neighbourhood dining that happens to be world-class. New openings like Palay are bringing Filipino flavours to Greeves Street, while institutions like Marion continue to set the standard for wine bar excellence. The result is a food scene that feels both cutting-edge and comfortably familiar, where innovation meets tradition over shared plates and natural wine. This guide is part of our comprehensive Melbourne food guide, covering the city’s best dining across every suburb.

1. Cutler

The vibe: Andrew McConnell’s flagship fine diner where Gertrude Street goes upscale without losing its soul.

Fifteen years on, Cutler remains the gold standard for Melbourne fine dining, housed in that stunning converted metalworks factory at 55-57 Gertrude Street. The 2026 menu still centres around that legendary $240 dry-aged rib-eye, but McConnell’s seasonal approach means every visit feels fresh. The autumn vegetable entree with golden beetroot, red radish, fig, walnut and goat’s cheese showcases why this place earned its hats — it’s produce-driven cooking at its most elegant, in a space that feels distinctly Fitzroy rather than trying to cosplay as some European establishment.

Order this: Dry-aged rib-eye (1.2kg) ($240)
Also try: Autumn vegetable entree with golden beetroot ($34)
Address: 55-57 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Wednesday to Saturday from 5:30pm, Sunday from 12pm
Price range: $$$ ($160+ per person)
Book: OpenTable via Seven Rooms
Insider tip: Sunday lunch is legendary — book weeks ahead and pace yourself for the best long lunch in Melbourne.

2. Marion Wine Bar

The vibe: The wine bar every other wine bar wishes it could be.

Sitting next door to Cutler, Marion might be the most impressive wine list in Fitzroy, which is saying something in a suburb full of serious grape lovers. The constantly changing Euro-inspired menu matches the wine program’s ambition — think mussels with ’nduja, fried bread and aioli, or that silky chicken liver parfait with madeira that regulars rave about. The space spans two shopfronts, and whether you’re at the bar or a proper table, the vibe is neighbourhood wine bar elevated to art form.

Order this: Chicken liver parfait with madeira and cornichons ($28)
Also try: Mussels with ’nduja and fried bread ($32)
Address: 51-53 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday from 5pm, Saturday-Sunday from 8am
Price range: $$ ($60-80 per person)
Book: Seven Rooms
Insider tip: The sommelier’s selection changes daily — trust their recommendations for the best natural wine discoveries.

3. Poodle Bar & Bistro

The vibe: Euro bistro meets Melbourne swagger in the most Instagram-worthy dining room in Fitzroy.

Poodle has become a Fitzroy institution in record time, and for good reason — Josh Fry’s cooking combines French technique with Italian soul and just enough Australian irreverence to feel like home. The space feels like a grand European bistro that accidentally landed on Gertrude Street, with art-deco touches and enough personality to fill both shopfronts. Chef’s Selection menu for groups of 6-12 takes the stress out of ordering and lets you experience the full range of Fry’s talents.

Order this: Prawn vol-au-vent ($18)
Also try: Salt and vinegar onion rings ($12)
Address: 81-83 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Lunch Saturday & Sunday, dinner Tuesday-Sunday
Price range: $$ ($70-90 per person)
Book: Seven Rooms
Insider tip: The vintage rib-eye is worth the splurge, especially when paired with one of their endless martinis.

4. Naked for Satan

The vibe: Rooftop pintxos bar with Melbourne’s best view and enough spiced vodka to make you forget about rent.

The original Brunswick Street institution has evolved beyond its pintxos roots into something bigger and better — a multi-level playground where the rooftop bar delivers killer city views alongside killer cocktails. The bar food menu might have moved away from traditional Basque skewers, but the international selection hits hard. Oysters with kimchi or cava jelly, sliders, and that famous spiced vodka selection keep the party going from lunch until late.

Order this: Oysters with kimchi ($4 each)
Also try: Spiced vodka flight ($28)
Address: 285 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Daily from 12pm (until midnight Sunday-Thursday, 1am Friday-Saturday)
Price range: $ ($40-60 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: Hit the rooftop during sunset for the full Melbourne panorama — the city lights from up here are unbeatable.

5. Belles Hot Chicken

The vibe: Nashville heat meets Gertrude Street cool.

Since 2014, Belles has been perfecting the art of Nashville-style hot chicken, and by 2026, they’ve got it down to a science. The spice levels range from gentle to face-melting, and the crispy coating stays crunchy even when drenched in that signature hot oil. Weekend brunch brings chicken and waffles to Fitzroy, one of the few places in Melbourne doing it properly. The boozy slushies and craft beer selection help cool the fire.

Order this: Hot chicken sandwich with pickles ($22)
Also try: Mac and cheese side ($12)
Address: 150 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Monday-Sunday 11:30am-9pm (until 9:30pm Friday-Saturday)
Price range: $ ($25-35 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: Start with “Hot” spice level on your first visit — their “Fire” and “XXX” will genuinely test your limits.

6. Vegie Bar

The vibe: The vegetarian institution that proved plant-based food could be hearty, satisfying, and completely unpretentious.

Nearly three decades on Brunswick Street, Vegie Bar remains a Fitzroy essential with its 50+ dish menu and portions that’ll keep you full until next week. This isn’t precious vegetarian food — it’s comfort eating that happens to be meat-free, with influences from around the world. The atmosphere is pure Brunswick Street bohemia: communal tables, eclectic décor, and the kind of warmth that makes solo diners feel like regulars. Expect to queue, especially on weekends.

Order this: Moroccan tagine with preserved lemons ($26)
Also try: Vegie Bar nachos with everything ($24)
Address: 380 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Daily 6pm-10pm
Price range: $ ($25-35 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: The daily specials board often features the most interesting dishes — ask your server what’s cooking.

7. Mario’s

The vibe: Time-warp Italian that’s been feeding Fitzroy since 1986.

Mario’s feels like stepping into old Melbourne — crisp white tablecloths, neon signage, and that famous window bar overlooking Brunswick Street. This is simple Italian done right: handmade pasta, classic garlic bread, and coffee that tastes like it came straight from a Roman bar. The menu hasn’t changed much in decades because it doesn’t need to. Sometimes perfection is aglio e olio and a glass of house wine while watching Fitzroy walk past the window.

Order this: Spaghetti aglio e olio ($18)
Also try: Garlic bread (obligatory) ($8)
Address: 303 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Daily 7am-late
Price range: $ ($25-40 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: Grab a window seat for the full Mario’s experience — people-watching on Brunswick Street is part of the entertainment.

8. Rice Paper Scissors

The vibe: Asian-fusion energy that never stops buzzing.

This Brunswick Street staple proves that sometimes the best restaurants are the ones that never slow down. The share-style menu hits every Southeast Asian craving with authority — five-spice lamb ribs that fall off the bone, jungle curry that’ll clear your sinuses, and som tum that balances sweet, sour, and spicy perfectly. The $10 happy hour cocktails and banquet options make it perfect for groups who want to eat well without breaking the bank.

Order this: Five-spice crispy lamb ribs ($28)
Also try: Spicy jungle curry with vegetables ($26)
Address: 307 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday 5:30pm-late
Price range: $$ ($50-70 per person)
Book: OpenTable
Insider tip: The banquet menu takes the guesswork out of ordering and ensures you get the full flavour journey.

9. Toddy Shop

The vibe: South Indian comfort food that feels like eating in someone’s favourite auntie’s kitchen.

Mischa Tropp has created something special in this intimate 20-seater — authentic Keralan flavours served with the kind of hospitality that makes first-timers feel like family. The mix of wet and dry dishes creates perfect balance: spicy fish nadan alongside chicken pepper fry, with flaky parota bread to scoop everything up. The outdoor parklet adds precious extra seats, but the cosy interior is where the magic really happens.

Order this: Traditional spicy fish nadan ($32)
Also try: Chicken pepper fry ($28)
Address: 191A Smith Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday from 5:30pm
Price range: $$ ($55-75 per person)
Book: Seven Rooms
Insider tip: Order family-style with a mix of wet and dry dishes — the staff will guide you to the perfect combination.

10. Añada

The vibe: Andalusian tapas bar that’s been getting Southern Spanish flavours right since 2008.

While other Spanish restaurants chase trends, Añada has quietly perfected the art of Andalusian tapas on Gertrude Street. The menu draws from peasant food traditions of Southern Spain and the Muslim Mediterranean, creating dishes that feel both authentic and distinctly Melbourne. The house-made paella is worth the wait, and the charcuterie selection showcases Spain’s best preserved meats alongside local additions.

Order this: House-made paella for two ($65)
Also try: Andalusian charcuterie selection ($24)
Address: 197 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Saturday from 5:30pm, Sunday lunch
Price range: $$ ($60-80 per person)
Book: OpenTable
Insider tip: The sherries are phenomenal — let your server guide you through a proper Spanish drinking experience.

11. Rocco’s Bologna Discoteca

The vibe: Italo-disco meets Italo-American comfort food in the most fun dining room in Fitzroy.

Rocco’s brings party vibes to Gertrude Street with a menu that celebrates Italian-American classics executed with Melbourne precision. That legendary meatball sub — pork and veal polpette drowning in sauce and stuffed into a proper roll — is worth the trip alone. The rigatoni alla vodka and focaccia with hot honey prove that sometimes the best Italian food happens outside Italy.

Order this: The meatball sub ($28)
Also try: Rigatoni alla vodka ($26)
Address: 15 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday from 5:30pm
Price range: $$ ($55-75 per person)
Book: Seven Rooms
Insider tip: The date night special includes the meatball sub to share plus wine — perfect for casual romance.

12. Bar Liberty

The vibe: Natural wine pioneer that balances serious wine knowledge with genuine warmth.

Bar Liberty helped define Fitzroy’s natural wine scene, and their list remains one of Melbourne’s most thoughtfully curated selections. The produce-driven food matches the wine program’s quality — seasonal vegetable plates, perfectly grilled spatchcock, and that signature sourdough flatbread that regulars order every visit. The upstairs private dining room and rear courtyard bar Drinkwell expand the options without losing the intimate neighbourhood feel.

Order this: Seasonal vegetable plate (market price)
Also try: Sourdough flatbread with seasonal toppings ($18)
Address: 234 Johnston Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Monday-Saturday from 5pm, Sunday from 12pm
Price range: $$ ($65-85 per person)
Book: Walk-in preferred
Insider tip: Sunday lunch in the courtyard is peak Melbourne dining — arrive early for the best tables.

13. Palay

The vibe: Bright blue door welcome to authentic Filipino flavours done with Melbourne finesse.

One of 2026’s standout new openings, Palay brings Filipino comfort food to Greeves Street with the kind of execution that suggests this place will become a neighbourhood essential. The silog breakfasts — garlic fried rice, fried egg, and your choice of protein — set the standard for Filipino brunch in Melbourne. Evening tapas showcase the full range of Filipino flavours, from sizzling sisig to sweet maja blanca dessert.

Order this: Longganisa silog with pickled papaya ($22)
Also try: Pork sisig with 60-degree egg ($26)
Address: 135 Greeves Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday 8am-3pm, Thursday-Saturday 5pm-late
Price range: $ ($35-55 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: The mango coffee float is a revelation — Filipino café culture meets Melbourne coffee obsession.

14. Norma

The vibe: French Mediterranean bistro where Benjamin Tremblet’s grandmothers’ recipes meet Melbourne ingredients.

Smith Street’s newest French addition feels like it’s been there forever, thanks to Tremblet’s approach of honouring tradition while embracing local produce. The menu is designed for sharing, which makes perfect sense when dishes like the duck leg confit and Bourguignon demand to be experienced communally. Even the wine list includes bottles from the family vineyard near Bordeaux — this is personal cooking served with genuine hospitality.

Order this: Confit duck leg with parsnip purée ($38)
Also try: Bourguignon with root vegetables ($34)
Address: 197 Smith Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Wednesday-Sunday from 6pm
Price range: $$ ($70-90 per person)
Book: Restaurant website
Insider tip: The family vineyard wines are only available here — ask about current releases from Bordeaux.

15. Ramen Ako’s

The vibe: Intimate ramen counter where less is definitely more.

With just 31 seats and three ramen options, Ako’s proves that focus beats variety every time. Named for owner Sho Iijima’s mother, this Brunswick Street spot honours her chicken soup tradition with two exceptional broths: light chintan or rich paitan, plus a standout vegan mushroom option. The simplicity extends to everything — gyoza, karaage, beer, and sake complete the offering without unnecessary complexity.

Order this: Chicken ramen with paitan (rich) broth ($18)
Also try: Gyoza (6 pieces) ($12)
Address: 368 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday 6pm-9pm (100 bowls daily maximum)
Price range: $ ($25-35 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: Arrive early — once they’ve served 100 bowls, they close for the night.

16. Punters Club

The vibe: Classic Aussie pub reborn with nostalgic charm and elevated pub grub.

The refurbished Punters Club captures everything great about Australian pub culture — pool table, jukebox, XXXX on tap — while elevating the food to match 2026 expectations. Dishes like tuna tartare with Smith’s salt and vinegar chips or South Melbourne dim sum prove that pub food can be both comforting and clever. The Ploughman’s plate and steak-egg-and-chips keep traditionalists happy.

Order this: Tuna tartare with Smith’s chips ($24)
Also try: Ploughman’s plate with cheddar croquettes ($28)
Address: 376 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Daily from 4pm
Price range: $ ($40-60 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: The pool room fills up fast on weekends — grab a table early if you want to play.

17. Alta Trattoria

The vibe: Northern Italian regional cooking that transports you straight to Piedmont.

Alta’s dedication to Piedmont cuisine sets it apart in Melbourne’s crowded Italian scene. The tajarin with rabbit ragù bianco represents everything this kitchen does well — traditional techniques applied to exceptional ingredients with enough restraint to let flavours speak for themselves. The Vitello Tonnato and gnocchi with Tuscan sausage prove the kitchen’s range across Northern Italian regions.

Order this: Tajarin pasta with rabbit ragù bianco ($28)
Also try: Vitello Tonnato with fried capers ($26)
Address: 274 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Saturday from 6pm
Price range: $$ ($60-80 per person)
Book: Facebook page
Insider tip: The Italian wine list is exceptional — ask about Piedmont producers you won’t find elsewhere.

18. Gaea

The vibe: 16-seat fine dining laboratory where Mo Zhou turns seasonal ingredients into edible art.

This monthly-changing degustation restaurant represents the cutting edge of Melbourne dining, where fermentation, wild ingredients, and handmade ceramics create experiences that linger long after the last course. Sommelier Charles Duan’s non-alcoholic pairings are as thoughtful as the wine selection, incorporating everything from pomelo shrub to pu’er tea sodas. Book well in advance.

Order this: Chef’s monthly degustation menu ($165)
Also try: Non-alcoholic pairing with fermented drinks ($85)
Address: 166 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Thursday-Saturday from 6pm
Price range: $$$ ($250+ per person with drinks)
Book: Restaurant website
Insider tip: The fermented carrot juice with lamb fat caramel sounds weird but tastes extraordinary — trust the process.

19. Carnation Canteen

The vibe: Neighbourhood fine dining that earned cult status through exceptional Mediterranean cooking.

Audrey Shaw’s 20-seater took Melbourne by storm with its organic, biodynamic approach to Mediterranean cuisine. The weekly changing menu keeps regulars coming back, never knowing exactly what they’ll find but trusting in Shaw’s ability to transform simple ingredients into something transcendent. Those Cape Moreton prawns with bisque represent the kind of precise cooking that made reservations so hard to secure.

Order this: Whatever features the best seasonal produce (menu changes weekly)
Also try: Heirloom tomatoes with buffalo ricotta ($24)
Address: 165 Gore Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Wednesday-Saturday from 6pm
Price range: $$$ ($120-150 per person)
Book: Restaurant website
Insider tip: Follow their Instagram for weekly menu updates — some dishes are worth planning visits around.

20. Masti

The vibe: Vibrant Indian restaurant where Punjabi tradition meets Melbourne innovation.

Manpreet Sekhon’s Masti proves that Indian food can be both deeply traditional and thoroughly modern, with dishes like 24-hour black dahl and honey chilli cauliflower showcasing the full spectrum of subcontinental flavours. The space is as bright and energetic as the food, with cocktails that incorporate Indian spices and cooking techniques. The dhaba-style goat curry alone justifies the visit.

Order this: 24-hour black dahl ($26)
Also try: Dhaba-style goat curry ($32)
Address: 354-356 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday from 5pm
Price range: $$ ($55-75 per person)
Book: OpenTable
Insider tip: The chai espresso martini bridges Melbourne coffee culture and Indian spice traditions perfectly.

21. Gogyo

The vibe: Serious ramen from the Ippudo family, specialising in that distinctive charred miso style.

Gogyo’s kogashi (charred) miso ramen stands out in Melbourne’s competitive ramen scene through pure technique — the smoky, umami-rich broth represents years of Japanese ramen development distilled into one perfect bowl. The fusion elements, like sukiyaki wagyu spring rolls, show they’re not afraid to play with tradition when it improves the eating experience.

Order this: Kogashi charred miso ramen ($22)
Also try: Sukiyaki wagyu spring rolls ($16)
Address: 413 Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Sunday from 6pm
Price range: $ ($35-50 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: The hojicha burnt cheesecake is an unexpected triumph — save room for dessert.

22. Deep End Pizza

The vibe: American pizza pilgrimage site where Chicago deep dish meets Melbourne appetite.

Deep End tackles regional American pizza styles with the kind of obsessive attention that makes pizza nerds weep with joy. The Chicago deep dish is properly overwhelming — cheese-loaded architecture that requires tactical eating. The thinner New York and New Haven styles offer respite for those who want to taste sauce and dough along with the molten cheese avalanche.

Order this: Chicago deep dish with Italian sausage (feeds 2-3) ($45)
Also try: Detroit pan pizza with pepperoni ($28)
Address: 412A Brunswick Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Wednesday-Sunday from 5pm
Price range: $ ($25-40 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: Order the birria collab special when available — Mexican meets American pizza culture in the best way.

23. Tamura Sake Bar

The vibe: Warm Japanese hospitality where vinyl spins and sake flows in equal measure.

Fumi Tamura’s intimate sake bar captures everything great about izakaya culture — the welcoming atmosphere, the perfect drinking snacks, and sake exploration guided by genuine expertise. The blackboard map helps navigate different regions and styles, while dishes like shimesaba (cured mackerel) and Japanese fried chicken provide the perfect accompaniment to serious sake drinking.

Order this: Shimesaba (cured mackerel) ($18)
Also try: Japanese fried chicken karaage ($16)
Address: 1/43 Gertrude Street, Fitzroy VIC 3065
Hours: Tuesday-Saturday from 6pm
Price range: $ ($45-65 per person)
Book: Walk-in only
Insider tip: Let Fumi guide your sake selection — her knowledge of producers and regions is encyclopaedic.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best street for restaurants in Fitzroy?

Brunswick Street, Gertrude Street, and Smith Street each offer distinct dining experiences. Brunswick Street is known for its eclectic mix of casual eateries and institutions like Vegie Bar and Mario’s. Gertrude Street delivers fine dining and wine bars like Cutler and Marion. Smith Street brings international flavours like Toddy Shop’s Keralan cuisine. Explore all our Fitzroy guides for more.

Is Fitzroy good for vegetarians?

Absolutely. Fitzroy has been a vegetarian and vegan hub for decades, with Vegie Bar leading the way since the 1990s. Many restaurants offer strong plant‑based options, and the suburb’s diverse culinary scene ensures vegetarians won’t miss out. Check our Melbourne food guide for more vegetarian‑friendly spots across the city.

Where should I go for a special occasion in Fitzroy?

For celebrations, head to Cutler for its hatted fine dining and dry‑aged rib‑eye, or Gaea for an avant‑garde degustation experience. Marion Wine Bar offers a more intimate but equally impressive setting with its curated natural wine list. For something different, Carnation Canteen’s ever‑changing Mediterranean menu delivers memorable dining. Pair your meal with a visit to one of Fitzroy’s best bars for after‑dinner drinks.

The Bottom Line

Fitzroy’s restaurant scene in 2026 represents Melbourne dining at its most diverse and democratic. Whether you’re after a $15 bowl of ramen or a $200 fine dining experience, the quality bar remains consistently high across every price point. The suburb’s strength lies in its refusal to choose between authenticity and innovation — you’ll find traditional Italian cooking at Mario’s, experimental fermentation at Gaea, and everything in between executed with the kind of passion that makes Melbourne’s food scene world-renowned.

The best strategy for eating in Fitzroy? Start with the institutions to understand the foundation, then explore the newcomers to see where the scene is heading. Book where you can, but leave room for spontaneity — some of the best meals happen when you follow your nose down Brunswick Street and see what catches your attention.

Most importantly, eat with friends. Fitzroy restaurants are built for sharing — whether it’s pintxos at Naked for Satan, family-style curries at Toddy Shop, or splitting that massive deep dish pizza at Deep End. The suburb’s community spirit extends to its dining culture, where solo diners are welcomed but group meals are celebrated.
