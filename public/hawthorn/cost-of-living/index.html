Cost of Living in Hawthorn 2026: The Real Numbers

Updated 16 March 2026 | Marcus Cole reporting

Hawthorn sits in that sweet spot that makes property editors simultaneously impressed and exhausted. It’s leafy. It’s close to the city. It’s got Swinburne Uni, Glenferrie Road’s strip of shops, and enough gastro pubs to make you forget you’re paying $600 a week in rent. But what does it actually cost to live here? Not the glossy brochure version — the real, spreadsheet-on-the-fridge version?

I crunched the numbers so you don’t have to. And I’ll be honest: some of them stung.

Rent: The Elephant in the Leafy Room

Let’s start with the number that dictates everything else.

As of March 2026, the median rent in Hawthorn sits at:

  • 1-bedroom apartment: $420–$480/week
  • 2-bedroom apartment: $550–$620/week
  • 3-bedroom house: $750–$900/week
  • Studio (shared building): $320–$380/week

That 1-bed at $450/week? That’s $23,400 a year before you’ve bought a single grocery. A couple splitting a 2-bed at $580/week each puts away $30,160 annually. Not catastrophic by inner-Melbourne standards — and significantly cheaper than neighbouring Kew, where 3-bed houses regularly breach $1,000/week — but not exactly budget living either.

The sweet spot in Hawthorn is the older 1970s block apartments along Burwood Road or near the train line. They lack the gym and rooftop of the new builds on Glenferrie Road, but they also lack the $50/week price premium.

Marcus’s rule: If you’re spending more than 30% of your after-tax income on rent, you’re stretching. On a $70K salary in Hawthorn, you can just about manage a 1-bed. Below $65K and you’re looking at a studio or a flatmate.

Groceries: What a Weekly Shop Actually Looks Like

Hawthorn isn’t cheap for groceries, but it’s not South Yarra expensive either. Here’s what a realistic weekly shop for one person looks like:

ItemCost
Weekly shop (Coles/Woolworths)$85–$120
Weekly shop (Aldi, if you trek to Camberwell)$60–$85
Loaf of sourdough (Breadshop on Glenferrie)$7.50
Litre of milk$2.80–$3.50
Dozen eggs (free range)$6.50–$8.00
Chicken breast (1kg)$12–$14

The big variable is whether you shop at the local IGA on Glenferrie Road (convenient but pricey) or make the trip to Aldi in Camberwell or the larger Coles in Hawthorn. Most locals I spoke to do a split — Aldi for staples, local for top-ups and fresh bread.

One thing worth noting: Hawthorn’s lack of a big weekend market (no Queen Vic or Prahran Market nearby) means you’re stuck with supermarket pricing for most fresh produce. The occasional trip to the farmers’ market at Hawthorn Bowls Club on the first Sunday of the month saves a bit if you time it right.

Monthly grocery estimate: $340–$480 for one person.

Transport: The Myki Reality

Hawthorn is well-served for public transport, which helps keep costs down if you’re not driving.

  • Myki zone 1+2 pass (weekly): $53.00
  • Myki zone 1+2 pass (monthly): $176.80
  • Myki zone 1+2 pass (yearly): $1,918.40
  • Daily cap (touch on/off): $10.60
  • Train to Flinders Street: 18–22 minutes from Hawthorn station
  • Tram 75 to CBD: 35–45 minutes depending on traffic along Toorak Road

Most Hawthorn locals take the train. The 75 tram exists but it’s slower and the Toorak Road traffic can be brutal during peak hour. The train from Hawthorn to the city is genuinely one of the better eastern line commutes — you’ll usually get a seat if you board at Hawthorn rather than waiting for Glenferrie.

If you’re driving, fuel is averaging around $1.95–$2.10/L in March 2026 at the servos along Burwood Road. Parking in the CBD is a different pain entirely — budget $25–$35/day for a car park, or $180–$220/month for a monthly pass if you commute daily.

Monthly transport estimate: $175 (Myki pass) or $350+ (car commute to CBD).

Dining Out: The Glenferrie Road Test

Hawthorn’s dining scene is more “solid neighbourhood spots” than “destination dining,” and that’s actually a plus for your wallet.

Here’s what you’re looking at:

  • Flat white at a local cafe: $4.60–$5.20
  • Big breakfast (2 eggs, bacon, toast, the works): $22–$27
  • Banana bread (with butter, because we’re not savages): $7–$9
  • Pub parma at a Hawthorn local: $22–$26
  • Mid-week dinner for two (casual, with a glass of wine): $80–$110
  • Fancy dinner for two (wine included): $140–$180
  • Cheap eats — dumplings or a bowl of pho: $14–$18

Glenferrie Road is your main drag and it’s got a decent range. The pubs (The hatters, The Alchemist) do solid $20 lunch specials. The cafe scene is competitive enough that quality stays high — when your nearest neighbours are Kew and Camberwell, you can’t afford to serve bad coffee.

For something a bit different, cross over to the Richmond side along Swan Street where the Vietnamese and Thai options are cheaper and arguably better. A $14 bowl of pho in Richmond versus a $24 pad thai in Hawthorn is a comparison worth making when your budget’s tight.

Monthly dining estimate (moderate — 3 meals out per week): $350–$500.

Utilities: The Quiet Drain

Melbourne utilities in 2026 remain annoyingly expensive. Here’s the realistic monthly breakdown for a 1-bedroom apartment in Hawthorn:

  • Electricity: $120–$160/month (higher in winter with reverse-cycle heating)
  • Gas: $40–$70/month (many newer builds are all-electric now)
  • Internet (NBN 50/20): $65–$80/month
  • Water (usage + supply): $25–$40/month
  • Mobile phone plan: $40–$65/month

Total monthly utilities: $290–$415.

The big swing factor is winter. If you’re in an older apartment with poor insulation and rely on gas heating, your July bill can easily double. I’ve seen 1-bed flats on Burwood Road hit $250 in electricity during a cold snap. If you’re apartment hunting, ask about the energy rating and whether the building has gas or is all-electric — it makes a genuine difference to your winter bills.

Pro tip: most locals in Hawthorn have ditched Foxtel. Streaming services (Netflix, Stan, Binge) run $15–$25/month each, and between them you’ve got enough content to survive a Melbourne winter without leaving the couch.

Gym Memberships: Staying Active in Hawthorn

  • Anytime Fitness Hawthorn: ~$65/week (24/7 access, decent equipment)
  • F45 Hawthorn: ~$60/week (group classes, peak pricing)
  • Swinburne Uni gym (if eligible): ~$25/week
  • Budget option — run the Yarra Trail: Free (and honestly, one of Melbourne’s best running paths)
  • Casual class pass (boutique studios): $25–$35 per class

The Yarra Trail running path from Hawthorn Bridge up towards Burnley and beyond is genuinely excellent. If you’re on a tight budget, that plus a set of resistance bands at home is a perfectly functional fitness routine. But if you need the accountability of a membership, Hawthorn’s gym options are average-priced for inner Melbourne — you won’t find the $10/week budget gyms here, but you also won’t find the $80/week boutique rip-offs.

Monthly gym estimate: $65–$260 depending on your choice.

Coffee: A Non-Negotiable Budget Item

Let’s not pretend you’re going to stop buying coffee because you moved to a suburb with excellent cafes.

  • Flat white (standard): $4.60–$5.20
  • Long black: $4.40–$5.00
  • Batch filter: $5.00–$5.50
  • Oat milk surcharge: usually included now, some places still charge $0.60–$0.80 extra

At one coffee a day, five days a week, you’re looking at roughly $100–$115/month. That’s $1,200–$1,380 a year. It’s not nothing. But in Hawthorn, where the cafes along Glenferrie Road are genuinely good, it’s one of those expenses that pays for itself in sanity.

The locals’ picks rotate, but as of early 2026, the Glenferrie Road strip between Burwood Road and John Street has the strongest concentration. Order a flat white, sit in, and eavesdrop on the Swinburne students complaining about assignments — it’s peak Hawthorn.

Monthly coffee estimate: $100–$130.

Entertainment and Going Out

Hawthorn isn’t Melbourne’s nightlife epicentre, and that’s fine. Here’s what you’ll actually spend:

  • Cinema (Hoyts or similar): $20–$24 per ticket
  • Pub gig or live music: $15–$30 entry
  • Drinks at a pub (3 x schooners): $24–$30
  • Cocktail at a bar: $22–$28
  • AFL at the MCG (general admission): $30–$50 depending on the game
  • Streaming subscriptions (Netflix + Stan + one other): $45–$65/month

The real entertainment budget hack in Hawthorn is that you’re one train stop from Richmond and three from the CBD. You don’t need to spend Hawthorn pub prices when Swan Street is a 10-minute walk across the bridge. The Lido, the Bridge Hotel, and the Swan Street strip are cheaper and louder than anything in Hawthorn proper.

Monthly entertainment estimate: $150–$300 (moderate).

The midpoint lands around $3,800/month, or roughly $45,600 a year. After tax on a $75K salary, you’d take home about $58,600. That leaves $13,000 for savings, emergencies, and the stuff that actually makes life worth living — like weekend trips, new shoes, and the occasional moment of “wait, I can actually afford this.”

If you’re on $65K, the margin gets thin. Really thin. Hawthorn is absolutely livable on that salary, but you’d need flatmates or a cheaper studio to build any meaningful savings.

What We Skipped and Why

A few things you might expect in a cost-of-living breakdown that we left out:

Childcare: This varies wildly depending on your child’s age, the centre, and whether you qualify for subsidies. Hawthorn childcare runs $130–$180/day before subsidies. If you want this covered properly, get in touch and we’ll do a dedicated childcare costs piece.

Pet costs: Renting with a pet in Hawthorn is a whole separate article. Most rental listings specify “no pets,” and those that allow them often charge $20–$30/week extra. Vet bills, food, and boarding are variable. We’ll cover this in a dedicated pet-friendly suburb guide.

Private health insurance: Too individualised to price meaningfully. Basic hospital cover runs $110–$160/month for a single adult, but whether you need it (and whether the Medicare surcharge applies to your income) is a personal calculation.

Council rates: If you’re renting, this isn’t your bill. If you’re buying, Boroondara Council rates for Hawthorn properties typically run $1,800–$2,800/year depending on your property value. We’ll cover buying costs in a separate Hawthorn property guide.

Education (private school fees): Hawthorn has several well-known private schools. Fees range from $15,000 to $35,000+ per year. That’s a conversation for a different article entirely.

How Hawthorn Compares to Neighbours

Hawthorn sits in a competitive cluster of inner-eastern suburbs. Here’s how the numbers stack up:

  • Richmond — Cheaper rent ($380–$440 for a 1-bed), grittier vibe, better nightlife, noisier streets. Good if you want to save $50–$80/week on rent and don’t mind the trade-off. Read our full Richmond cost of living breakdown.

  • Kew — Pricier across the board. 3-bed houses start around $950/week and the dining scene is quieter. Better for families, worse for nightlife. See our Kew suburb guide.

  • Camberwell — Similar rents to Hawthorn but further from the CBD. The Camberwell market is a genuine draw, and the tram into the city is reliable if slow. Good if you value space over proximity. Full Camberwell cost of living guide here.

The honest take? Hawthorn offers the best balance of price, location, and lifestyle of the four. It’s not the cheapest (Richmond wins there) or the quietest (Kew) or the most spacious (Camberwell), but it’s the one where most people find the best equilibrium.

The Bottom Line

Hawthorn in 2026 is a solidly mid-to-upper-inner-city suburb in terms of cost. It won’t bankrupt you like South Yarra or Toorak, but it won’t let you coast like Footscray or Braybrook. You need a decent income to live here comfortably — ideally $75K+ for a single person, or a combined household income north of $130K for a couple who wants to save meaningfully.

What you get for that money is a suburb with character, good transport, proper coffee, a surprisingly decent pub scene, and the quiet satisfaction of living somewhere that feels like it was designed for people who actually live there, not just visit.

Hawthorn doesn’t try to impress you. That’s what makes it worth the money.
