Cost of Living in Kensington 2026: The Real Numbers

Updated 16 March 2026 | Marcus Cole reporting

Kensington is one of those suburbs where everyone has an opinion and nobody checks the maths. It’s 4km from the CBD, wedged between Flemington’s wide streets and North Melbourne’s grit, and it carries a reputation as the “affordable inner-north option” — a label that’s been getting harder to justify every quarter.

So let’s do what nobody does and put actual numbers on it. Not vibes. Not “it depends.” Real 2026 AUD figures for what it costs to actually live in Kensington, postcode 3031, right now.

I’ve pulled data from Numbeo, Domain, REIV, Myki fare schedules, and priced out groceries at the shops I know locals actually use — not the organic co-op with $14 eggs, but the places where Kensington residents do their weekly haul. Let’s get into it.

Rent: The Big One

Kensington’s rental market sits in an awkward middle ground. It’s not as dear as neighbouring North Melbourne, where 1-bedroom apartments near Errol Street regularly crack $2,200/month. But it’s no longer the bargain it was five years ago either.

Current median rents (March 2026):

Property TypeWeeklyMonthly
1-bedroom unit$480–$520$2,080–$2,252
2-bedroom unit$550–$600$2,383–$2,600
3-bedroom house$720–$800$3,120–$3,467
Studio/room share$320–$400$1,387–$1,733

For context, the broader Melbourne median for units is sitting around $580/week, but that’s skewed by inner-east prices. Kensington’s unit median hovers closer to $500/week — which puts it slightly below average for the inner ring but well above what most people consider “affordable.”

The unit rental yield for investors in the area sits at 5.17%, which tells you landlords are still making this work. For tenants, it means a 2-bedroom in a decent block on Macaulay Road or Epsom Road runs you about $2,400/month. That’s roughly $29,000 a year before you’ve paid a single bill.

The salary check: To keep rent under 30% of your take-home pay on a 1-bed ($500/week), you’d need a net monthly income of around $6,667 — which translates to a gross salary north of $105,000. If you’re on the average Melbourne net salary of about $6,123/month, you’re spending closer to 33–36% on rent alone. Doable. Comfortable? Not really.

Groceries: Where Kensington Saves You Money

Here’s where the suburb actually gives you a win. Kensington has a solid mix of grocery options that keep your weekly shop manageable if you’re smart about it.

The Coles on Epsom Road is the workhorse for most locals. Victoria Market is a 10-minute ride or a 20-minute walk, and for produce it blows any supermarket away on price.

Typical weekly grocery spend for a single person:

ItemWeekly Cost
Basics (bread, milk, eggs, rice, pasta)$25–$35
Fruit & vegetables$20–$30
Meat & protein$25–$40
Pantry staples & snacks$15–$25
Total$85–$130

A household of two should expect $180–$220/week, which tracks with the Victorian average of $212/week. The key is Victoria Market — a kilo of tomatoes that costs $6.63 at Coles goes for $3–$4 at the market if you pick the right stall. Same with bananas, onions, and basically anything that grows.

Local tip: The Asian grocer on Racecourse Road does rice, noodles, sauces, and frozen goods at prices that’ll save you 30–40% compared to the supermarket. If you’re cooking with any Asian pantry staples, this is not optional — it’s essential.

Transport: The Myki Reality

Kensington is genuinely well-served for public transport, which is lucky because driving here is a special kind of punishment — the parking situation near the station is what I’d politely call “character-building.”

Myki costs (as of 1 January 2026):

  • Zone 1+2 daily cap: $11.40
  • Weekend/public holiday daily cap: $8.00
  • Weekly cap: $57.00
  • Monthly estimate: $200–$220

The 57 tram runs through Kensington along Epsom Road and Macaulay Road, connecting you to the CBD in about 20 minutes on a good day. The Flemington Bridge train station sits right on the border and gets you to Southern Cross in under 15 minutes.

If you’re relying solely on public transport, budget $200–$220/month. If you drive to work and park in the CBD, you’re looking at $25–$35/day for parking plus fuel at about $1.85/litre. A car owner in Melbourne spends, on average, about $27,800/year on transport all-up — that includes rego, insurance, fuel, and tolls. CityLink tolls alone can hit $12 for a single trip, so if your commute involves the tollway, factor that in hard.

The cycling option: Kensington to the CBD is flat, about 5km, and separated bike lanes exist on most of the route along Epsom Road and Moonee Ponds Trail. A decent commuter bike costs $500–$800 upfront and saves you $200+/month in Myki fares. It’s the best financial decision most Kensington residents could make, and yet almost nobody does it.

Dining Out: What a Meal Actually Costs

Kensington’s dining scene is modest compared to neighbouring Footscray (which has a food density that punches well above its weight for Vietnamese, Ethiopian, and modern Australian). But there are solid options and the prices reflect the postcode.

What you’ll pay:

  • Cheap eat (bowl of pho, kebab, slice): $14–$18
  • Average restaurant meal: $22–$30
  • Mid-range restaurant (2 people, 3 courses, no drinks): $100–$140
  • Fast food combo: $15–$17
  • Pint of craft beer: $12–$14
  • Glass of wine (mid-range): $12–$16

The smart money in Kensington is walking the 10 minutes to Flemington for Friday night dinner — you get the best of the Vietnamese strip on Racecourse Road without the Richmond traffic. A bowl of pho at one of the spots near the Flemington bridge runs about $16 and will keep you full until Sunday. Footscray’s Hopkins Street is similarly close and arguably better for a food crawl — the Ethiopian restaurants there remain criminally undervalued.

Monthly dining budget (single person, eating out 3–4 times/week):

  • Budget-conscious: $350–$450
  • Moderate: $500–$700
  • “I have no self-control”: $800+

Utilities: The Boring-but-Necessary Section

Nobody moves to Kensington for the electricity provider, but you still need to pay them.

Monthly utility costs for a 1-bedroom apartment:

BillMonthly Cost
Electricity$100–$130
Gas (if applicable)$60–$80
Water$65–$80
Internet (NBN Unlimited)$70–$80
Mobile phone plan$25–$50
Total$320–$420

Numbeo puts basic utilities for an 85m2 apartment at $309/month on average, which aligns with what I’m seeing. The biggest variable is electricity — summer air-con and winter heating can swing your bill by $50–$80/month depending on the apartment’s insulation quality, which in Kensington’s aging apartment stock is often… let’s say “optimistic.”

Split bills with a housemate and your share drops to $160–$210/month, which is genuinely manageable. This is the strongest argument for sharing in the inner north — the per-person savings on utilities are immediate and significant.

Gym, Coffee & Entertainment: The Lifestyle Costs

Gym

Kensington doesn’t have a huge gym scene. Most locals either use Anytime Fitness (about $65–$75/month for 24/7 access), join a larger chain further afield, or run along the Maribyrnong River trail — which is free and genuinely one of the best running paths in Melbourne.

  • Budget gym membership: $45–$65/month
  • Mid-range (F45, boutique studios): $60–$100/month
  • Premium/luxury gym: $100–$150/month

Coffee

The flat white is a non-negotiable life expense in this city. In Kensington, you’re looking at:

  • Regular flat white: $4.50–$5.20
  • Large/specialty: $5.50–$6.50
  • Batch filter: $4.00–$5.00

That’s $22.50–$32.50 a week if you’re a one-coffee-a-day person. Over a year, $1,170–$1,690 on coffee alone. Nobody wants to do that maths, but there it is.

Entertainment

  • Cinema ticket (Village or Hoyts): $22–$25
  • Live music/pubs (entry + 2 drinks): $40–$60
  • Streaming services (Netflix/Stan/etc.): $15–$25/month
  • AFL game (general admission): $30–$50
  • Night out (dinner + drinks + transport): $80–$150

The entertainment advantage of Kensington is proximity. You’re 15 minutes from the CBD’s live music scene, a short tram to the MCG, and a bike ride from the best of the inner west. You pay for that location in rent, but you save on Ubers.

Here’s the full picture for a single person in a 1-bedroom apartment, living reasonably but not frugally:

Groceries$380–$520
Transport (Myki)$200–$220
Utilities & internet$320–$420
Dining out (3–4x/week)$450–$600
Gym$55–$80
Coffee (daily)$100–$140

TOTAL$3,755–$4,480

To cover that comfortably (under 50% of take-home), you’d need a net monthly income of $7,500–$9,000 — or roughly $110,000–$135,000 gross annually. Below $90K, you’re making compromises. Below $75K, you’re probably sharing a 2-bed with someone and pocketing the $400/month difference.

For comparison, Footscray comes in roughly 10–15% cheaper across the board, particularly on rent and dining. North Melbourne runs 10–15% higher. Kensington genuinely sits in the middle — which is either “Goldilocks” or “the worst of both worlds” depending on your perspective.

What We Skipped and Why

Every cost-of-living article has gaps. Here’s what we consciously left out and why:

Childcare. At $3,300+/month for full-time preschool in Melbourne, it’s the elephant in every family budget. But it’s a separate, massive topic with its own subsidy structures (CCB, CCS). We’ll cover inner-north childcare in a dedicated article because it deserves more than a footnote.

Private health insurance. Wildly variable depending on age, coverage level, and whether you’re claiming the Medicare surcharge. Not useful as a Kensington-specific figure.

Pet costs. If you’re renting with a pet in Kensington, you already know the struggle. The financial part ($30–$50/week for a dog) is less painful than finding a landlord who’ll allow one.

Heating/cooling specifically. We included it in utilities, but the honest answer is: Melbourne apartments vary wildly. A north-facing 1-bed with double glazing will cost half to heat compared to a south-facing 1-bed in a 1970s block on Macaulay Road. We gave the range rather than a single number because a single number would be lying.

Alcohol beyond “dining out.” If you’re buying grog for home, a mid-range bottle of wine is $20, a six-pack of craft beer is $25–$30, and a bottle of spirits runs $45–$65. Your consumption patterns are your business.

The Verdict

Kensington in 2026 isn’t a bargain. The days of calling it “affordable inner-north” are mostly over — that baton has passed to areas further west, like Maidstone and Braybrook, or south to the pockets of South Melbourne that haven’t been swallowed by towers.

But it’s still sensibly priced compared to what sits directly around it. You’re paying less than North Melbourne, getting better transport access than Footscray (Flemington Bridge station is underrated), and living in a suburb that feels like it’s still figuring out what it wants to be — which means there’s genuine community energy here, not just property speculation.

The maths works if you’re earning $90K+ as a single person, or if you’re sharing with a partner or housemate. Below that threshold, the inner west offers better value pound for pound. But if you’re anchored to the inner north for work, family, or just stubbornly love the Maribyrnong River trail at 6am, Kensington will take your money and give you a decent postcode in return.

Just don’t call it “up-and-coming” to a local. It’s been “coming” for 15 years and it’s not getting any cheaper.
