Weekend Guide: Melbourne CBD 2026 — What to Do Saturday & Sunday

Updated 16 March 2026 | Isabella Greco reporting

The CBD gets a bad rap from locals who write it off as tourist territory. Fair enough if your only frame of reference is Bourke Street Mall on a Saturday arvo. But Melbourne’s actual city centre — the laneways, the side streets, the floors above the floors — runs deeper than most Melburnians bother to explore. This guide is your excuse to stop scrolling and start walking.

Here’s how to spend a proper Saturday and Sunday in the CBD, built around real places you can actually get into, with addresses and times so you’re not guessing.

SATURDAY

Morning: Queen Victoria Market Before It Gets Chaotic

Get to Queen Victoria Market (Queen St, open 6am–3pm Saturdays) before 9am and you’ll have the produce halls nearly to yourself. The fruit and veg vendors along the western end are generous with samples, and the cheese stalls in the Deli Hall will let you taste half a wheel before you commit. Grab a bratwurst from the Bratwurst Shop & Caffè (Building 5, Deli Hall) — $10 for a classic, cash or card — and eat it standing up like a local.

If the market’s seasonal Night Market is running (Wednesday evenings, November through March), it’s a completely different beast: street food, live DJs, local designers. But for a Saturday morning, the old-school market energy is the draw.

Cost: Free entry. Budget $15–30 if you’re buying produce or brunch supplies.

Late Morning: NGV International or Ian Potter Centre

Two world-class galleries, zero dollars entry. Walk south along Swanston Street to St Kilda Road and you’ll hit the NGV International (180 St Kilda Rd). The permanent collection includes works by Monet, Picasso, and a strong contemporary Australian wing. The architecture alone — the Great Hall with its stained-glass ceiling — is worth the trip.

If you’d rather stay on the city side of the river, the Ian Potter Centre: NGV Australia sits at Fed Square (Flinders St) and covers Australian art from colonial times to now. Both are open 10am–5pm daily, and both are free for general admission. Special exhibitions usually cost $20–35.

Cost: Free (general). Special exhibitions $20–35.

⏰ This Weekend Only: The NGV’s summer blockbuster exhibition wraps up 30 March. If you haven’t seen it yet, Saturday is running out. Book tickets at ngv.vic.gov.au.

Afternoon: A Walk That Isn’t the Southbank Promenade

Skip Southbank (unless you’re specifically heading to Fitzroy via the Capital City Trail). Instead, do the Laneways Loop:

  1. Start at Flinders Street Station
  2. Cut through Degraves Street — coffee row, small bars, not much that’s new, but the energy is undeniable
  3. Pop out onto Centre Place (off Flinders Lane) — tighter, grungier, more interesting
  4. Walk east along Flinders Lane past the old garment district buildings (now galleries and design studios)
  5. Duck into Hosier Lane (between Flinders St and Swanston St) — the street art changes weekly, sometimes daily
  6. Continue to AC/DC Lane (off Flinders Lane, named literally) for a photo and a drink at Cherry Bar if it’s open

The whole loop is about 1.5 km and takes 30 minutes at a dawdle. Add time for gallery-hopping along the way. The Flinders Lane gallery strip between Exhibition and Russell streets has spaces like NGV Studio, Mailbox Art Space, and Finkelstein Painting — all free, all walk-in.

Cost: Free. Coffee along the way: $4.50–6.

Evening: Drinks and Dinner

For drinks: Walk up to Laneway Recording (238 Flinders Lane) — a cocktail bar hidden above a fake record store entrance. The cocktails sit around $22–26 and the bartenders actually know what they’re doing. Alternatively, Eau De Vie (16 Malthouse Lane, off Flinders Lane) does classic cocktails in a moody, dimly lit space that feels like it belongs in a 1940s film.

For dinner: Chin Chin (125 Flinders Lane) is still a reliable option if you book ahead (they take reservations now, which solves the old two-hour-wait problem). Thai-influenced menu, dishes $18–35, and the dumpling bar downstairs (Chin Chin’s sister spot, Baby) is worth a separate visit. If you want something quieter and less packed, Tipo 00 (37 Little Bourke St) does hand-rolled pasta that is genuinely some of the best in the city — the cacio e pepe is non-negotiable. Mains $24–36. Open until 10pm Saturday.

Cost: Budget $60–100 for drinks and dinner per person.

🏆 THE MOVE — Saturday Night Edition

The Move: Degraves Street coffee → NGV → Laneways Loop → Cocktails at Laneway Recording → Dinner at Tipo 00 → Walk home along the river

The Backup: If Tipo 00 is full, Sarti (342 Little Collins St) does Northern Italian with a wine list that punches well above its price.

The Splurge: Book Vue de Monde (Level 55, Rialto Building, 447 Collins St) — $250+ per person for degustation. Not an every-Saturday thing, but the view alone justifies the cost once.

SUNDAY

Morning: The Victoria Markets Brunch Crawl

Saturday market energy is different from Sunday, and if the Queen Victoria Market is closed (it’s shut Sundays except for the Winter Night Market season), pivot to the CBD’s brunch heavyweights.

Top picks:

  • Higher Ground (650 Little Bourke St) — Set inside a heritage power station building. The interior is industrial cathedral: soaring ceilings, exposed brick, and a mezzanine level. Order the smoked ocean trout with buckwheat pancakes ($26) or the mushroom and truffle toast ($24). Opens 8am. Expect a 20–30 minute wait before 9:30am on Sundays.

  • Projected Coffee (355 Little Bourke St) — Smaller, less hyped, excellent single-origin filter coffee ($5) and a rotating brunch menu. Good if you want to sit down without the theatre of Higher Ground.

  • Marvel Street (Ground Floor, 22 Bourke St) — A newer addition. Clean fit-out, strong egg-focused menu. The breakfast burrito ($19) is substantial enough to carry you to lunch.

Cost: $25–40 per person with coffee.

Late Morning/Afternoon: Explore Beyond the Grid

This is where most CBD weekend guides stop. They shouldn’t.

Option A: Walk across Kings Bridge to Southbank and the Arts Precinct. The walk from Flinders Street Station across the Princes Bridge takes about 10 minutes. On the other side, you’ve got the Arts Centre Melbourne (100 St Kilda Rd), the Australian Centre for the Moving Image (ACMI) at Fed Square, and the Royal Botanic Gardens (Birdwood Ave, free entry, open 7:30am–dusk). The Botanic Gardens are 38 hectares of gardens, lawns, and lakes — a proper Sunday afternoon decompress.

Option B: Cut through to Little Bourke Street’s Chinatown precinct. Between Swanston and Exhibition, Little Bourke has been Melbourne’s Chinese dining strip for over 150 years. Dumpling restaurants dominate: Shanghai Village (112 Little Bourke St) does soup dumplings for $8–12, and the turnover is so fast the tables are never sticky for long. Tim Ho Wan (206 Bourke St) — the Hong Kong dim sum chain — is reliable for cheap steamed and fried options, with most items $6–10.

Option C: Head up to Carlton or Fitzroy. Both are a 15-minute walk north from the CBD and worth the legs. Carlson brings Italian food and Lygon Street’s cafe culture. Fitzroy brings vintage shops, rooftop bars, and Gertrude Street’s design stores. If you’re already thinking about next weekend, our Fitzroy weekend guide covers it street by street.

Cost: Free to walk. Dumplings/dim sum: $15–25.

Afternoon: A Proper Long Lunch or a Slow Wander

Long lunch pick: Cumulus Inc. (45 Flinders Lane) does a shared-plate lunch that works perfectly for groups or solo diners who want to graze. The roasted lamb shoulder (market price, usually $38–45 for two people to share) is a Sunday staple. Wine by the glass starts at $14. Open until 3pm for lunch service, then transitions to a bar.

Slow wander pick: Walk south from Flinders Street along St Kilda Road, cross the Riverwalk toward the Yarra Trail, and follow the path toward Birrarung Marr — the terraced parkland behind Federation Square that most people walk past without noticing. In March, the grass is still green, the trees are full, and there are usually buskers playing somewhere between the railway bridge and the fairy fountain.

Cost: Long lunch $50–80 per person. The walk is free.

Evening: Wind Down

Sunday evening in the CBD is quieter than Saturday, and that’s the point.

  • Polly (113 Hardware St) — A rooftop bar above Hardware Lane with views over the city. Cocktails $20–24, open until 10pm Sunday. It’s above a restaurant called ChinChin — actually, separate venue, confusing name situation — just look for the Hardware Street entrance and follow the stairs up.

  • Rooftop Bar (Curtin House, 252 Swanston St, Level 7) — One of the OG rooftop spots in Melbourne. Cinema below, bar above. $18–22 cocktails, and the sunset view looking west over the CBD is hard to beat in March when the days are still long.

  • If you’d rather sit inside with a glass of wine and no noise, French Saloon (Hardware St) has a back dining room that’s calm, unhurried, and serves a short but sharp wine list. Expect $18–20 per glass, excellent French-Australian food.

Cost: $30–50 for drinks and a light bite.

What We Skipped and Why

Every CBD guide has its blind spots. Here’s what we left out and why:

The Melbourne Star / Observation Wheel: Closed permanently as of 2023. If you see it still listed in guides, that guide is outdated. The site is being redeveloped.

Crown Casino complex: Technically on the Southbank side of the river and technically open on weekends. But the dining options inside Crown are overpriced for what you get, the atmosphere is designed to keep you gambling rather than eating, and there are simply better options five minutes away on the actual Southbank promenade or in the CBD proper. We cover Southbank separately if you’re keen.

Lygon Street “Little Italy” block: We mentioned Carlton generally, but the specific strip of Lygon between Swanston and Faraday is a tourist corridor. The real action is on the side streets — Grattan, Drummond, and Pigdon — where the family-run places have been operating for 30+ years without a TripAdvisor listing.

Federation Square events: Fed Square runs programming almost every weekend — outdoor screenings, markets, cultural festivals. The problem is it changes weekly, so by the time you read this, whatever was on might have moved. Check fedsquare.com/whats-on on the day.

Eureka Skydeck: It’s fine. $42 for the observation deck, $39 for the Edge experience (the glass box that slides out from the building). If heights are your thing, it’s a genuine thrill. We skipped it because most weekend CBD visitors are looking for food, drink, and culture — not a 30-second adrenaline hit.

⚡ URGENCY BANNER

March weekends are running out. Autumn hits late March in Melbourne, and the CBD’s outdoor dining, rooftop bars, and riverside walks shift tone fast. By April, the layers come out and the rooftop bars start closing for the season. This is your window.

The Bottom Line

Melbourne CBD on a weekend is not the same city it is at 9am on a Tuesday. The office towers empty out, the laneways fill up, and the restaurants shift from corporate lunch energy to something more relaxed. You don’t need a packed itinerary — you need a rough direction and a willingness to duck into the places that catch your eye.

Saturday is for markets, galleries, and a long evening that starts with cocktails and ends with pasta. Sunday is for a slow start, a walk you didn’t plan, and an early dinner before the week kicks back in.

The CBD rewards people who look up. Most of the good stuff is on the second floor, down the laneway, or behind the unmarked door.
