Best Burgers in Northcote 2026: The Definitive Rankings

Updated 16 March 2026 | 8 places tested | Adam Nowak reporting

High Street Northcote runs for roughly three kilometres, and within that stretch you’ll find more burger diversity per block than most Melbourne suburbs manage across their entire postcode. From smash patty specialists to late-night fish shops turning out beef patties on Turkish bread, Northcote’s burger scene is wide-ranging and deeply opinionated.

We ate our way through eight venues over six weeks — dine-in and takeaway, peak and off-peak, sober and slightly worse for wear — to figure out which burgers in Northcote are actually worth your money and which ones are riding on postcode hype.

Here’s the definitive ranking.

1. 300 Grams — The Smash King of High Street

📍 313 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 Burgers $12–$18 | Meal deals with fries ~$20–$25 🍔 Style: Smash patty, American fast-food nostalgia

300 Grams has done something genuinely clever: taken the In-N-Out blueprint, stripped out the franchise sterility, and injected it with Melbourne personality. The orange walls are impossible to miss. The video game-themed naming convention (every burger has a character name) is committed to the bit.

The 300 Gs is the flagship — a double smash patty with cheese, lettuce, onion, tomato, pickles, and the house-made 300 sauce. The patties are thin, crispy-edged, and stacked with purpose. This isn’t a fat pub patty situation; this is about lacy Maillard reaction crust and getting the ratio right.

The Norfcote is the local exclusive — beef, egg, bacon, onion, cheese, tomato, lettuce, beetroot, ketchup and mayo. It’s the burger equivalent of throwing everything from the fridge on a single plate, except it actually works because the execution is tight.

For the chicken crowd, the Hot Bird brings buttermilk-brined fried chicken with slaw, cheese, and chilli mayo. It hits the same nerve as the best fried chicken sandwiches in Melbourne.

The verdict: Best burger in Northcote, full stop. Consistent, well-priced, and actually delivers on the nostalgia it’s selling.

🍔 THE MOVE

If you only have time for one burger in Northcote, this is it: The Norfcote at 300 Grams. Get a double, add the loaded Mac fries, and wash it down with one of the organic sodas. You’ll spend about $25 and feel like you’ve had a proper meal.

2. Sideshow Burgers — The Showman

📍 392 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 Burgers $14–$19 | Combo meals $20–$26 🍔 Style: Loaded American-style, fun and over-the-top

Sideshow doesn’t take itself too seriously, which is exactly the right energy for a burger joint. The clown-inspired branding, the circus naming convention, the sheer audacity of some of the builds — it’s a place that wants you to have a good time.

The Snake Charmer is a fried chicken burger with bacon that manages to be both indulgent and properly seasoned. The Plate Spinner is the vegan option — a cheeseburger built on a plant-based patty that doesn’t feel like a compromise. Their loaded fries are chaotic in the best way.

What sets Sideshow apart is range. They serve breakfast through dinner (7am start most days), meaning you can grab a bacon-and-egg burger at dawn or smash a double at 11pm on a Friday. That flexibility makes it a genuine neighbourhood staple, not just a destination.

The verdict: A strong number two. The vibe is infectious, the menu is broad, and the quality holds up across the board. If 300 Grams is the pick for purists, Sideshow is for people who want the full experience.

3. Northcote Social Club — The Pub Entry

📍 301 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 Burgers $18–$22 | With chips ~$24–$28 🍔 Style: Pub burger, classic Australian

The Northcote Social Club has been the suburb’s beating heart for over two decades — live music, a sunny deck, and the kind of counter meals that keep locals coming back on a Tuesday. Their Angus beef burger falls squarely in the pub tradition: thick patty, solid bun, straightforward toppings, no pretension.

What make betters it is the quality of the beef. The Angus patty is properly seasoned, cooked to your preference, and has the kind of meaty depth you don’t get from smash patty joints. It comes with the usual suspects — lettuce, tomato, cheese, onion — and a pile of chips on the side.

This isn’t a burger that will blow your mind on Instagram. It’s the burger you eat after watching a band, sitting on the deck with a pint, watching the High Street roll by. Context matters.

The verdict: Best pub burger in Northcote by a comfortable margin. Not a destination burger, but a very good one in the right setting.

4. Sookie La La — The Diner Throwback

📍 593 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 Burgers $16–$21 | Shakes $8–$12 🍔 Style: 1950s American diner

Sookie La La closed for a stretch and came back, which in Melbourne’s hospitality landscape counts as a heroic act. The Art Deco fit-out is gorgeous — padded leather stools, vintage tilework, old-school filter coffee with real cream. It feels like stepping into a David Lynch film set, if Lynch directed a burger commercial.

The burgers are diner-style: thick, juicy, served on a soft milk bun with a generous hand. They’re not trying to compete with the smash patty crowd. This is about comfort food done with care. The shakes are thick enough to stand a spoon in, and the sundaes are absurd in scale.

The trade-off is pace. Sookie La La is not a quick in-and-out. It’s a sit-down, order a float, watch the world go through the window kind of place. For some people, that’s the whole point.

The verdict: A unique proposition on High Street. The burger alone might not crack the top three, but the total experience — the setting, the shakes, the atmosphere — puts it firmly in the top half.

5. Westgarth Fish and Burger — The Late-Night Contender

📍 99 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 Burgers $12–$16 | Fish and burger combos $18–$22 🍔 Style: Classic takeaway, late-night

Westgarth Fish and Burger is, first and foremost, a fish and chips shop. But the hamburger is a legitimate menu player here, and for a late-night option on High Street, it punches well above its weight.

The classic beef burger comes with the works — lettuce, tomato, beetroot, onion, cheese — on a soft white bun. It’s not reinventing anything. What it is doing is delivering a solid, satisfying burger at 10pm on a Wednesday when every other kitchen in Northcote has shut.

The fish burger deserves a mention too: a whole piece of battered fish with proper tartare sauce, crispy and fresh. If you’re one of those people who thinks fish burgers are second-rate, this one might change your mind.

The verdict: Not the best burger in Northcote during business hours. But when it’s 10:30pm and you need a feed, Westgarth is the best option by default — and the food is genuinely good, not just available.

6. Northcote Fish and Chips — The Old Faithful

📍 341 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 Burgers $13–$17 | Meal deals $18–$22 🍔 Style: Traditional takeaway, beer-battered, English-influenced

Northcote Fish and Chips has been on High Street long enough to have seen three waves of burger trends come and go. The English beer-battered fish burger is their calling card — thick, golden, and served with proper chips. But the beef burger is quietly competent too.

Post-COVID, they upgraded the offering: vegan options now sit alongside the classics, and alcoholic drinks have been added to the menu. It’s not your grandparents’ fish and chip shop anymore, but it hasn’t lost the fundamentals.

The beef burger is straightforward: a standard patty with the usual toppings, served without fuss. It’s the kind of burger that reminds you why you like burgers in the first place — bread, meat, sauce, done.

The verdict: A reliable option that won’t excite you but won’t disappoint you. Perfectly placed at number six — good enough to recommend, not quite good enough to chase.

7. Danny’s Burgers — The North Fitzroy Adjacent

📍 358–360 St Georges Road, Fitzroy North VIC 3068 💰 Burgers $8–$14 | Double cheeseburger + drink ~$13 🍔 Style: Old-school, budget, late-night

Danny’s isn’t technically in Northcote — it’s five minutes up the road in Fitzroy North, on St Georges Road. But when you’re compiling a Northcote burger list, ignoring Danny’s would be dishonest. Generations of Northcote locals have been crossing the boundary for Danny’s since 1945.

The prices are the first thing you notice. A double cheeseburger and a can of coke for under $14 is increasingly rare in Melbourne. The patties are thin, the cheese is melty, and the whole thing is wrapped in paper. It’s the burger equivalent of a V8 Holden — uncomplicated, unapologetic, and deeply satisfying.

The late-night hours (open until 2am most nights) make Danny’s an institution for post-gig, post-pub, and post-poor-decision hunger. The queue on a Saturday night is part of the experience.

The verdict: The cheapest and most nostalgic burger on this list. Not the best burger in Northcote, but the one with the most history behind it.

8. Northcote Social Club (The Parma Burger) — Honourable Mention

📍 301 High Street, Northcote VIC 3070 💰 The Parma Burger $24 🍔 Style: Pub-Italian hybrid

We’re including this as a separate entry because it operates in a different category from the Social Club’s Angus burger. The Parma Burger takes the schnitzel concept, adds the parmigiana treatment — napolitana sauce, melted cheese, ham — and shoves it into a burger bun. It’s a category unto itself.

At $24, it’s the most expensive burger on this list, and you’ll need the appetite to match. This is a meal that will put you to sleep. It’s rich, messy, and exactly what you want after a long week.

The verdict: Not a burger purist’s pick, but an undeniable good time.

What We Skipped and Why

Coburger & Co (Coburg) — Frequently mentioned alongside Northcote spots, but it’s on Moreland Road in Coburg, a 20-minute walk across the creek. We’re keeping this list tight. (We’ll cover the best burgers in Coburg separately.)

Juanita Peaches (Brunswick) — Outstanding venue, and Raph Rashid’s Beatbox Kitchen legacy is undeniable. But it’s on Edward Street in Brunswick. Check our Brunswick burger guide for the full rundown.

Chew Burger (Preston) — Wagyu beef brisket burgers with buns from Northcote Bakehouse, so there’s a connection. But the shop is on Plenty Road in Preston. Worth a trip, but not a Northcote entry.

Royal Stacks (CBD/Crown) — A Melbourne institution, but their Northcote listing on some platforms is outdated. The current locations are Collins Street and Crown. No physical Northcote store.

Easey’s (Collingwood) — The train-carriage burgers on Easey Street are iconic, but that’s Collingwood. Again, we’ll cover it in the inner north burger roundup.

The Final Word

Northcote’s burger scene in 2026 is in good shape. The smash patty wars between 300 Grams and Sideshow have raised the bar for everyone, the pub burger at the Social Club is a reliable stalwart, and the late-night options at Westgarth and Danny’s mean you’re never far from a decent feed.

If you’re visiting for the first time: go to 300 Grams. Order the Norfcote double. Get the Mac fries. Sit by the window and watch High Street do its thing.

If you’re a local and you think we’ve got it wrong — good. That’s the point. Northcote has too many good burgers for any single list to settle the argument.
