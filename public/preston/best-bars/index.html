Best Bars in Preston — 2026 Local Guide

T
Tyler James Mar 22, 2026
Preston

Preston has a bar scene that reflects the suburb — not short on places to drink, with a mix of dive bars, wine spots, and proper cocktail joints. Whether you want a quiet after-work beer, a proper cocktail, or a place to meet friends on a Saturday night, this suburb covers it.

Hardout Bar — Plenty Road

Best for: Craft beer, local wine, and vinyl DJs

Hardout Bar has the soul of a pub with the drinks list of a craft beer bar. The Plenty Road venue is decorated with books from Black Spark Cultural Centre, local art, and eclectic furniture. The outdoor area is small but perfectly positioned. The drink selections lean Victorian: local craft beers, regional wines, and pet-nats. Vinyl DJs spin on weekends covering everything from soul to hip-hop. It’s the kind of bar where you walk in for one drink and stay for four hours because the playlist is too good to leave.

Open 4pm-late daily. Best nights: Thursday-Saturday.

Oliva Social — 102-104 High Street

Best for: Cocktails with European sophistication

Oliva Social straddles the line between neighbourhood pub and cocktail bar. High ceilings, polished concrete floors, and a front courtyard with fairy lights and picnic tables that feels more like a European beer garden than an Australian pub. The Chinotto Connection (whiskey, Chinotto San Pellegrino, lemon juice, honey) is their signature and it’s excellent. Beyond cocktails, the beer and wine lists are solid, and the food menu covers pizzas, tapas, and grazing platters at reasonable prices.

The courtyard fills up fast on warm evenings — arrive before 7pm on weekends or expect a wait.

Rebel Rebel — Plenty Road

Best for: Dive bar dates and no pretension

Named after the Bowie song and living up to it. Dim red lighting. Vintage photos on the walls. Records stacked barside. The drinks list is straightforward — spirits, beers, wine, and a couple of cocktails that prioritise flavour over presentation. In winter, they do mulled wine and hot toddies. The outdoor space is intimate — fairy lights, candles, just enough room for a handful of tables.

Surly’s Bar and Garden — High Street

Best for: Local ales, pet-nats, and mismatched furniture charm

Surly’s opened in December 2019, closed three months later for COVID, and somehow emerged stronger. The mismatched furniture sourced from Facebook Marketplace and the 1930s-40s aesthetic give it genuine character. The drinks list is where it stands out: ultra-local ales, pet-nats, and vegan cocktails. No kitchen, but local restaurants deliver — settle in for a proper session.

The Raccoon Club — 145 Plenty Road

Best for: Board games, pool, and grungy vibes

A red door on Plenty Road leads into a long, narrow venue filled with people playing pool and board games while taxidermy raccoons watch from above the bar. Walls covered in political statements, nude drawings, CDs, records, and VHS tapes. Well-priced drinks and no kitchen — you’re welcome to get food delivered from surrounding restaurants.

When to Go

After work (5-7pm) — The sweet spot for most Preston bars. Relaxed, locals unwinding.

Friday night — High Street and Plenty Road fill up. Get in by 6pm or expect to wait.

Saturday — Peak social time. Bars are full, expect queues at popular spots.

Sunday session — Underrated. Some of Preston’s bars do Sunday sessions that are genuinely great — more relaxed, often live music or DJs.

FAQ

Where’s the best cocktail bar in Preston? Oliva Social at 102-104 High Street. The Chinotto Connection is the signature drink and it’s worth the trip.

Are there any dive bars in Preston? Rebel Rebel on Plenty Road is a proper dive bar in the best sense. The Raccoon Club at 145 Plenty Road is similarly grungy and brilliant.

What time do Preston bars close? Most stay open until 1-2am on weekends. Weeknights tend to wind down around midnight.

The Verdict

Preston isn’t trying to out-cool the inner-city bar hubs. The bars here work because they serve the locals first — places you’d go weekly, not Instagram destinations you visit once. That gives the suburb’s bar scene an authenticity that more hyped areas often lack. Five to six genuinely good bars within walking distance of each other along High Street and Plenty Road is more than enough for a proper night out.
