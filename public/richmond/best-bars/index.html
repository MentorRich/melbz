Best Bars in Richmond 2026: Swan Street & Church Street

Lina Nguyen

Nightlife Editor • Mar 16, 2026 • 9 min read

Best Bars in Richmond 2026: Swan Street & Church Street

Updated 16 March 2026 | 6 places tested | Lina Nguyen reporting

Richmond doesn’t try to be cool. That’s exactly why it is.

While Fitzroy and Collingwood get the Instagram love, Richmond has quietly built one of Melbourne’s most drinkable neighbourhoods — a place where Swan Street’s footy-day chaos meets Church Street’s more polished energy, and where you can still find a $9 pint at 2am without being judged for it.

I spent two weeks working my way along both strips (and a few side streets) to find the bars actually worth your time in 2026. Here’s what made the cut.

The Corner Hotel

Where: 57 Swan Street, Richmond

If Melbourne’s live music scene had a town hall, it’d be the Corner Hotel. This place has been running since the 1940s, and the room — complete with its infamous single column right in the middle of the crowd — is one of the most recognisable gig venues in the country. You haven’t done Melbourne music properly until you’ve had your view of the stage blocked by that column at least once.

But the Corner isn’t just about the bandroom. Upstairs, there’s a rooftop bar and beer garden that’s genuinely pleasant on a weekday afternoon, which is a sentence you rarely apply to a live music venue. The rooftop has greenery, decent sightlines over the neighbourhood, and enough space that you don’t feel packed in like a sardine (the bandroom downstairs handles that energy).

Drinks: Solid tap selection — think Local, Balter, Stone & Wood, plus a rotating craft handle. Wines from $12/glass, spirits from $15. Happy hour specials on weeknights.

Vibe: Post-gig debrief, pre-gig nerves, or just a Tuesday arvo with a schooner. The crowd runs from indie kids to grey-haired regulars who’ve been coming since the ’80s.

Price range: Pints $10–$14, cocktails $18–$22, wines $12–$16.

Best for: Live music nights (check their gig calendar — it’s stacked), rooftop drinks before footy, or a casual midweek beer.

Maeve Fox

Where: 472 Church Street, Richmond

Tucked next to East Richmond station with a direct sightline to the MCG, Maeve Fox occupies one of the best地理位置s in the suburb — though you’d never guess it from the street. Walk in and you’re met with dim lighting, dark timber, and a cocktail list that takes itself just seriously enough without becoming pretentious.

The cocktail game here is genuinely strong. The Dharma & Diego (Vida Mezcal, caramel, banana) is a standout, and the Smoke House (Chartreuse, gin, lime, rosemary) delivers exactly the smoky-herbal punch the name promises. But the real surprise is the food — Maeve Fox does gourmet jaffles. Spaghetti and cheese, three-cheese, chorizo. They’re the kind of thing you order ironically and then eat three of.

Drinks: Cocktails from $18, tap beers from $10, wine from $11/glass. Half-price cocktails some weeknights — check their specials board.

Vibe: After-work drinks that stretch into the evening. Date-night energy without the dress code. A spot where a group of mates and a couple on their third date can coexist comfortably.

Best for: Cocktails with ambition, sneaky weeknight sessions, and post-MCG drinks when you don’t want to deal with the Crown precinct crowds.

Brogan’s Way Distillery

Where: 616 Swan Street, Richmond

A father-daughter gin distillery right on Swan Street, which sounds like the setup for a heartwarming film and honestly kind of is. Brogan’s Way is one of those rare places where you can watch your drink being made from raw ingredients — the copper stills are right there behind the bar, working away.

The gin range is broad and deliberately approachable. Their Strawberries and Cream gin is the crowd favourite (served in a martini or a G&T, both work), but don’t sleep on the Navy Strength if you want something with actual backbone. They do gin flights — three glasses, different styles — which is the move if you’re new to craft gin and want to find your lane.

Food leans European: crisp flatbreads, cheese boards, gin-friendly bar snacks. Nothing fussy, but everything pairs well with what’s in your glass.

Drinks: Gin cocktails from $19, gin flights from $28 (three pours), tap beers from $11, wine from $13.

Vibe: Educational without being condescending. A Friday afternoon here feels like you’re learning something while also having a very good time. Suits, date nights, and gin enthusiasts in equal measure.

Best for: Gin discovery, food-and-drink pairings, and impressing someone without spending a fortune.

Clover

Where: Swan Street, Richmond (quieter end, near the Burnley Street intersection)

Clover is the kind of wine bar that makes you feel like you’ve stumbled onto something you want to keep secret. Run by chef Charley Snadden-Wilson (formerly of The Moon, Etta, and Embla) and wine guru Lyndon Kubis (The Moon, Toorak Cellars, Milton Wine Shop), it’s a 48-seat space that punches well above its weight in both food and wine.

The menu is modern Australian with a French lean — think razor-sharp technique applied to seasonal ingredients. It’s the type of place where a simple plate of roasted carrots with cultured butter and hazelnuts becomes a revelation. The wine list spans Australia, France, Italy, and Germany, with a genuine emphasis on lesser-known producers. Ask the staff for recommendations and they’ll steer you right.

Drinks: Wines from $15/glass, $65–$120/bottle. A few beers for the non-wine crowd. No cocktail list — this is a wine bar that knows what it is.

Vibe: Intimate, warm, and unhurried. A weeknight dinner that turns into a three-bottle evening. Perfect for people who care about what’s in their glass.

Best for: Date nights, wine nerdery, and long dinners where the food keeps getting better as the night goes on.

Harlow

Where: 447 Church Street, Richmond

Harlow is the bar that Richmond didn’t know it needed until it had one. The rooftop is the main draw — it catches the afternoon sun, offers views across the neighbourhood, and has enough space that you can actually get a seat without arriving at noon. On a summer arvo, it’s one of the best rooftop setups in the inner east.

Downstairs, the vibe shifts to something more pub-adjacent: acoustic live music on weekends, a menu of modern pub grub, and a crowd that skews slightly older than Swan Street’s footy faithful. The bottomless boozy lunch ($59 for a main plus two hours of free-flowing drinks) is a legitimately good deal if you time it right.

Drinks: Tap beers from $10, cocktails from $17, wines from $12. Bottomless lunch package $59pp (weekends).

Vibe: Sunday arvo energy, seven days a week. Relaxed, social, and firmly in the “no one’s here to be seen” camp.

Best for: Rooftop sessions, weekend bottomless lunch, acoustic music nights, and anyone avoiding the Swan Street footy crush.

Bar 9T4

Where: 94 Swan Street, Richmond

Bar 9T4 is Richmond’s late-night pressure valve. Named after its address (94 Swan Street — say it out loud), this place has been running since long before the word “mixology” entered the lexicon, and it has absolutely no interest in adopting it.

This is not a craft cocktail bar. This is a place where you go when the sun’s gone down, the footy’s finished, and you want loud music, cheap drinks, and zero pretension. The DJs play across genres, the crowd peaks around midnight, and the doors stay open until 3am on Fridays and Saturdays. It’s loose, it’s fun, and it knows exactly what it is.

Drinks: Beers from $8, basic mixed drinks from $12, shots from $10. This is happy-hour pricing at midnight territory.

Vibe: Pre-footy pints, post-gig decompression, and the kind of Saturday night where you don’t check the time until you’re outside wondering how it got so late.

Best for: Late nights, cheap drinks, footy crowds, and nights when you want to dance without paying a cover charge.

What We Skipped and Why

Not every Richmond bar made the cut. Here’s why a few well-known names didn’t get a full write-up:

The Bridge Hotel — Labyrinthine and memorable, yes, but the crowd on weekends is overwhelmingly footy-focused, and the upstairs commercial house playlist hasn’t changed since approximately 2009. Worth a visit, but not a “best of” contender in 2026.

Fargo and Co. — The rooftop terrace is solid and the bottomless brunch draws crowds, but the food and drink offering doesn’t quite distinguish itself from the dozen other two-level pubs along this stretch. Reliable, not remarkable.

Prince Alfred Hotel — A Chapel Street-adjacent institution with a massive beer garden, but it sits right on the Richmond-South Yarra border and pulls more from the south side. We love it — we just think it belongs in a South Yarra bars guide rather than here.

Lilac Wine Bar — Technically in Cremorne, not Richmond proper, though the two are barely separated. Lilac is excellent — grungy warehouse energy, French bistro food, vinyl on the turntable — but we’re covering it in our Cremorne neighbourhood guide instead.

The Royston Hotel — A true locals’ pub with great parmas and plenty of taps, but it’s more “beloved local” than “destination bar.” If you’re already in Richmond and want a quiet pint, it’s perfect. If you’re coming from across town, there are bigger draws.

Getting There and Getting Home

Richmond is one of Melbourne’s most accessible suburbs for a night out:

  • Train: East Richmond station (Church Street) or Richmond station (Swan Street) — both on the Sandringham line
  • Tram: Route 78 along Church Street, Route 70 along Swan Street/Bridge Road
  • Driving: Paid parking along both streets, but honestly — train it. Parking on a Saturday night is an exercise in frustration.
  • Rideshare: Surge pricing kicks in hard after midnight, especially after MCG events. Walk to a side street and order from there.

The Verdict

Richmond in 2026 isn’t trying to compete with the degustation-and-natural-wine crowd in Collingwood or the rooftop spectacle of the CBD. What it offers is better: a drinking neighbourhood that actually feels like a neighbourhood. Places where the bartenders know regulars by name, where you can get a quality gin made from scratch or a $9 schooner without either experience feeling like a compromise.

Start on Swan Street for the energy. End on Church Street for the cocktails. And if it’s after midnight and you’re still standing, Bar 9T4 will take care of you.

Richmond doesn’t do pretension. It does good drinks, good food, and enough variety that every night out feels different from the last. That’s not flashy. That’s just Melbourne at its best.
