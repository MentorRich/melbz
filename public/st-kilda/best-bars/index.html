19 Best Bars in St Kilda for Every Kind of Night Out

Lina Nguyen
13 Mar 2026 · 21 min read
St-Kilda

St Kilda’s bar scene runs from legendary music venues where international acts have graced the stage to intimate wine bars hidden down laneways, rooftop cocktail spots with bay views, and dive bars that feel like your local even on your first visit.

What’s covered in this guide:

  • The Icons: Essential St Kilda institutions including The Espy and Prince Public Bar
  • Rooftop & Waterfront: Beach-adjacent venues with killer views and coastal vibes
  • Wine Bars & Cocktail Spots: Intimate spaces for serious sipping and conversation
  • Pubs & Live Music: Where to catch gigs and settle in for a proper session
  • Local Favourites & Late-Night: Neighbourhood gems and after-midnight options

There’s something magical about St Kilda after dark. Maybe it’s the way the fairy lights strung along the Esplanade catch the bay breeze, or how Luna Park’s neon glow spills across Fitzroy Street as revellers bounce between venues. This bayside suburb has been Melbourne’s playground since the 1850s, and that party spirit lives on in every corner pub, rooftop bar, and laneway wine dive.

The nightlife here isn’t just about the drinks — though trust me, we’ve got those sorted. It’s about the stories these places hold. The Espy where Crowded House, Hunters & Collectors, and basically every Australian band worth their salt has played. Dogs Bar, pouring natural wines since 1989 when the rest of Melbourne was still figuring out what a wine bar even was. The Prince, where European elegance meets Melbourne’s obsession with a perfect bloody mary.

From Acland Street’s European café culture that transforms into wine bar territory after 5pm, to Fitzroy Street’s bustling strip that never seems to sleep, to the Esplanade’s beachfront venues where you can watch ships drift across Port Phillip Bay between rounds — St Kilda’s got every mood covered. Whether you’re after a quiet Tuesday night natural wine with a mate, a Friday after-work cocktail with harbour views, or a Saturday night that starts at 8pm and ends at sunrise, we’ve mapped out exactly where to go.

The Icons

These are the venues that define St Kilda’s drinking culture. They’ve earned their legendary status through decades of excellent service, unforgettable nights, and that indefinable quality that makes a place feel essential rather than just good.

1. The Espy

The vibe: Melbourne’s most famous live music venue that also happens to pour exceptional cocktails across twelve different bars spread over five levels.

This is where legends are made, both on stage and behind the bar. The Espy reopened in 2018 after a multimilion-dollar renovation, but they’ve kept the soul that made this place special — raw, electric energy that makes every night feel like you’re part of something bigger. The Sunroom on the top level is pure magic: floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking the bay, botanical cocktails that taste like summer, and an atmosphere that shifts from sophisticated afternoon drinks to buzzing evening pre-drinks spot.

Order this: Sunroom Spritz ($23) — elderflower, gin, cucumber, and pure St Kilda sunshine in a glass
Also try: Any cocktail from the main bar downstairs ($22-24) — the bartenders here are artists
Address: 11 The Esplanade
Hours: Mon-Wed 11am-midnight, Thu 11am-1am, Fri 11am-3am, Sat 9am-3am, Sun 9am-1am
Price range: $$$
Best for: Epic nights out, live music, impressing visitors, special occasions
Insider tip: The Sunroom gets packed after 7pm on weekends — arrive by 5pm for the best tables with bay views

2. Prince Public Bar

The vibe: Effortlessly cool neighbourhood bar that perfected the art of being sophisticated without being stuffy.

Walking into Prince Public feels like stepping into that friend’s house who just happens to have impeccable taste and a fully stocked bar. The fit-out is subtle luxury — marble, brass, and warm timber — but the energy is relaxed and welcoming. This place gets busy after work and stays busy, which tells you everything about the quality. Their wagyu burgers are genuinely some of the best in St Kilda, and the wine list skews European with some serious drops if you’re celebrating something special.

Order this: The Prince Martini ($24) — a perfect example of why simple classics done well beat fancy for fancy’s sake
Also try: Wagyu burger ($32) — pairs perfectly with their house red
Address: 29 Fitzroy Street
Hours: Open all day
Price range: $$$
Best for: After-work drinks, dinner dates, weekend catch-ups
Insider tip: Happy hour Monday-Friday 4-6pm — $11 pints and $9 wine that’s actually worth drinking

3. Bar Di Stasio

The vibe: Old-school Italian glamour where every cocktail is an event and the service makes you feel like Melbourne royalty.

This is where St Kilda does serious cocktails. Bar Di Stasio has been setting the standard for Italian-inspired drinks since long before negroni o’clock became a thing. The white-jacketed bartenders move with the precision of orchestra conductors, and watching them work is half the entertainment. The fit-out screams 1960s Rome — all marble and mirrors and sophisticated lighting that makes everyone look like they just stepped off a film set. It’s expensive, but when you want to feel properly pampered, nowhere else comes close.

Order this: Negroni Sbagliato ($26) — the prosecco version that’s perfect for pre-dinner
Also try: Any of their seasonal specials (around $25-28) — the menu changes with Italian seasons
Address: 31 Fitzroy Street
Hours: 12pm-late daily
Price range: $$$$
Best for: Special occasions, impressing dates, before dinner at Di Stasio restaurant
Insider tip: Book ahead for weekend evenings — this place fills up with people who know quality when they see it

Rooftop & Waterfront

St Kilda’s greatest asset is its location right on Port Phillip Bay, and these venues make the most of that advantage. Whether you’re after beachfront casual or elevated bay views, this is where Melbourne’s coastal drinking culture shines.

4. Captain Baxter

The vibe: Sophisticated beach club energy with a retractable roof that transforms based on weather and mood.

Captain Baxter sits right on the St Kilda Sea Baths, and the views are genuinely spectacular — uninterrupted harbour panoramas that make you forget you’re in a city. The retractable roof means this place works year-round: open-air beach vibes in summer, cosy greenhouse feels in winter. Their Veuve Sun Club package ($89pp) includes bottomless Veuve Clicquot for two hours, which sounds excessive until you’re three champagnes deep watching the sunset paint the bay gold.

Order this: Porn Star Martini ($24) — tropical, Instagram-worthy, and surprisingly well-executed
Also try: Aperol Spritz ($22) — classic beach drink in the perfect beach setting
Address: 10-18 Jacka Boulevard, Sea Baths
Hours: Thu-Fri 5pm-late, Sat-Sun 12pm-1am
Price range: $$$$
Best for: Sunset drinks, birthday celebrations, summer sessions
Insider tip: The corner tables near the windows are worth the wait — ask to be put on the list rather than settling for interior seating

5. Republica

The vibe: Relaxed beachfront casual where you can rock up in swimmers and thongs or dressed for dinner and feel equally at home.

Republica gets the beach bar formula exactly right: quality drinks without the pretension, food that’s actually good rather than just convenient, and an atmosphere that shifts seamlessly from family-friendly afternoon hangs to buzzy evening drinks. The beachfront location is unbeatable — you’re literally watching the waves while you sip your Aperol Spritz. Their happy hour is genuinely generous: $12 Aperol Spritzes and $30 cocktail pitchers that are perfect for groups.

Order this: Aperol Spritz ($22, $12 during happy hour) — the ultimate beach drink in the ultimate beach setting
Also try: Cocktail pitcher ($30 during HH) — Pimm’s or sangria, perfect for groups
Address: 10-18 Jacka Boulevard
Hours: Mon-Thu 12pm-10pm, Fri-Sat 12pm-midnight
Price range: $$$
Best for: Beach hangs, group drinks, sunset sessions
Insider tip: Happy hour Monday-Friday 5-6pm — the best value beachfront drinking in Melbourne

6. Stokehouse

The vibe: Elegant waterfront dining where the upstairs bar serves cocktails that match the million-dollar bay views.

The upstairs bar at Stokehouse is where you go when you want to feel fancy without the formality. Floor-to-ceiling windows frame Port Phillip Bay perfectly, and the cocktails ($22-26) are crafted with the same attention to detail as the renowned restaurant downstairs. This isn’t a party venue — it’s where you come for sophisticated conversation, quality spirits, and some of the best seafood bar snacks in the city. Perfect for pre-dinner drinks that might accidentally turn into dinner because the view makes it impossible to leave.

Order this: Aperitif of choice ($22-24) — the bar team knows their spirits and will recommend perfectly
Also try: Oysters (market price) — some of the freshest in Melbourne with bay views to match
Address: 30 Jacka Boulevard
Hours: Daily from 12pm
Price range: $$$$
Best for: Special occasions, romantic dates, sophisticated catch-ups
Insider tip: Book ahead for window seats — the view difference between window and interior tables is dramatic

7. Pontoon

The vibe: Laid-back waterfront hangout that captures the casual beach culture St Kilda does best.

Pontoon keeps things simple and does it well: cold beer, decent cocktails, and waterfront views without the fuss. This is where locals come when they want beachside drinks without beachside prices or pretension. The deck overlooking the water is perfect for afternoon sessions that drift into evening, and the relaxed atmosphere means you can nurse one drink for hours or settle in for a proper session — your call. It’s the kind of place that makes you remember why you love living near the water.

Order this: Beer on tap (varies) — cold, simple, perfect for the setting
Also try: Fish and chips ($24) — quality pub food with water views
Address: Lower Esplanade
Hours: Daily
Price range: $$
Best for: Casual hangs, afternoon drinks, unpretentious good times
Insider tip: The deck gets full sun in the afternoon — perfect in winter, brutal in summer without the shade sails

Wine Bars & Cocktail Spots

This is where St Kilda’s sophisticated side comes out to play. From natural wine pioneers to Art Deco cocktail temples, these venues prove that good drinks and intimate atmosphere never go out of style.

8. Dogs Bar

The vibe: The original St Kilda wine bar that’s been pouring natural wines and hosting live music since most wine bars were just an ambitious idea.

Dogs Bar is an institution that earned its status the hard way — by being consistently excellent for over thirty years. This place was doing natural wines before they were trendy, hosting live music before it was a marketing strategy, and welcoming dogs before it was instagram-worthy. The vibe is effortlessly bohemian: mismatched furniture, art on every wall, and an energy that shifts from intimate afternoon wines to buzzy evening hang depending on the night. The live music happens nightly and it’s always free, which in 2026 feels almost revolutionary.

Order this: Natural wine by the glass ($12-18) — the staff know their drops and love recommendations
Also try: Creole dishes ($5-25) — unexpected but excellent food that pairs perfectly with wine
Address: 54 Acland Street
Hours: Mon-Tue 4pm-11pm, Wed-Sun 12pm-1am
Price range: $$
Best for: Live music, natural wine education, bohemian atmosphere
Insider tip: The back courtyard is dog-friendly and often has the best acoustic acts — arrive early for a good table

9. Dawn & Mabel’s

The vibe: Brand-new Art Deco wine bar that feels like it’s been serving excellent regional wines in elegant surroundings for decades.

Dawn & Mabel’s is the newcomer doing everything right. The Art Deco fit-out is stunning — all brass and marble and vintage cutlery that makes every glass feel special — but it’s the wine program that really impresses. They’re focusing on regional Victorian producers, which means you’ll discover drops you can’t find elsewhere in Melbourne. The atmosphere manages to be both sophisticated and welcoming, perfect for wine nerds and casual drinkers alike. Already feels essential, which is impressive for a venue that’s barely months old.

Order this: Regional Victorian drop recommended by staff ($15-22) — they know their local producers intimately
Also try: Cheese board ($28) — perfectly curated to complement the wine selection
Address: 83 Acland Street
Hours: Wed-Sun
Price range: $$$
Best for: Wine discovery, intimate dates, sophisticated night out
Insider tip: The vintage cutlery isn’t just decoration — each piece has been carefully selected and adds to the authentic period atmosphere

10. St LuJa

The vibe: 1930s Art Deco gentleman’s club atmosphere where excellent Guinness meets serious cocktails and Thursday wing nights turn into proper sessions.

St LuJa does old-school glamour without the stuffiness. The 1930s Art Deco fit-out is gorgeous — dark wood, brass fixtures, and lighting that makes everyone look like they’ve stepped out of a period film — but the energy is welcoming and fun. Their claim to pour the “second best Guinness in Melbourne” might sound modest, but it’s actually fighting words in a city that takes its stout seriously. Thursday’s $30 bottomless wings deal regularly turns into impromptu parties, and their cocktail program focuses on classic preparations done perfectly.

Order this: Guinness ($12) — creamy, perfectly poured, and they’re not kidding about the quality
Also try: Old Fashioned ($22) — classic preparation that showcases their serious approach to spirits
Address: 9 Fitzroy Street
Hours: Wed-Sun from 4pm
Price range: $$$
Best for: Date nights, wing sessions, classic cocktails
Insider tip: Thursday bottomless wings ($30) from 6pm often become impromptu parties — book ahead or risk missing out

11. The Vineyard

The vibe: Cosy neighbourhood wine bar where natural wine enthusiasts gather for intimate conversations and exceptional drops in unpretentious surroundings.

The Vineyard proves that you don’t need fancy fit-outs to create a special atmosphere — just excellent wine, knowledgeable staff, and genuine passion for what you’re pouring. This tiny space focuses exclusively on natural wines, with a rotating selection that changes based on what the team is excited about. It’s the kind of place where you’ll end up in conversation with other wine lovers, learn about producers you’ve never heard of, and probably stay much longer than you planned. Intimate in the best possible way.

Order this: Whatever the staff recommends ($14-20) — they taste everything and love sharing discoveries
Also try: Natural wine flight (around $35) — the best way to explore different styles and producers
Address: 71A Acland Street
Hours: Wed-Sun
Price range: $$
Best for: Natural wine education, intimate conversations, neighbourhood drinking
Insider tip: The wine selection changes frequently — if you taste something special, buy a bottle to take home because it might not be there next week

Pubs & Live Music

These venues capture the essence of Melbourne’s legendary pub and live music culture. From craft beer temples to venues where tomorrow’s headliners are playing tonight, this is where St Kilda’s community spirit comes alive.

12. The Local Taphouse

The vibe: Craft beer paradise where serious beer nerds and casual punters unite over 19 rotating taps and some of the best pub food in the eastern suburbs.

The Local Taphouse is what happens when beer obsessives are given unlimited resources and creative freedom. Nineteen rotating taps featuring everything from local breweries to rare international drops, with over 400 different beers pouring annually. But this isn’t just about beer tourism — the atmosphere is genuinely welcoming, the pub food is excellent, and the happy hour deals ($10 pints Monday-Thursday 4-6pm) make quality craft beer accessible. Located in St Kilda East, so it attracts more locals than tourists, which always improves the energy.

Order this: Whatever’s on special tap ($12-16) — the rotation means there’s always something new to discover
Also try: Beer and burger combination ($28) — they know how to pair food with their drops
Address: 184 Carlisle Street, St Kilda East
Hours: Daily 12pm-late
Price range: $$
Best for: Craft beer education, casual dinners, neighbourhood sessions
Insider tip: The bottle shop attached means you can take home anything you discover on tap — perfect for continuing the session or sharing discoveries with mates

13. Veludo

The vibe: Seven-days-a-week party venue where live music Thursday-Sunday and Veludo Fridays ($3.90 basics) create the kind of chaos that makes legends.

Veludo is controlled chaos in the best possible way. Open until 3am seven days a week, live music most nights, and Veludo Fridays that turn into impromptu festivals thanks to $3.90 basic spirits that make no economic sense but perfect party sense. This isn’t where you come for quiet conversations — it’s where you come when you want to remember why you love nightlife. The energy is infectious, the crowd is diverse, and the music ranges from local bands to DJs who know how to work a room.

Order this: Basic spirit and mixer ($3.90 on Fridays, $6 other days) — revolutionary pricing for the location
Also try: Pint of beer ($8 during happy hour) — quality drops at prices that feel like time travel
Address: 175 Acland Street
Hours: 7 days until 3am
Price range: $
Best for: Live music, late-night sessions, meeting new people
Insider tip: Veludo Fridays get absolutely packed — arrive before 8pm or be prepared to queue

14. The George Lounge

The vibe: Luxurious late-night basement venue specialising in Australian spirits and the kind of sophisticated atmosphere that makes 2am feel like 10pm.

Hidden in the basement beneath Fitzroy Street, The George Lounge is where St Kilda’s nightlife gets properly sophisticated. This isn’t a pub — it’s a temple to Australian spirits and craft cocktails, with a fit-out that screams luxury and an atmosphere that encourages lingering. Friday and Saturday nights only (plus special events), with happy hour 4-7pm for those who like to start their evening properly. The crowd tends to be older and more sophisticated, making this perfect for when you want quality rather than quantity.

Order this: Australian whisky neat ($18-35) — they stock drops you won’t find elsewhere
Also try: Signature cocktail featuring native botanicals ($24) — showcases the Australian spirit focus beautifully
Address: Basement, 129 Fitzroy Street
Hours: Fri-Sat plus special events
Price range: $$$$
Best for: Late-night sophistication, spirit education, special occasions
Insider tip: Happy hour 4-7pm offers significantly better value on premium spirits — perfect for pre-dinner or early evening drinks

15. Village Belle Hotel

The vibe: Classic Australian pub where beer gardens, live music, and New Year’s Eve parties create the community atmosphere that makes neighbourhoods feel like home.

Village Belle is exactly what a great local pub should be: welcoming, unpretentious, and anchored in its community. The beer garden is perfect for afternoon sessions that drift into evening, the live music program brings in quality local acts, and their New Year’s Eve parties are legendary among locals. This isn’t about fancy cocktails or innovative food — it’s about cold beer, good company, and the kind of atmosphere that makes you want to become a regular. Essential for understanding St Kilda’s community spirit.

Order this: Schooner of local beer on tap ($9-12) — cold, simple, perfect
Also try: Pub schnitzel ($22) — classic preparation that hits the spot after a few drinks
Address: 202 Barkly Street
Hours: Daily
Price range: $
Best for: Local atmosphere, live music, community feel
Insider tip: The beer garden gets packed during major sporting events — arrive early with mates to claim a table

Local Favourites & Late-Night

These venues capture the authentic St Kilda spirit that locals fiercely protect. From neighbourhood bars where regulars know each other’s names to late-night institutions that keep the party alive after everywhere else shuts down.

16. Chronicles Bar

The vibe: Relaxed neighbourhood bar where locals gather for unpretentious drinks and conversation in surroundings that feel like someone’s well-appointed living room.

Chronicles Bar is the epitome of a local’s local — the kind of place where you’ll see the same faces week after week, where the bartender remembers your order, and where tourist crowds remain blissfully minimal. The atmosphere is conversational rather than party-focused, making this perfect for catch-ups with mates or quiet drinks when you want to feel part of a community rather than anonymous in a crowd. Simple drinks done well, fair prices, and the kind of welcoming energy that makes you want to become a regular.

Order this: House red or white ($10-12) — quality drops at neighbourhood prices
Also try: Classic cocktail ($16-18) — well-executed basics without the fancy markup
Address: 91 Fitzroy Street
Hours: Daily
Price range: $$
Best for: Local atmosphere, quiet drinks, becoming a regular
Insider tip: Tuesday nights tend to be locals-only and perfect for experiencing the genuine community atmosphere

17. Hotel Barkly

The vibe: Dive bar energy with live music, pool tables, and late licensing that attracts everyone from tradies finishing work to musos starting their night.

Hotel Barkly is beautifully chaotic in the way only a proper dive bar can be. Pool tables, live music, a crowd that ranges from afternoon drinkers to late-night party animals, and an atmosphere that’s simultaneously rough around the edges and completely welcoming. This is where different parts of St Kilda’s community intersect: students, workers, artists, and lifelong locals all finding common ground over cold beer and loud music. The late licence means the party continues long after more civilised venues call it a night.

Order this: Pot of beer ($7-9) — cheap, cold, perfect for the atmosphere
Also try: Basic spirit ($8-10) — drinks priced for locals, not tourists
Address: 109 Barkly Street
Hours: Daily with late licence
Price range: $
Best for: Dive bar atmosphere, live music, late-night sessions
Insider tip: The pool tables get competitive later in the evening — if you’re not confident with a cue, stick to afternoon sessions

18. Pint on Punt

The vibe: Craft beer focused sports bar where rotating taps and multiple screens create the perfect combination of beer education and sporting entertainment.

Pint on Punt gets the sports bar formula exactly right: excellent beer selection, enough screens to follow multiple games simultaneously, and an atmosphere that’s passionate about both sport and beer without being obnoxious about either. The rotating taps mean there’s always something new to try, and the food menu is designed to complement beer rather than compete with it. Perfect for when you want to watch the game with quality drinks and like-minded people who appreciate both good beer and good sport.

Order this: Whatever’s new on tap ($12-15) — the rotation means constant discovery opportunities
Also try: Beer flight ($25) — perfect way to compare different styles side by side
Address: 42 Punt Road
Hours: Daily
Price range: $$
Best for: Watching sport, craft beer discovery, casual sessions
Insider tip: Game days get packed early — arrive at least an hour before major matches to secure a good viewing position

19. Surabaya Johnny’s

The vibe: Intimate backstreet micro venue where blues, rock, and country acts play to audiences close enough to see every guitar string and feel every drum beat.

Surabaya Johnny’s is tiny, hidden, and absolutely essential for understanding Melbourne’s live music culture. This backstreet micro venue books blues, rock, and country acts who play to audiences measured in dozens rather than hundreds, creating an intimacy that’s impossible to replicate in larger spaces. When a band plays here, you’re not watching a show — you’re part of it. The drinks are simple and fairly priced, but that’s not why you’re here. You’re here for the music and the kind of authentic atmosphere that makes cities worth living in.

Order this: Beer or basic spirit ($8-12) — drinks are secondary to the music experience
Also try: Just focus on the music — that’s what this place does best
Address: Ask locals — part of the charm is that it’s not easy to find
Hours: Check listings for gig nights
Price range: $
Best for: Live music intimacy, discovering new acts, authentic atmosphere
Insider tip: Arrive early for gigs — with limited capacity, tickets often sell out and there’s no door sales

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best rooftop bars in St Kilda?
Captain Baxter offers luxury rooftop drinking with retractable roof and bay views, while Republica provides more casual beachfront access. For elevated dining experiences, the upstairs bar at Stokehouse delivers sophisticated harbour views with premium cocktails.

Which St Kilda bars are open late?
Veludo operates until 3am seven days a week, Hotel Barkly has late licensing for dive bar sessions, and The George Lounge offers sophisticated late-night drinking Friday-Saturday. The Espy extends to 3am weekends for post-gig drinks.

Where can I find the best live music in St Kilda?
The Espy remains Melbourne’s premier live music venue with international and local acts. Dogs Bar hosts free live music nightly in intimate settings, while Surabaya Johnny’s offers the closest possible connection between artists and audience. Village Belle Hotel features quality local acts in classic pub surroundings.

The Perfect St Kilda Bar Crawl

Start: Prince Public Bar (5pm) — Sophisticated beginning with excellent happy hour deals
Stop 2: Bar Di Stasio (6:30pm) — Classic cocktails and Italian glamour
Stop 3: The Espy Sunroom (8pm) — Bay views and botanical cocktails
Stop 4: Captain Baxter (9:30pm) — Waterfront sophistication
Stop 5: Dogs Bar (11pm) — Natural wines and live music
Finish: Veludo (late) — Dance floor and $6 spirits until 3am

This route moves from Fitzroy Street sophistication to waterfront glamour to Acland Street’s bohemian energy, covering St Kilda’s bar spectrum while staying physically manageable.
