7 Best Cafes in Thornbury Melbourne for 2026

G
Grace Park Mar 22, 2026
Thornbury

Thornbury takes its coffee seriously — this is Melbourne after all. The cafe scene here reflects the neighbourhood: independent operators, no chains, and enough competition that bad coffee doesn’t survive. High Street is the main strip, but some of the best spots are tucked away on backstreets. Here’s where to go.

1. Short Round

The vibe: The neighbourhood brunch institution that treats seasonal menus like a religion.

Short Round at 731 High Street is the cafe Thornbury locals recommend by name. The menu changes with the seasons — house-made date and orange waffles with vanilla mascarpone in winter, fresh corn fritters with herb salad in summer. The coffee is specialty-grade, the staff remember your order after two visits, and the space is small enough that it fills fast on weekends.

Order this: Date and orange waffles with vanilla mascarpone ($22) Address: 731 High St, Thornbury Hours: Mon-Fri 7:30am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm Insider tip: Arrive before 9am on weekends or expect a 15-20 minute wait. No booking system.

2. Rat the Cafe

The vibe: A quiet backstreet gem on Wales Street where the sourdough is naturally leavened and the eggs are truly free-range.

Rat sits on Wales Street, tucked away from the High Street bustle. The produce is mostly organic, the bread is naturally leavened in-house, and the eggs are genuinely free-range. The outdoor seating under a big tree is a sun trap on clear mornings — the best seat in Thornbury.

Order this: Seasonal brunch plate with house sourdough ($19) Address: Wales St, Thornbury Hours: Wed-Sun 7:30am-3pm; Mon-Tue closed Insider tip: They do a small but well-chosen wine selection for “brunch with a glass” situations.

3. Brother Alec

The vibe: A laid-back High Street cafe where $13 gets you nachos that could feed two.

Brother Alec at 719 High Street is Thornbury’s best argument for cafe culture without cafe prices. The small nachos at $13 are genuinely generous, the coffee is strong, and the vibe is so relaxed you’ll wonder if the staff are on the same caffeine timeline as you. About 20 seats, fills fast but never feels crowded.

Order this: Small nachos and a flat white ($13 + $4.50) Address: 719 High St, Thornbury Hours: Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm Insider tip: Weekday mornings before 9am are peaceful. After 10am it’s a local rush.

4. Le Cafe Flo

The vibe: French-leaning cafe culture on High Street without the Parisian attitude or prices.

Le Cafe Flo covers the classics: croque madame, eggs Benedict with proper hollandaise, fresh pastries that smell like a boulangerie. The coffee is served with care, and the fixed-price dinner nights (when they run them) are excellent value. Popular with families on weekends.

Order this: Croque madame with a cafe au lait ($19 + $5) Address: High St, Thornbury Hours: Wed-Mon 8am-3pm; Tue closed Insider tip: Pastries sell out by 10am on Saturdays. Get there early if pastry-motivated.

5. Barton Fink

The vibe: Morning coffee spot turned evening cocktail bar — both done with equal skill.

Barton Fink on High Street does serious morning coffee alongside a proper evening cocktail bar. The beans are well-sourced, the milk texturing is on point, and the food menu complements without overwhelming. The window seats have good natural light and decent Wi-Fi for working remotely.

Order this: Flat white and a pastry ($4.50 + $6) Address: High St, Thornbury Hours: From 7:30am daily (cafe); bar from 5pm Wed-Sat Insider tip: Their take-home coffee beans are well-priced. Grab a bag for your home machine.

6. Thornbury Espresso Bar

The vibe: The 1956 original, reborn during the pandemic — coffee heritage with a modern twist.

Thornbury Espresso Bar first opened in 1956. It was brought back to life during the pandemic as a specialty coffee spot with afternoons transitioning into spritzes and natural wine. The sunny beer garden hosts live music and events, making it the most versatile cafe on this list.

Order this: Double espresso or flat white ($4-$5) Address: High St, Thornbury Hours: Mon-Fri 7:30am-4pm; bar events Fri-Sat evenings Insider tip: Their pop-up event nights — wine tastings, live jazz — are worth following on socials.

7. Larks of Joy

The vibe: Bright, cheerful, and genuinely accommodating of dietary needs.

Larks of Joy is the cafe that does everything well without being famous for any one thing. The coffee is consistently strong, vegetarian, vegan, and gluten-free options are genuine rather than afterthoughts, and the takeaway operation runs smoothly for commuters on the 86 tram.

Order this: Brunch plate of the day with a long black ($19 + $4.50) Address: Thornbury Hours: Mon-Fri 7am-3pm, Sat-Sun 8am-3pm Insider tip: Order ahead on their app for quick tram-side pickup. The 86 stop is right outside.

The Verdict

Thornbury’s cafe scene is strong and growing. Short Round is the flagship, Rat the Cafe is the backstreet institution on Wales Street, and Brother Alec is the budget champion on High Street. All three are worth a dedicated visit from anywhere in the inner north.

FAQ

What’s the best cafe for working remotely in Thornbury? Barton Fink has good Wi-Fi, natural light, and bench space. Weekday mornings are quietest.

Which Thornbury cafe has the cheapest brunch? Brother Alec at 719 High Street. Nachos for $13 and a flat white for $4.50 — under $18 total.

Are Thornbury cafes dog-friendly? Most welcome dogs in outdoor areas. Water bowls appear without asking at the better spots.
