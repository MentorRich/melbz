10 Best Cafes in Thornbury for Your Daily Fix in 2026

10 Best Cafes in Thornbury for Your Daily Fix in 2026

Thornbury’s café culture isn’t trying to impress anyone, which is exactly why it’s so good. While other suburbs compete for “Melbourne’s best café” listicle spots, Thornbury cafes quietly serve excellent coffee, seasonal food, and a sense of neighbourhood that can’t be manufactured by an interior designer. Most of these spots are within walking distance of each other along High Street, and all of them are within the 86 tram catchment. Here’s where your mornings should happen.

Last updated: 17 March 2026 | Thornbury Vibe Score: 78/100 🟢

1. Short Round

The vibe: A High Street local that treats seasonal menus and specialty coffee like the basics they are.

At 731 High Street, Short Round is the café that converts casual coffee drinkers into people who care about single-origin beans. The seasonal menu uses fresh, high-quality ingredients — house-made date and orange waffles with vanilla mascarpone and toffee pecans in winter, corn fritters with herb salad in summer, and a brunch lineup that changes often enough that regulars never get bored. The space is compact and warm, the baristas are genuinely skilled, and the vibe is welcoming without being saccharine. If Thornbury had a café ambassador, this would be it.

Order this: Seasonal specials board (whatever the barista recommends) ($18–24) Address: 731 High St, Thornbury Hours: Mon–Fri 7:30am–3pm, Sat–Sun 8am–3pm Insider tip: The cocktail list at brunch is low-key brilliant. An espresso martini at 10am on a Saturday is a Thornbury power move.

2. Rat the Cafe

The vibe: Wales Street’s best-kept secret — organic produce, naturally leavened bread, and zero pretension.

Rat sits on a quiet residential corner of Wales Street, opposite a primary school and far enough from High Street that you’ll feel like you’ve discovered a secret. The coffee is specialty-grade, the brunch is made with mostly organic produce, and the bread is naturally leavened in-house. It’s been a neighbourhood institution for years, with long-running staff who make you feel like you’re visiting someone’s home rather than a business. The outdoor seating under a big tree is the best morning seat in Thornbury when the weather cooperates.

Order this: Brunch plate with house sourdough and truly free-range eggs ($20) Address: Wales St, Thornbury Hours: Wed–Sun 7:30am–3pm; Mon–Tue closed Insider tip: They do a small wine and natural wine selection for arvo visits. Rat at 4pm with a glass of pet-nat is an underrated move.

3. The Brickie & The Barista

The vibe: Industrial-chic meets serious coffee — a dual-concept space that nails both the morning and afternoon brief.

The Brickie & The Barista lives up to its name with a space that’s equal parts brick-themed décor and coffee obsession. The breakfast menu covers all the essentials — properly poached eggs, house-baked banana bread, smashed avo with real feta — and the coffee is consistently among the best on the strip. By afternoon, it transitions smoothly into a more relaxed space with a drinks menu that goes beyond espresso. It’s spacious enough to work from (power points available) without feeling like a co-working space.

Order this: Smashed avo with feta on sourdough and a batch brew ($18 + $4) Address: Thornbury Hours: Mon–Fri 7am–4pm, Sat–Sun 8am–4pm Insider tip: Take-home coffee beans are excellent value. Ask what’s seasonal — they rotate single-origins regularly.

4. Brother Alec

The vibe: The anti-hipster café — good food, good coffee, stupidly low prices.

Brother Alec at 719 High Street is proof that you don’t need a $25 avocado toast to have a good brunch. The small nachos at $13 are a genuine cheap eat, the coffee is strong and well-made, and the whole operation runs with a relaxed efficiency that feels more like a mate’s kitchen than a business. The space seats maybe 20, which creates an intimacy that bigger cafes can’t replicate. It’s where Thornbury locals go when they want good food without the performance.

Order this: Small nachos and a flat white ($13 + $4.50) Address: 719 High St, Thornbury Hours: Mon–Fri 7am–3pm, Sat–Sun 8am–3pm Insider tip: Weekday mornings are the sweet spot. After 10am on weekends you’ll queue. Not a long queue — but a queue nonetheless.

5. Le Café Flo

The vibe: A French-inspired café where the croque madame tastes like it was made in Lyon, not Thornbury.

Le Café Flo brings genuine European café culture to High Street. The croque madame is the dish to judge them by — proper béchamel, gruyère that actually tastes like something, and a fried egg with a perfectly runny yolk. The pastries are worth an early alarm, the café au lait is served properly, and the overall vibe is calm and unhurried. It’s popular with families on weekends and solo diners with books on weekday mornings.

Order this: Croque madame and a café au lait ($19 + $5) Address: High St, Thornbury Hours: Wed–Mon 8am–3pm; Tue closed Insider tip: The fixed-price dinner nights (when available) are excellent value — check their social media for dates.

6. Crunch Cafe

The vibe: A spacious, no-fuss café that does everything on the breakfast menu properly and fills up fast.

Crunch is the workhorse of the Thornbury café scene — not the flashiest, but arguably the most reliable. The breakfast menu is extensive (eggs every way, big brekkie plates, smoothie bowls, pancakes), the portions are generous, and the lunch options mean you can roll in at 2pm and still get fed. It’s one of the few Thornbury cafes with enough space that you can usually find a table on a busy Saturday without hovering.

Order this: Big breakfast with the lot ($22) Address: High St, Thornbury Hours: Mon–Fri 7am–4pm, Sat–Sun 8am–4pm Insider tip: The smoothie bowls are genuinely underrated — the açaí bowl is a lighter alternative that doesn’t sacrifice flavour.

7. Barton Fink

The vibe: A café-to-bar chameleon that excels at both — specialty coffee by day, cocktails by night.

Barton Fink pulls double duty as a serious café and a neighbourhood cocktail bar, and the transition is seamless. The morning coffee is specialty-grade with a well-curated food menu, and by evening the space transforms into a moody, welcoming bar. It’s one of the few Thornbury spots where you could start your day with a flat white at 8am and end it with an old fashioned at 11pm without leaving the building.

Order this: Morning flat white and a pastry ($4.50 + $6) Address: High St, Thornbury Hours: Daily from 7:30am (café hours); bar from 5pm Wed–Sat Insider tip: If you’re a solo worker, the window seats have good light and fast Wi-Fi. Just buy something every couple of hours — they notice.

8. Larks of Joy

The vibe: A cheerful, bright café where dietary needs are taken seriously and the coffee hits every time.

Larks of Joy lives up to its name with a bright, happy space and food that caters genuinely to vegetarian, vegan, and gluten-free diners. The brunch menu is well-executed with attention to detail — these aren’t sad afterthoughts for dietary needs, they’re genuinely good dishes that happen to be plant-based or gluten-free. It’s popular with young families, which means the weekend vibe is lively without tipping into chaos.

Order this: Their daily brunch special with a long black ($19 + $4.50) Address: Thornbury Hours: Mon–Fri 7am–3pm, Sat–Sun 8am–3pm Insider tip: Order ahead on their app if you’re on the 86 tram and passing through — they do excellent takeaway.

9. Smug Relish

The vibe: Confidently named and consistently excellent — the café that’s earned the right to be smug.

Smug Relish has been quietly excellent for long enough that the name feels less like a boast and more like a statement of fact. The house-made relishes are the star, the eggs are always cooked right (even on a busy Saturday, which is a genuine skill), and the coffee is the kind that makes you understand Melbourne’s coffee obsession. It’s a small space with fiercely loyal regulars, so you might feel like an outsider on your first visit — but by your third, you’re family.

Order this: Eggs Benedict with house-made relish ($17) Address: Thornbury Hours: Wed–Mon 8am–3pm; Tue closed Insider tip: They sell their relishes sometimes. If you like it (you will), ask at the counter about take-home jars.

10. Cafe Gummo

The vibe: A newer entry to the Thornbury café scene with creative dishes and a fresh perspective.

Cafe Gummo is one of the newer additions to the Thornbury café landscape and has quickly built a following for creative brunch dishes and strong coffee. The menu takes some risks — you’ll find combinations here that don’t appear at other Thornbury cafes — and the execution is consistently good. It’s the spot for adventurous brunchers who’ve been everywhere else and want something different.

Order this: Ask for the dish of the day — they change it up ($18–23) Address: Thornbury Hours: Wed–Mon 7:30am–3pm; Tue closed Insider tip: Follow their Instagram for daily specials — they post the menu each morning and the interesting stuff sells out.

The Bottom Line

Thornbury’s café scene is a genuine strength. Short Round and Rat the Cafe are the two you need to visit first — one for the High Street energy, one for the quiet-backstreet perfection. Brother Alec is the budget champion at $13 a plate, and Barton Fink is the only spot where your morning coffee can turn into an evening cocktail without you leaving the building.

Your Thornbury Vibe Score this week: 78/100 — The café scene holds up against Northcote and Brunswick without breaking a sweat.
